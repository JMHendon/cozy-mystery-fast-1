# Bible — the world of Merrow

## Merrow, Maine

A harbor village on the Midcoast, on a granite peninsula between Rockland and Camden (fictional). Year-round population about 1,900; triple that in July. By late October the summer people and leaf-peepers are gone and the town is "back to itself": working harbor, shuttered ice-cream stand, the last foliage hanging on in rusts and dull golds, woodsmoke, fog in the mornings, hard bright cold afternoons, dark by six (clocks haven't changed yet; sunset about 5:40). Water temperature in the harbor: about 52°F. Nights near freezing. Halloween is a Friday.

Character of the town: proud, private, funny, generous to its own, suspicious of people "from away" (which Archie will be until he dies, and possibly after). Lobstering is the economy; the select board and town meeting are the government; The Galley is the parliament.

The town's Halloween legend: **the Great Fairweather's vanishing.** On Halloween night 1988, after his annual show at the opera house, Gus Fairweather walked down to the harbor in his opera cloak, took his skiff out toward the bell buoy, and was never seen again. The skiff was found the next morning drifting near Merrow Light with his cloak folded on the seat. Chief Walt Calloway closed it as a drowning; no body was found. Kids still dare each other to say "Fairweather" three times on the wharf at midnight on Halloween.

### Places

**The Merrow Town Hall & Opera House (1899).** Main Street, opposite the Congregational church. White clapboard, two stories, a square bell tower. Ground floor: town office, select board room, a coat rack and a radiator that clanks like a ghost. Second floor, up a wide creaking staircase with a brass rail: **the opera house** — a 300-seat hall with a flat wooden floor (folding wooden chairs, stacked or set in rows), a pressed-tin ceiling, tall arched windows on both long walls looking down on Main Street and toward the harbor, iron radiators, a horseshoe **balcony** at the back with a **plywood light booth** (a 1970s dimmer board Hollis can play like an organ), and a raised **proscenium stage** with the town's treasure: the **1899 painted grand drape** — a canvas curtain painted with Merrow Harbor in summer, gulls and schooners and a too-blue sky. The stage has a **trapdoor** center-stage (Gus built it; it drops to a crawlspace under the stage that runs to a hatch at the alley stairs), wings stage left and right, a fly loft with old hemp rigging, a **prop room** off stage left (locked; shelves, a sink, a dress-form), and a narrow **green room** under the stage reached by stairs from stage right. Back stairs from stage left down to the alley. The building is cold always; the radiators knock; the windows rattle in a northeast wind. It was condemned-adjacent until Archie bought it; now half-restored — scaffolding on one wall, new wiring in the stage, the drape professionally cleaned, Gus's old posters (some) framed in the stairwell.

*Ownership:* Town voted at a special town meeting in September to sell it (it was costing $90,000 a year it didn't have). Warren Gilley pushed a Boston hotel developer's offer (condos + boutique hotel). Archie offered more, with a deed restriction keeping it a public theater forever. Archie won, 312 votes to 41.

**Fairweather House ("the Magic House").** End of Shore Road on Gull Point, a mile from Main Street. An 1886 Queen Anne Victorian: turret, wraparound porch, fish-scale shingles, paint gone a peeling purple-gray, a widow's walk with a view of the harbor and Merrow Light. Empty from 1988 until August (Vivienne kept up the taxes and a caretaker). Gus built illusions into it: a **revolving bookcase** in the library, a **dumbwaiter** that goes from the kitchen to the turret, a **staircase to nowhere** (it stops at a ceiling), a **trapdoor in the pantry** to the root cellar, a speaking tube from the turret to the kitchen, and portraits whose eyes really do have holes behind them. Rooms: big kitchen (center of family life — scarred farm table, a cast-iron range Archie can't work, a modern range he also can't work), library, front parlor full of Gus's posters and props, turret room (Gus's study — **the trunk** lives here), June's room (she picked the one with no secret doors; she has checked), Vivienne's room (the best one, naturally). The **carriage house** behind is Lenny's workshop and apartment; also where the **hearse** lives. Gus's overgrown garden runs down toward the rocks: rugosa roses gone to hips, rhubarb, and along the fence a stand of tall blue **monkshood** that Gus planted "for the name — wolfsbane, for Halloween." Several Merrow gardens have monkshood; it's an ordinary fall perennial here.

**The trunk.** A 1920s steamer trunk, black, brass corners, painted with faded gold stars, stenciled THE GREAT FAIRWEATHER. Locked with a lock Gus built himself: **seven brass letter-dials**. On the lid, in Gus's handwriting on a card under celluloid: *To be opened when I'm gone. Not before. — G.F.* Archie has been trying combinations nightly since August (he keeps a notebook of failures: 11,000+ words). Vivienne refuses to try: "He said when he's gone." Lenny refuses to force it: Gus told the whole town in the eighties that anyone who forced it would ruin what was inside ("a glass vial of India ink over the contents" — maybe true, maybe a bluff; nobody will risk it). (The combination and contents are planner-only — see mystery-spine §8. Drafters: the trunk stays shut until the final chapter.)

**The hearse.** A 1958 Cadillac Miller-Meteor hearse, black, gold lettering on the side panels: THE GREAT FAIRWEATHER — MARVELS & MYSTERIES. Gus toured in it; it sat in the carriage house for thirty-seven years; Lenny restored it in September. Archie drives it everywhere. It gets eleven miles to the gallon and every dog in town barks at it. June will not be dropped off at school in it.

**The Galley.** Bev's diner at the head of the town wharf. A low gray-shingled building with a long counter (twelve stools, red vinyl), six booths along windows over the harbor, a pie case, a chalkboard, a coffee urn the size of a lobster pot, a bell over the door. The **liars' table**: a round table by the kitchen where the old lobstermen sit from 5 a.m. and don't talk to Archie. Opens at 5, closes at 2 (dinner Fri/Sat in summer only). Bev's thermoses are steel, dented, with masking-tape labels.

**The wharf and harbor.** The town wharf with its lobster co-op, bait shed (smell), fuel dock, harbormaster's shack (Ozzie). Moorings full of lobster boats with names like *Second Wind* and *Rosie & Liam*. **The fishermen's memorial**: a granite block at the end of the wharf carved with the names of Merrow men and women lost at sea since 1847 — the most recent names near the bottom: *Peter Langley 2003* … *Thomas Mayo 2025* (fresh-cut, sharper than the rest). Out in the harbor: the **bell buoy** off Shag Ledge (you hear it from town in fog), and **Merrow Light** on the point — white tower, automated, a green flash every six seconds.

**Sea View.** Memory-care residence on the hill above town: a converted 1920s summer hotel with a long porch, rocking chairs, a common room with a piano and a view of the bay. Walt Calloway lives here; so does Carol Gilley.

**Merrow Public Library.** A small granite 1911 library on Elm Street with a green dome and a radiator that hisses. Librarian Deb Coombs. The **Fairweather Collection**: one glass-fronted shelf of local-history items including Gus's own books. Gus's 1971 debunking book **_The Dead Don't Whisper: How Fraudulent Mediums Fool the Grieving_** is in-library-use only except for townspeople with Deb's blessing; it still has its old **sign-out card** in a pocket on the inside back cover, because Deb likes it there. The card's handwritten history (planner-facing; see mystery-spine): it has been signed out only a handful of times in forty years, including *W. Calloway 11/88*, *H. Langley 12/03*, and, recently, three times by *J. Fairweather*.

**The Spindrift Inn.** A white 1840s sea captain's house turned inn on Water Street, owner Pru Haskell. Lorelei's crew stays here (Lorelei has the Captain's Suite with the widow's-walk access). Small bar/parlor with a fireplace.

**Hollis's house.** A small weathered Cape on Pudding Lane with a picket fence, blue door, a huge fall border (dahlias, asters, tall blue monkshood), a potting shed with a sagging roof, and a front room taken over by costumes on dress forms. Inside: books everywhere, a cat named Malvolio, photographs on the piano (a boy on a dock, a man in oilskins — never commented on before the click).

**The quarry.** An old flooded granite quarry two miles inland — the town's swimming hole. Danny Langley drowned there in 2001. Mentioned at most once before the click, as texture.

**Penobscot Regional High School.** Where June goes; twenty minutes away by bus.

**The county jail / State Police.** Knox County Jail in Rockland (where Ivy is held). MSP Major Crimes works out of the barracks in Augusta / a Midcoast field office; they set up in the Merrow select board room as a temporary command post. Initial appearances at the Knox County courthouse in Rockland.

**Hospital.** "The hospital in Rockport" (Pen Bay), twenty minutes by road.

## Procedure — Maine (keep honest)

- Homicides outside Portland and Bangor are investigated by the **Maine State Police Major Crimes Unit**, with local police assisting. The **Office of the Chief Medical Examiner** (Augusta) handles unattended/suspicious deaths. The **Attorney General's Office** prosecutes homicides (not the county DA).
- An unexpected death of a healthy-seeming 58-year-old during a public event, with symptoms before collapse, would go to the OCME. A targeted toxicology screen for aconitine is not routine; it must be requested — Archie's suggestion via Tess is what triggers it. **Convention:** preliminary targeted results within ~48 hours and a crime-lab swab result within ~72 hours are compressed for story pace; label as convention in planning, don't make a fictional deadline a medical fact on the page.
- Interviews are voluntary unless in custody; Miranda applies in custody. Archie is never arrested; he is a "person of interest" and is asked to come in, and he does, voluntarily, repeatedly, talking far too much.
- Ivy is arrested on Tuesday night for **tampering with physical evidence** and **falsifying physical evidence**, held; after the scarf result and her own statement she's charged with murder on Thursday (AG's office); initial appearance Thursday afternoon in Rockland; held without bail pending a Harnish hearing (Maine's bail hearing for murder).
- Search warrants: a judge signs; Tess gets one for Hollis's house and shed on Halloween night.
- Chain of custody matters: Archie handing over things he found (the remote, Kev's footage, the B-roll) creates real friction about how he got them.

## Gus Fairweather (backstory, planner-facing)

Augustus "Gus" Fairweather, born 1925, Merrow. Vaudeville kid, war (Navy, Pacific), then forty years as *the Great Fairweather*: touring illusionist, radio, early TV, Vegas lounges in the sixties, then home to Merrow every October for his Halloween show at the opera house — a town institution. Famous debunker: wrote *The Dead Don't Whisper* (1971) after exposing a string of New England séance frauds; his act included a "Séance Unmasked" segment. Widower (his wife Rose died 1970). Called his grandson Archie "Sparrow" because the boy was small, loud and always hungry. Signature closing illusion: **the Empty Chair** — a single chair under a spotlight, a sheet, a count of three, and the sheet falls away from someone who wasn't there. Vanished Halloween night 1988 at 63.
