# Mystery Spine — the truth, the clocks, the clues

## 1. What actually happened

**Killer:** Hollis Tibbetts, 66 (socially "Hollis Langley" 1983–c.2005; always "Mrs. Tibbetts" professionally).
**Victim:** Lorelei Crane, 58 (born Lorraine Cranmer; "Sister Lori Cranmer" 1995–2008).
**Weapon:** a concentrated aconite (monkshood root) extract in a lanolin-and-beeswax base, smeared inside the rim of the small end — the mouthpiece — of Lorelei's luminous-banded aluminum spirit trumpet.
**Mechanism:** Lorelei's own fraud. In the dark, her plant (Ivy, at her left) released Lorelei's left hand; Lorelei lifted the trumpet and spoke through it, lips to the poisoned rim, for about ten minutes.
**Why it looked impossible:** every sitter "held hands." Two people in that circle let go — the two working the trick — and only one of them put her mouth on the trumpet. The killer was never in the circle; she was in the light booth.
**What Hollis expected:** a death ruled natural. Aconite is not on routine toxicology and kills by arrhythmia: a 58-year-old woman's heart gives out in the dark at a séance, and her fans call it the spirits. She did not foresee that the one man in the room would know what a numb mouth means. She kept the jar and the gloves deliberately — her proof, if anyone else were ever blamed. That is her moral line.

### The history
- 1983: Hollis Tibbetts marries Peter Langley, lobsterman (b. 1952). Socially "Hollis Langley"; at the high school, "Mrs. Tibbetts."
- 1986: son Daniel ("Danny") born — a grade ahead of Tess Calloway at Penobscot Regional.
- Aug 2001: Danny, 15, drowns at the granite quarry swimming hole. Accident.
- Oct 2001 – Nov 2003: Peter answers a Grange-hall flyer (with photo) for "Sister Lori Cranmer — Messages from Loved Ones." Thursday-evening private sittings in a rented room over a laundromat on Park Street, Rockland — forty-one of them; $350 each at first, then "special sittings" at $2,500 as the voice of Danny grew more insistent: total $61,500 (a second mortgage Hollis didn't know about). Through the trumpet "Danny" says *Dad. It's warm here. The water's warm. I'm not cold anymore* — and, later, *Come see me on the water.* Peter writes every message in a notebook.
- 18 Nov 2003: Peter takes his boat, the *Hollis T.*, out in a gale warning. Found aground on Shag Ledge; body recovered two days later. Ruled accidental.
- Late Nov 2003: Hollis finds the notebook, the receipts and the Grange flyer. Takes them to Chief Walt Calloway. Walt writes it up in his own hand in the Merrow PD paper files (filed **by complainant**: "Langley, H."), with a photocopy of the flyer; phones the AG's office and Rockland PD; Sister Lori has moved on; nothing chargeable ("She never told him to go out in a storm, Hollis. Not in so many words."). No state file is opened. Walt keeps it quiet out of kindness.
- 9 Dec 2003: Hollis signs Gus Fairweather's *The Dead Don't Whisper* out of the library — card: **H. Langley 12/03** — and learns exactly how it was done (p. 112).
- 2004–2020: Mrs. Tibbetts adds a **"séance unit"** to her senior English course — Gus's book, page 112, Houdini, the Fox sisters; "how the grieving are fooled." A generation of Merrow kids can chant "lips to the small end." (Her grief became her curriculum. Nobody knows why she added it.)
- Lorelei's ledger, Nov 2003, in her private shorthand: P.L. lost at sea; saw it in the paper; God forgive me; no more trumpet for privates. Fans later call 2003–2006, when Sister Lori dropped the trumpet from private sittings, **"the Trumpet Hiatus"** (fandom lore: Brother Amos told her to).
- 2014 onward: Lorelei becomes famous. Hollis watches.
- Feb this year: Tommy Mayo lost off Monhegan.
- **May — an exposure plan.** After watching an episode in which Lorelei works a Gloucester fisherman's widow, Hollis writes to Marchand Media pitching the Merrow Opera House, the 37th anniversary of the Great Fairweather's Halloween vanishing, a haunted theater. Her intent: bring Lorelei to the town where Peter's name is cut in granite, and confront her on camera with Peter's notebook. July: contract with the town; $40,000 location fee for the roof. **July:** during pre-production research on local sitters, Ivy finds Tommy Mayo's obituary and Lorelei's team messages Shannon offering a free reading. Shannon tells Hollis.
- Aug: Archie arrives. Sept: Archie buys the opera house (closing Nov 15); wants to fight the town to cancel the séance. Hollis talks him out of it: "Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house." (She needed the séance — but also, then, still believed in exposure.)
- **Mid-September — the turn.** Shannon's second paid reading: *"Tommy misses you on the water, Shan."* Shannon repeats it to Hollis over coffee. It is the first step of Peter's pattern. Hollis understands that exposure won't save Shannon — you can't debunk a widow; grief is debunk-proof; Peter wouldn't have stopped either. That week she gives Shannon "the speech" (it doesn't work), and digs roots from the back of her monkshood border.
- Late Sept – mid Oct: cold maceration of the roots in vodka for three weeks; alcohol evaporated at room temperature on the radiator under a cloth; worked into lanolin and beeswax from the costume shop (she uses it for leather) in a small round tin. The remainder of the extract in a blue Ball jar on the second shelf of her potting shed.
- ~Oct 18, after the first frost: she cuts down the entire monkshood border and **lifts the whole bed** — normal timing for cutting back, odd for perennials (gardeners don't lift monkshood). The dose needed tens of grams of root: the bed went.

### Saturday, Oct 25 — the clocks (keep these four separate)

**(1) Interference — when the trumpet was poisoned:** 7:51 p.m. Hollis, as house manager (the town's open-flame permit names her), placed and lit the candles at 7:46, including the tall pillar candle a hand's width from the polished aluminum horn. At 7:51 its flame and reflection flare both infrared cameras: Marco pans his handheld off to the windows to reset exposure (7:51:10–7:51:50), and Teddy, seeing the flare on his monitor, calls, "Hollis — sorry — can you slide the horn left, it's fighting the candle." (Had he not asked, she'd have moved it herself "for fire safety.") She had set the water glasses first. Under her black cotton stage glove she wears a nitrile finger cot (costume-shop dye habit). She loaded the fingertip from the tin in the wings with the tray in her other hand. She lifts the horn the way anyone lifts a funnel — finger hooked into the narrow end — slides it left, and walks off with the tray. Gloves and cot into her cardigan pocket.
**Failsafe:** from 7:52 until lights-out at 8:00 she watches the table from the booth. Had anyone else lifted the horn, she would have brought up the work lights and called a fire hold.
**(2) Encounter:** ~8:17–8:28 p.m., Lorelei speaking through the trumpet in the dark.
**(3) Harm apparent:** tingling/burning lips from ~8:18 — she reads it as the menthol lozenge Ivy gave her at 7:50; the slick rim she reads as Archie's raspberry-toot. Voice thick and slurring ~8:26 (sitters take it for the spirit fading). 8:28 the trumpet falls; "My mouth — I can't feel my —"; collapse. 8:29 house lights. EMS 8:41; ambulance 8:52. Refractory VT/VF at the hospital in Rockport; **pronounced dead 9:40 p.m.**

Police request all media from Marchand Media. **Teddy hands over the official IR drives and lies**: the handheld card was "exteriors only, already shipped to LA"; Marco has flown home; Marchand's lawyers slow-walk. **Ivy moves the green hatbox of her mother's old appointment books from the suite to Pru Haskell's office safe** ("Mother's private things").

## 2. The police's honest wrong answers

**Phase 1 (Sat–Mon): ingestion → Archie.** Tea at 7:40 on camera; thermos and cup-caps washed by Bev Sunday morning; he named aconite first; $2M feud; Gus's monkshood in his garden, stalks freshly cut (Vivienne, for a vase); handled the trumpet; "you fool cameras for a living." Lip swab positive doesn't break it (she drank tea). Chief Pinkham alibis Archie 7:45–7:58, which only matters later.

**Phase 2 (Tue–Thu): the trumpet → Ivy.** Lorelei held it herself (Kev); Ivy lied about the hands, ran the kill switch (remote, her prints), wiped the mouthpiece in front of a witness and kept the scarf (aconitine + lipstick), inherits ~$9 million, was overheard saying she was done, set out the props, and said "It's my fault." The dark is conceded; the B-roll then concedes 7:36–7:59 except the 40 seconds. So Brannock's honest final position: *If the toot was real, the trumpet was clean at 7:36, and the only unobserved access is forty seconds in which a retired schoolteacher slid it at the producer's request, in gloves, with no connection to the victim we can find — and in which your mother, a fan and a widow stood near. If the toot was faked, I have Ivy at 7:20 — or I have the one man on tape with motive, poison in his garden and the hands to do it, who "tooted" it.* Rigor producing the wrong result.

**Where aconite came from (AG's theory for Ivy):** several Merrow gardens grow it; she had three days in town before the séance; no purchases or searches on her devices — the theory is weak and Brannock knows it, which is why the murder charge waits until her statement.
