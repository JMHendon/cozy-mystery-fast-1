# Mystery Spine — the truth, the clocks, the clues

## 1. What actually happened

**Killer:** Hollis Tibbetts, 66 (socially "Hollis Langley" 1983–c.2005; always "Mrs. Tibbetts" professionally).
**Victim:** Lorelei Crane, 58 (born Lorraine Cranmer; "Sister Lori Cranmer" 1995–2008).
**Weapon:** a concentrated aconite (monkshood root) extract worked into a clear salve, smeared inside the rim of the small end — the mouthpiece — of Lorelei's luminous-banded aluminum spirit trumpet.
**Mechanism:** Lorelei's own fraud. In the dark, her plant (Ivy, seated at her left) released Lorelei's left hand; Lorelei lifted the trumpet and spoke through it, lips to the poisoned rim, for about ten minutes. She absorbed the aconite through her lips and mouth.
**Why it looked impossible:** every sitter "held hands"; the only hand that was free in that circle belonged to the victim. The killer was never in the circle at all — she was in the light booth.

### The history
- 1983: Hollis Tibbetts marries Peter Langley, lobsterman (b. 1952). Socially she's "Hollis Langley"; at the high school she stays "Mrs. Tibbetts."
- 1986: son Daniel ("Danny") born.
- Aug 2001: Danny, 15, drowns at the granite quarry swimming hole. Accident.
- Oct 2001 – Nov 2003: Peter, wrecked, answers a flyer at the Grange for "Sister Lori Cranmer — Messages from Loved Ones." Forty-one private sittings in a rented room above a laundromat on Park Street, Rockland, Thursday evenings. $61,500 (a second mortgage Hollis didn't know about). Through the trumpet, "Danny" says: *Dad. It's warm here. The water's warm. I'm not cold anymore.* Over time: *Come see me on the water.* Peter writes every message in a notebook. Hollis does not know.
- 18 Nov 2003: Peter takes his boat, the *Hollis T.*, out in a gale warning. Found aground on Shag Ledge; his body recovered two days later. Ruled accidental.
- Late Nov 2003: Hollis finds the notebook and the receipts. Takes them to Chief Walt Calloway. Walt writes it up (complaint report in his hand: "Complainant Hollis Langley… 'Sister Lori Cranmer'…"), calls the AG's office and Rockland PD; Sister Lori has already moved on; nothing chargeable — "She never told him to go out in a storm, Hollis. Not in so many words."
- 9 Dec 2003: Hollis signs Gus Fairweather's *The Dead Don't Whisper* out of the Merrow library — card: **H. Langley 12/03**. Page 112 explains the trumpet: the medium's freed hand, lips to the small end.
- Lorelei's own ledger, Nov 2003: *"P.L. lost at sea. Saw it in the paper. God forgive me. No more trumpet for privates."* (She resumed the trumpet for privates in 2006.)
- 2014 onward: Lorelei becomes famous. Hollis watches every season.
- Feb this year: Tommy Mayo lost off Monhegan. April: Shannon Mayo tells Hollis that Lorelei Crane's people have messaged her about a free reading. Hollis sees it starting again.
- May: Hollis writes to Marchand Media pitching the Merrow Opera House, the 37th anniversary of the Great Fairweather's Halloween vanishing, and "local sitters with genuine losses — I can introduce you." The show bites. July: contract with the town; $40,000 location fee for the opera-house roof.
- Aug: Archie arrives. Sept: Archie buys the opera house; wants to cancel the séance; Hollis talks him into beating Lorelei instead.
- Late Sept: Hollis digs monkshood roots from her own border, makes a vodka tincture, reduces it, and works it into petroleum jelly in a small round tin. She keeps the remainder in a blue Ball jar on the second shelf of her potting shed.

### Saturday, Oct 25 — the clocks (keep these four separate)

**(1) Interference — when the trumpet was poisoned:** 7:51 p.m., during the B-roll cutaway. She carries on setting water glasses.
**(2) Encounter — when the poison was absorbed:** ~8:17–8:28 p.m., while Lorelei speaks through the trumpet in the dark.
**(3) Harm apparent:** numb lips ~8:22 (she ignores it — cold metal); voice thick and slurring ~8:26 (sitters take it for the "spirit fading"); 8:28 the trumpet falls; "My mouth — I can't feel my —"; collapse. 8:29 house lights up. EMS 8:41; ambulance departs 8:52. **9:40 p.m. pronounced dead** at the hospital in Rockport (ventricular fibrillation; resuscitation fails).

## 2. The police's honest wrong answers

**Phase 1 (Sat–Mon): natural death → poisoning by ingestion.** Ingestion onset fits 7:30–8:20. Candidates: Archie's tea (7:40, on camera; thermos returned to Bev Sunday morning and washed), the lozenge (7:50), water (on the table), wine (5:30, too early). Archie: public feud, $2M wager, named aconite before anyone else could, monkshood in his garden (some stalks freshly cut — by Vivienne, for a vase), handled the trumpet and poured her tea on camera, and "fools cameras for a living." Brannock's reasoning is sound.

**Phase 2 (Tue–Thu): the trumpet → Ivy.** Lab + Kev's footage prove Lorelei held the trumpet herself. Ivy lied about the hands, ran the IR kill switch (remote with her prints), wiped the mouthpiece in front of a witness and kept the rag, inherits ~$9 million, was overheard telling her mother she was done, knew the method better than anyone and set out the props at 7:20. Archie's toot at 7:35 would clear her — but a magician's toot is exactly the kind of thing a magician could fake; the camera is side-on. Ivy's own guilt-wracked statement seals it. Rigour producing the wrong result.

**What breaks it:** the B-roll (Ivy never touched the table after 7:45; so the toot window 7:36–8:00 excludes her *if* the toot was real), and then motive: the ledger.

## 8. Planner-only series truth
- **The trunk's combination is SPARROW** (Gus's pet name for Archie, which Archie had forgotten until "Gus" used it at the séance; Lorelei got it from a 1988 *Merrow Packet* article Ivy found: "survived by his daughter Vivienne and his grandson Archie, whom he called his 'little sparrow'"). Archie deliberately refuses to try it (Ch 24): he won't let a dead fraud open Gus's trunk. June opens it (Ch 44).
- Inside: Gus's opera gloves, a deck of cards, a folded note — *Sparrow — you remembered. That's the whole trick. — G.* — and, on top, a Key West picture postcard postmarked **March 1996**, addressed to the house, in Gus's hand: *Sparrow — still working on the trick. Don't let your mother sell the house. — G.* Gus was alive eight years after he "drowned," and someone with the combination put the card inside.
- Gus folded his opera cloak on the skiff seat in 1988. Archie, without thinking, folds Gus's cloak on Ozzie's seat before he dives (Ch 38) — a habit Gus taught him (seeded Ch 2: "Just like Dad"). In Ch 44 he remembers: a man going over the side by accident doesn't fold his cloak.
- Walt Calloway helped Gus vanish. Book 1 seeds only: "Gus Fairweather still owes me a boat" (Ch 4); Hollis's memory that Gus winked at Walt on his way out of the theater that night (Ch 42). Nothing more.
