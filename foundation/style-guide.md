# Style Guide — Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather

## The book in one breath
A warm, funny, fast, fair-play American cozy with a larger-than-life lead whose fundamental weather is delight. The crime is serious; the world is gentle and fixable. Readers come back for Archie's company and the town that slowly claims him.

## Point of view and tense
- **Close third person, past tense, Archie's POV** — every chapter except **Chapter 34 (Tess's POV)**, the single deliberate exception.
- The narrator may know ordinary social facts (a person's job, history, the price of a house, how a harbor works). The narrator never knows more than the POV character about the crime, never states another character's private thoughts as fact, never identifies guilt early, never foreshadows ("Later, he would wish…" is banned).
- Other people's inner states reach the page through behavior, speech, public history, or Archie's clearly framed interpretation — and Archie may be confidently wrong.
- Archie never diagnoses his own wound in narration. He can notice the shape of a thing; he does not name it as his mechanism.

## Language
- **American English and spelling.** Maine idiom where it belongs ("ayuh," "from away," "wicked," "dooryard," "upta camp" — sparingly, in the mouths of people who'd say them; never phonetic dialect spelling beyond "ayuh").
- **Content:** no on-page violence or gore; no sex; the death is collapse and aftermath, never clinical. Profanity: essentially none. Lenny may say "hell" once; Bev may say "damn" once. Archie never swears — he has substitutes, used sparingly ("Holy Houdini" at most twice in the book).
- **No comps or genre references:** never mention real mystery novels, TV detectives, or comparable series. No real living magicians by name. Houdini is fair as history/culture, sparingly.

## Chapter shape (craft/cozy-mystery.md, "Bite-sized chapters")
- One dramatic scene per chapter wherever possible; at most two closely connected scenes. **Target 1,500–2,000 words. Hard ceiling 2,300** unless the brief records an approved exception.
- Each chapter: a dominant immediate purpose, a meaningful turn, a reason to keep reading. End on a consequential change, decision or live question — not on an arbitrary cliffhanger, not on a thematic epigram.
- **Open every chapter with orientation:** where, when (day/time where it matters), who's present, what Archie is doing or wants. Compact, concrete, then go.
- Chapter headings: `# Chapter N` then a title line `## Title`.

## The register (craft/light-energy-structure.md)
- **Delight is the weather.** Interest becomes participation: Archie asks, tries, joins, buys, recruits. Not every chapter needs a joke; no chapter should lose his voice because the subject turned serious.
- **Comedy belongs to characters, never the author.** Every laugh is somebody's.
- **Wit is owned, not ambient.** In any scene, Archie plus *at most one* sparring partner get polished lines. Everyone else lands effects in their own currency: plain speech, literalism, repetition, action instead of speech, a task they keep doing, a missed joke, silence. **At least a quarter of exchanges are flat** — logistics, courtesy, mundane answers. The wit lands harder with space around it.
- **Let grief be grief.** Shannon, Ivy, Vivienne and Hollis get real stillness where events earn it. Archie's humor may flicker as armor; it never varnishes someone else's pain.
- **Lovability laws:** he loses small and often; he gets caught; his wins cost someone; delight escapes unauthorized; kindness and surveillance are one gesture; a bill-collector is on the page. Never let a perceptive rebuke produce instant humble insight — Archie reforms slowly, if at all, across the book.
- **Generosity can succeed.** Sometimes his gift simply works and everyone has a wonderful time.

## Narration — commercial middle register
- Default: oriented, physically textured, ordinary subject–verb–object order, one Archie judgment permitted, paragraphs allowed to end on an action, a fact, or silence. **Not every paragraph needs a capper.** Plain endings make the bright ones bright.
- **Orient before interpreting.** Put the concrete subject, action and stimulus on the page before any interpretation.
- **First-pass comprehension outranks display.** No riddles. Pronouns must have an unmistakable antecedent. A tired commuter must be able to parse every sentence in one pass.
- **One inferential turn per sentence, max.** Emotions arrive as behavior, not abstract-noun pairs ("caught between grief and hope" — no).
- **Semantic order:** person → action → object → consequence. No locative inversions ("Beneath the table lay…") unless the effect is real.
- **The manner clause is rationed** — "the way X does Y," "with the air of a man who…," "as though…," "like someone who…": at most **one per ~500 words, one per scene, never two in a paragraph.** Keep only those whose vehicle adds information.
- **Abstraction-as-actor** ("relief reached the surface," "silence settled") — rare; prefer a person doing a thing.
- **Aphorisms** — the narrator may have one now and then; give it its own sentence; ration hard.
- **Similes** — one clean image beats layered comparisons. No more than two figurative comparisons per page.
- **Banned tics:** eyes twinkling/sparkling; "a beat"; "something shifted"; "let out a breath he didn't know he was holding"; "the silence stretched"; smirks; "chuckled darkly"; jaw tightening as default emotion; "electric"; characters "padding" anywhere; "orbs"; ending scenes on one-line paragraphs that state the theme.
- **Description forms a picture.** Establish the space before the close-up (shape, light, use, who's in it). Make light, weather, season and clothing physically consistent (late October Maine: cold, early dark, woodsmoke, fog mornings, bright hard afternoons). Vary portraits — don't introduce every person with age→hair→face→clothes. Let some things simply be visible; not every detail is symbolic or a clue.

## Dialogue
- Character cards are ranges, not scripts. **Never copy a sample line from a card or a brief.** Briefs name functions, not wording.
- Dialogue tags: "said" by default; action beats fine; never "he smiled" as a tag. No comma-splices between dialogue and action beats.
- Numbers in dialogue and prose spelled out except times, years, addresses, and large figures where digits read cleaner ($2 million, 7:51).
- Archie's patter can run long when it's a performance; let others interrupt, ignore, or answer literally.
- Nobody explains the theme. Nobody says "the case mirrors…". Nobody gives a speech about grief or magic or truth. Earned short sincere lines only.

## Mystery hygiene (craft/cozy-mystery.md, "Misdirection is fair")
- State each required physical fact clearly **once**, in ordinary use, where the reader can retain it. No ominous close-ups, no "little did he know," no lingering on objects that will matter.
- **Never stage a reaction shot of a person to make them look guilty or innocent beyond what the scene naturally gives.**
- Keep the four clocks separate (tampering / exposure / symptoms / police knowledge). Don't let a character state as medical fact something the lab hasn't established.
- Police are competent. Brannock and Tess hold honest positions on real evidence. Never make them stupid so Archie can shine.
- Procedure is Maine's: State Police Major Crimes leads; OCME in Augusta; the Attorney General's office prosecutes homicide. Voluntary interviews; Miranda in custody; chain of custody matters.

## Clues within living scenes
Character presence, pleasure and disagreement are the main experience, not slack around protected clue paragraphs. If a scene exists only to deliver a fact, redesign the encounter so the fact rides on a collision between people with independent wants.

## Encounters, not interviews (craft/storygrid-commandments.md)
- At every principal encounter, the other person has an **independent positive goal** and takes an action that changes Archie's options: they redirect, bargain, recruit, compete, refuse access, insist on an obligation, act first.
- No "interview chains." An aftermath conversation about events the reader saw must change something now (a decision, a tactic, a relationship, an available route) — otherwise compress it to a clause.
- Archie must be the cause — of successes and of damage.

## Physical-business budgets (anti-convergence)
- Coin walk across knuckles: max once per three chapters. Card from nowhere: max once per three chapters. Archie winding his watch: allowed when thinking, never twice in a chapter. The emerald scarf: a few times in the book. Pickpocket lifts: exactly three in the book, placed by the briefs (Ch 4, Ch 20, Ch 43 — the last is Tess's).
- "If a move feels like the obvious best choice, another drafter has already used it." Prefer the specific, situational action over the signature tic.
