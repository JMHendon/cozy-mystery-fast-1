# Drafting Instructions (for chapter-drafting agents)

You are drafting chapters of *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather*, a cozy mystery intended to be a NYT-bestseller-quality launch of a series. You are a professional novelist with a superb commercial ear. The opening chapters (1–6, in `manuscript/`) were written by the lead author and **set the voice**: read them first, in full, and match their register, rhythm, warmth, humor, narration density and paragraph shapes. They are your calibration — not a source of phrases to reuse.

## Read before writing
1. `foundation/style-guide.md` — the contract. Obey it.
2. `foundation/characters-drafting.md` — cards. Ranges, not scripts.
3. `foundation/bible.md` — places and procedure (the sections your chapters use).
4. `foundation/outline.md` — your chapters' briefs **and** the briefs immediately before and after them.
5. `manuscript/ch01.md` … every existing chapter up to yours, in order. Read the immediately preceding chapter with special care for continuity of time, clothing, weather, who knows what, and running jokes.
6. `craft/cozy-mystery.md` (Bite-sized chapters; Design encounters), `craft/storygrid-commandments.md`, `craft/light-energy-structure.md` — the standard you'll be judged by.

## How to write each chapter
- **One dramatic scene** (max two closely connected). **1,500–2,100 words**; hard ceiling 2,300 unless your brief says otherwise.
- Open with **orientation** (where, when, who, what Archie wants) in a compact concrete paragraph or two.
- Build a **real encounter**: Archie pursues something; the other person has an independent positive want and acts on it; Archie adapts; something changes. Map the five commandments in your head (inciting incident → progressive complication/turning point → crisis → climax → resolution) and make sure the chapter turns a value.
- Deliver the brief's **required facts** once each, clearly, at the stated emphasis, inside living action — never as a clue close-up.
- Keep companionship substantive: let enjoyable exchanges breathe even when they carry no clue.
- **Wit is owned:** Archie + at most one sparring partner get polished lines. Everyone else is plainer, literal, busy, or silent. At least a quarter of exchanges are flat.
- End on a consequential change, decision or live question. No thematic epigram endings.
- **Never** copy a line from a card, brief or sample. Write fresh.
- Avoid the banned tics (style guide). Ration manner clauses (≤1 per 500 words) and similes.
- American English. No profanity. No gore. No comps.
- "If a move feels like the obvious best choice, another drafter has already used it." Archie's signature tics (coin walk, card from nowhere, winding his watch) are budgeted — check the preceding chapters before using one.

## Output
- Write each chapter to `manuscript/chNN.md` (two-digit), formatted exactly like the existing ones: `# Chapter N` / blank / `## Title` / blank / prose. Use `---` for a scene break if needed.
- After drafting, re-read your chapter once against: the brief (all required facts? exit as briefed?), continuity with the preceding chapter, the style guide's banned list, word count (`wc -w`). Fix what you find. Do not do more than one revision pass on your own prose.
- Append a short handoff note for the next drafter to `manuscript/handoffs.md`: chapter number, word count, any new named minor characters/places/objects you invented, any running joke or physical state (injuries, clothes, weather, time) the next chapter must honor, and any deviation from the brief with its reason.
- Do not modify foundation documents or other chapters.
