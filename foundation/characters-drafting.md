# Characters — Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather

Ages as of October, Book 1. Present day, year unstated. Halloween is a Friday.

---

## ARCHIE FAIRWEATHER — the lead (full presence, every chapter, POV)

**Archibald Augustus Fairweather, 47.** The most famous magician in the world. Stage name inherited from his grandfather: *the Great Fairweather.*

**Look.** Six-one, long-limbed, built like a heron that does Pilates. A thick mess of dark hair going silver at the temples that he will not cut ("It's part of the act." "You don't have an act anymore." "Then it's part of the man."). A face mostly made of grin, with deep laugh-lines and very fast, very dark eyes. Beautiful, restless hands — always doing something: a coin walking across the knuckles, a card appearing between two fingers, a pen spinning. Dresses like he got lost on the way to a better century and stopped at an outdoor-gear store: velvet jackets and brocade waistcoats over Henley shirts, a moth-eaten emerald scarf of Gus's that's eight feet long, expensive sneakers, a vintage watch he winds when thinking. In Maine he has added a red-and-black buffalo-check wool coat he bought on day one and wears with total sincerity.

**History.** Born in Boston; raised by his mother Vivienne; father "a trombonist, allegedly." Summers in Merrow with his grandfather Gus, who taught him his first trick at four. He was ten, in the second row, the Halloween night in 1988 Gus played his last show at the Merrow Opera House — and walked down to the harbor afterward, and was never seen again. Street magic in Harvard Square at nineteen; cruise ships; Vegas at twenty-six; network specials by thirty (*Archie Fairweather: Impossible*). Walked through Fenway's Green Monster. Vanished the *Delta Belle* riverboat on live TV. Escaped from a sealed safe at the bottom of Lake Tahoe. Also America's most famous debunker of psychics: **the Fairweather Challenge** — one million dollars to anyone who can demonstrate a paranormal power under his controls. Never paid out. Divorced, fondly, from Simone, a French-Canadian aerialist from his show, now remarried in Montreal. One daughter, June.

**Why Merrow.** Last spring June, fifteen, told him — not angrily, which was worse — that she had seen him more often on television than in person. At the end of his 3,000th Vegas show he stepped into a glass box, vanished, and flew to Boston to sit in the third row of her debate final, which he'd missed three years running. When Simone moved to Montreal, June didn't want to go. Archie promised her "a real year in a real place." Vivienne suggested Merrow: Gus's house had sat empty for thirty-seven years. They arrived in August for June's junior year. It is now late October.

**Want (Book 1).** To make this the best year of June's life — through spectacle, because spectacle is the only love language he's fluent in. And: to beat Lorelei Crane, publicly and completely, in his grandfather's theater. And (secretly, embarrassingly): for something to be real. "I've debunked four hundred mediums. I would give everything I own for one of them to be the real thing."

**Need.** To let the people he loves backstage. He keeps everyone in the audience — even June — and calls it a gift.

**Appetite law (the engine).** Archie cannot leave a locked thing locked. A mystery is not a problem to him; it's a present someone forgot to hand him. Interest becomes participation instantly: he asks, tries, joins, recruits, buys, climbs, bets. He takes things apart to see how they work — tricks, locks, people. He *wants* things: the best pie, the biggest pumpkin, to haul a lobster trap himself, to win at poker, to make a sad person laugh. He enjoys things out loud.

**Callable moves** (rotate; never the same twice in a chapter):
- **The theory.** A wildly improbable explanation delivered with total conviction and real reasoning ("Hear me out: smugglers"). Often wrong. Occasionally exactly right in a way nobody can see yet.
- **The demonstration.** He shows how a "miracle" was done — in a diner, a hospital waiting room, a squad car — turning the room into his audience. *Group-scene engine:* he starts a public game the room must join (pick a card, place a bet, hold this, watch closely). People join, resist, heckle, or ignore him.
- **The lift.** Pickpocketing as argument. He returns the thing with a flourish. Budget: **three times in the book**, escalating cost — the first delights, the second gets him caught, the third (Tess's badge? car keys?) is a real transgression with consequence.
- **The read.** He cold-reads a person — accurately, intrusively — as kindness and surveillance in one gesture. It can wound. Budget: once per act at full power.
- **The gift.** Money, access, connections, an extravagant meal, a favor from a famous friend. Often it simply works and everyone has a wonderful time. Occasionally it's the wrong gift.
- **The sincerity drop.** The patter stops mid-sentence and he says one true, plain thing. Rare. Devastating because rare. Budget: once per sequence at most.

**Physical-business budget** (to stop drafters converging): coin walk across knuckles — **max once per three chapters**; card produced from nowhere — **max once per three chapters**; winding his watch — when thinking, any time, never described twice in one chapter; the emerald scarf as prop — sparingly. No "eyes twinkling."

**Competence.** Elite: sleight of hand, lock-picking, escapes (straitjackets, water tanks — he can hold his breath for four minutes), misdirection theory, reading an audience, cold reading, illusion design (with Lenny), stagecraft, fearless with heights and water, memory (a "memory theater" technique). **Limits / shadow:** cannot cook (tries elaborate breakfasts; mostly fails), cannot drive stick, hopeless at actual police procedure, impatient, overconfident, talks too much when nervous, gets seasick on small boats (humiliatingly — the lobstermen notice), is a terrible liar *to people he loves* (he can fool a stadium but not his mother), cannot keep a surprise, mistakes a grand gesture for an answer, loses at poker because he refuses to cheat ("It's against the code") and can't stop telegraphing his joy at a good hand. He's sometimes confidently, charmingly wrong about a person.

**Voice.** Exuberant, quick, warm, playful. Showman's patter that he deploys at people ("Ladies and gentlemen—" to an audience of one irritated detective). Extravagant vocabulary used for comic effect, never pompous. Questions as games. Callbacks. Nicknames: "Sergeant" (to Tess, with relish), "Junebug" (June hates it), "Mother" (when she's being outrageous), "Lenny" (never shortened further). American, Boston-raised but long gone; no regional dialect except "wicked" under stress. Swears never — he says "Holy *Houdini*" and similar, sparingly.
- *Peak:* "Sergeant, I have spent thirty years making things impossible for a living. This isn't impossible. This is *rude.*"
- *Plain:* "I don't know. I really don't know." / "Is she going to be all right?" / "Thanks, Bev."
- *Wrong:* "It's Warren. It's obviously Warren. Look at his *shoes.*"

**Lovability laws.** He loses small and often (poker to Bev; lobstermen ignore him; June's eye-rolls; the trunk beats him daily). He gets caught (by Tess, by his mother, by June). His wins cost somebody something. His delight escapes unauthorized (glee at a clue, then he catches himself — or doesn't). Kindness and surveillance are one gesture. A bill-collector is always on the page. He is brave when it matters and never mentions it afterward.

**Wound (never diagnosed on the page).** Everyone he has loved has had a front-row seat and no backstage pass. He was the boy in the second row the night his grandfather vanished. He has never stopped trying to work out the trick. He does not say this.

**Ceiling.** Archie is the brightest voice in every room. He is never cruel; his wit punches up or at himself. He does not make speeches about the theme. He does not explain his own psychology.

## TESS CALLOWAY — the partner and procedural foil (full presence; romance slow-burn)

**Detective Sergeant Theresa "Tess" Calloway, 38.** Maine State Police, Major Crimes Unit (Midcoast). Grew up in Merrow; her father Walt was Merrow's police chief for thirty-two years. Mother left when Tess was nine. Divorced (briefly married to a Portland lawyer; "he argued with the weather"). Moved back to Merrow eighteen months ago when her father went into memory care; lives in her father's old house on Pound Hill, which she is renovating one weekend at a time and badly.

**Look.** Five-seven, a runner's build, dark-blonde hair in a braid that is a professional decision. Gray eyes that do most of her talking. Navy MSP fleece or a charcoal wool coat, jeans, good boots, no jewelry except a plain steel watch. Drives an unmarked gray Ford Interceptor and parks it exactly between the lines.

**Want.** To run a clean case and not be fooled. Professionally: to prove to Lt. Brannock she can lead a homicide in her own hometown without being compromised by it. Personally: to protect her father's dignity, and her own.

**Need.** To let someone help her without it being a weakness — and to admit that wonder isn't the same as a lie.

**Engine.** Tess hates being fooled because her mother fooled her for nine years. She is honest to the point of discomfort, allergic to performance, precise, patient, relentless. She does things by the book because the book has never once lied to her. She *will* break a rule — rarely, deliberately, and she'll pay for it.

**Secret (planted, not revealed in Book 1 except as a single line at the end).** She has watched every one of Archie's specials, pausing and rewinding, trying to work out the methods. At twelve, she worked one out — the "Lighthouse" from his first local TV appearance — and wrote him a letter. He wrote back, three words and his initials: *You're right. Don't tell anyone.* She still has it. She has told no one. He has no idea.

**Callable moves.** The one-word answer. The silence that makes people keep talking. The eyebrow. Literalism when annoyed. Procedure as a weapon ("Is that a statement, Mr. Fairweather?"). Catching his lifts — she catches the second one. She occasionally beats him at his own game (she reads people too, differently — cops' reading vs. performers' reading). Rare, real laughter, which Archie lives for and which she resents giving him.

**Competence.** Excellent detective: thorough, smart, good interviewer, knows Maine law and her chain of custody cold, trusted by locals because she's one of them. **Limits:** too proud to ask for help; too close to this town; her father's illness makes her short-tempered; she can't cook either; she misjudges Archie as a fraud at first and has to revise it.

**Voice.** Short, dry, exact. Maine-flat when tired. Deadpan rather than witty — her best lines are understatements. Never performs.
- *Peak:* "You bet a million dollars she was a fake, and now you'd like me to prove she was a fake. Do you see my problem?"
- *Plain:* "Go home, Archie." / "Dad, it's Tess." / "Coffee. Black."
- *Wrong:* "He's a professional liar. That's literally the job."

**Relationship arc (Book 1).** Contempt → grudging use of his expertise → real partnership → rupture (Warren; then the Ivy evidence and the producer deal) → he's cut off → convergence on Hollis by different routes → anger (he didn't tell her) → she hauls him out of the harbor → a quiet repair on the wharf. No kiss in Book 1. One almost. He does not know about the letter until she quotes it.

**Ceiling.** Tess is Archie's sparring partner and gets real wins. She is never his straight man, never helpless, never wrong about procedure. She is not "softened" by his charm; she changes because of what she sees him *do.*

## JUNE FAIRWEATHER — the daughter (full presence in her chapters; B-story)

**June Fairweather, 16.** Junior at Penobscot Regional High. Archie's daughter. Serious, ambitious, a debate champion who plans on law school "to do something real." Wears thrift-store cardigans, black jeans, Doc Martens, her mother's old silver hoops; hair in a dark bun with a pencil through it. Freckles. Her father's hands, which she tries not to notice.

**Engine.** June needs the world to make sense and to be on her own terms. She has been the most adult person in her father's life since she was nine. She is mortified by spectacle and starved for ordinary attention. She wants a normal year; she is beginning to suspect she doesn't want normal, she wants *him.*

**Secret (revealed Ch 5).** She has been teaching herself magic out of Gus's books — the library's copy of *The Dead Don't Whisper* has her name on its sign-out card. She's good. She has told nobody, because it's *his* thing.

**Callable moves.** "Dad." as a full sentence. Cross-examination (she debates her father and usually wins). Research — she is a superb researcher (she's the one who knows how to use the newspaper archive). Deadpan understatement. Rare, startling enthusiasm that she immediately suppresses.

**Arc.** Wants distance from the circus → is pulled in by the case (research, her own instinct) → Archie, finally, teaches her the method of the Empty Chair → she performs it alone when he has to go → she opens the trunk.

**Friend.** Mattie Pinkham, 16, Chief Pinkham's granddaughter: sternman on her grandfather's lobster boat, cheerful, loud, unimpressed by June's father and devoted to June. Cameo-plus.

**Voice.** Precise, quick, dry, sometimes sounds forty. Never cruel to her father; exasperated, protective. Plain more often than witty.
- *Peak:* "You said a regular year. Regular dads don't buy the town hall."
- *Plain:* "Okay." / "Is Grandma all right?" / "I'm doing homework."

## VIVIENNE FAIRWEATHER — the mother (relationship anchor)

**Vivienne Fairweather, 73.** Gus's daughter. His stage assistant from twelve to twenty-four ("I was sawn in half four thousand times and I have never been the same since"). Later a Boston stage actress and acting teacher; raised Archie alone. Silver bob, cheekbones, coral lipstick, kaftans and men's tweed coats, a fondness for dramatic hats. Took over the Merrow Players' costume shop within a week of arriving. Is being courted, with great seriousness, by the harbormaster, Ozzie Lund, and is enjoying it enormously.

**Engine.** Glamour and appetite in the face of loss. She has never accepted that her father is dead; the trunk is her proof — "He said open it when I'm gone. Nobody's opened it. Draw your own conclusions." She signs up for the séance not because she believes, but because she wants to hear somebody say his name in that room.

**Callable moves.** Grand pronouncements. Calling Archie "Archibald" when he's in trouble. Outrageous candor. Stealing scenes. Sudden fierce tenderness.

**Voice.** Theatrical, sardonic, generous. She is allowed one true peak per scene; otherwise she lands effects through action (she arrives, she takes over, she exits).
- *Peak:* "Darling, I've been sawn in half by better men than you."
- *Plain:* "I know it's not him. I'm not a fool. I just wanted somebody to say his name in there."

## LENNY SZABO — the illusion engineer (relationship anchor; the "how" man)

**Lenny Szabo, 70.** Cleveland-born son of Hungarian refugees; ex-tool-and-die machinist; built every illusion Archie has ever performed. Followed Archie to Maine because "who else is going to stop you from drowning in a water tank." Lives in the carriage house workshop. Small, broad, bald, enormous white eyebrows, three sweaters at once, reading glasses on his forehead. Hates the cold, the sea, lobster, and fun. Loves Archie like a son and would rather die than say so.

**Engine.** Literal, practical, laconic. He refuses jokes. His currency is action, literalism, procedure that ignores the joke, the explanation cut off with the rest still in him. "No." is a full sentence.

**Use.** He explains mechanisms (how a trumpet floats; how a lock works; how a trapdoor can get a man from the alley to the stage in forty seconds). He's the one who gets Archie up through the trapdoor at the end.

**Voice.**
- *Typical:* "No." / "It's a hinge." / "You'll drown." / "I built it. I know what it does."
- He gets one sincere line in the book, near the end, and it is short.

## CHIEF DALE PINKHAM — Merrow PD (supporting, warm)

**Dale Pinkham, 63.** Chief of the Merrow Police Department (himself, one full-time officer — Kayla Bissonnette, 24, sharp and ambitious — and a part-timer). Walrus mustache, belly, uniform jacket that doesn't close, lobsters on weekends. Archie's single biggest fan: owns every special on DVD and has opinions about the riverboat. Good at knowing everybody, bad at hiding his delight. Not a fool — he knows the town and he's honest. Defers to the State Police because that's the law in Maine (MSP leads homicides outside Portland and Bangor).

**Voice.** "Ayuh." Easy, slow, kind, prone to long sentences about who's related to whom. Fanboy moments he is embarrassed by.

## BEV THIBODEAU — The Galley (supporting, the town's hub)

**Beverly Thibodeau, 67.** Owner of The Galley, the diner on the wharf. Widow (Roland, 2009, heart). Franco-American; flat Maine voice; reading glasses on her head; forearms like a stevedore; makes the best blueberry pie on the Midcoast and does not care what you think of it. Thoroughly unimpressed by Archie and running out of ways to show it. Beats him at poker, every time.

**Engine.** Plain speech; refusing to play along; feeding people. She knows everyone's history and gives it up only when it's useful and decent to do so.

**Voice.** "Sit down." / "Pie's blueberry or apple. There's no third pie." / "Sit down. You look like something the tide left."

## WALT CALLOWAY — Tess's father (relationship anchor; series seed)

**Walter Calloway, 74.** Merrow police chief 1978–2010. Now in Sea View memory care with Alzheimer's (moderate). Lives in the past more than the present: 1988 is vivid; last Tuesday is gone. Sometimes thinks Tess is fifteen. Big, gentle, courtly, a former high-school hockey goalie with huge hands that shake now. He was chief the night Gus vanished and closed the case as a drowning. He remembers Gus with great affection. **Seed:** "Gus Fairweather still owes me a boat." Use only where a brief calls for it; never elaborate.

## LT. NEIL BRANNOCK — MSP Major Crimes (procedural foil)

**Lieutenant Neil Brannock, 54.** Commands the Midcoast Major Crimes Unit; Tess's boss. Tall, gray crewcut, reading glasses he resents, Dunkin' coffee, wedding ring, two kids in college. Careful, ambitious, very good at his job, hates celebrities and "circus." He holds the **honest wrong answer** — first Archie, then Ivy — on real evidence, with real reasoning. Never stupid, never corrupt, never a cartoon. His best argument against Archie is the true one: *you fool cameras for a living.*

**Voice.** Clipped, procedural, occasionally dryly funny. "Mr. Fairweather, I'm sure it's a wonderful trick. I'm asking where you were standing."

## THE CASE — guest cast (Book 1 only)

### LORELEI CRANE — the medium (victim)
**Lorelei Crane, 58.** Born Lorraine Cranmer in Fall River, Massachusetts. As "Sister Lori Cranmer" (1995–2008) she worked church basements, Grange halls and private sittings across New England. Since 2014, star of the streaming hit *Lorelei Crane: The Other Side* (eight seasons). Glamorous: jet hair with a single white streak, dark-red lipstick (her signature "Crane Red"), velvet, moonstone rings on most fingers, a smoky, laughing voice. Genuinely warm with waiters and fans; remembers names; funny about herself. A fraud of the highest skill — and she half-believes her comfort is a gift. Tired. Planning to retire after this season, which Ivy wants and Teddy doesn't. She and Archie recognize each other instantly: two performers. She should be likable enough that the reader feels her death. She does not remember Merrow; she has worked two hundred towns.

**Method (planner-facing):** hand release by her plant (Ivy, always seated to her left); a luminous-banded aluminum spirit trumpet she lifts with her freed left hand and speaks through, lips to the small end, moving it in arcs; "touches" by Ivy with a damp glove on a telescoping rod; "spirit static" — Ivy kills the infrared camera feed with a pocket remote at the peak moments. Hot reading: Ivy researches sitters online and in archives beforehand.

**Voice.** Warm, quick, intimate; calls people "honey" and means it as a technique. Lands her own peaks.
- "We're in the same business, Mr. Fairweather. You send them home lighter by the price of a ticket. I send them home with their dead."

### IVY CRANE — the daughter (mirror for June)
**Ivy Crane, 29.** Raised on the road inside the act; her mother's plant, researcher and technician since she was twelve. Pale, watchful, hair scraped back, oversized gray sweaters, a silk scarf of her mother's she always wears. Speaks little; precise; dry. Loves her mother fiercely and wants out: she has a place in a veterinary-technician program in Burlington, Vermont, starting in January, and hasn't told Lorelei. After the death: grief, guilt, and lies to protect her mother's legacy and her own complicity. Then — slowly — truth.
**What she does after the death:** lied that she held her mother's hand throughout; wiped the trumpet's mouthpiece with her scarf in the first seconds after the collapse (a performer's *reset* — removing the lipstick that would expose the fraud); kept the scarf; had the remote; inherits; was overheard fighting with Lorelei about quitting. Knew the method better than anyone.
**Voice.** Spare. "She held my hand." / "I don't do interviews." / "Everyone she ever lied to is in those books."

### TEDDY MARCHAND — the producer
**Teddy Marchand, 46.** Showrunner/producer of *The Other Side*, Marchand Media, Los Angeles. Fleece vest, white sneakers, two phones, network-speak, vapes mango. Cynical about the content, sincere about the ratings. Fought with Lorelei about her leaving; Marchand Media holds a key-person insurance policy on her. Withholds the behind-the-scenes "B-roll" card from the police because it shows Ivy planting the touch rod — it would expose the show as fraud and destroy the asset. Wants: the special to air, the franchise to survive, himself to survive it.

### KEV DUCHESNE — the superfan
**Kevin Duchesne, 31.** Won a contest to sit in the séance. Runs a ghost-hunting YouTube channel, *Spectral Kev* (11,400 subscribers), from Haverhill, Massachusetts. Earnest believer, gear-laden (EMF meter, "spirit box," three batteries for everything), wears a black hoodie with his own logo. Also a lifelong Archie fan, which is causing him a crisis of faith. Snuck into the prop room the night before to film the famous trumpet. Smuggled a tiny infrared camera into the séance in his hoodie pocket against contract — its footage, which he hides out of fear of being sued, shows Lorelei's ringed hand holding the trumpet.

### SHANNON MAYO — the widow (the person being worked)
**Shannon Mayo, 34.** Waitress at The Galley. Widow of Tommy Mayo, lobsterman, lost when his boat went down off Monhegan last February. Two kids, Liam (7) and Rosie (4). Former student of Hollis's; Hollis is godmother to Rosie. Lorelei's team contacted her in the spring; three phone readings since (first free, then $350 each — she's borrowed against Tommy's boat insurance). Seated to Lorelei's right; she truly held Lorelei's right hand the whole time. Angry at Archie after the death: he's taking Tommy away from her again. Arc: from the comfort of the lie to honest grief.

### WARREN GILLEY — the select board chair
**Warren Gilley, 61.** Chair of the Merrow select board; runs Gilley Insurance & Realty, founded by his grandfather. Bow ties, tidy gray hair, a Yankee manner that becomes pomposity under pressure. Wanted the town to sell the opera house to a Boston hotel developer; Archie outbid the developer at the special town meeting and Warren took it personally. Sat in the séance as the town's representative. **Secret:** his wife Carol has early-onset Alzheimer's and is in Sea View (same unit as Walt Calloway); her care is bankrupting him; he has been quietly selling opera-house memorabilia (Gus's 1950s posters, two 1899 gaslight sconces) on eBay before the sale closed. Lorelei, hot-reading from Ivy's research, hinted at it to him before the séance. He borrowed Hollis's prop-room key on Friday — to take a poster.

### HOLLIS TIBBETTS — the house manager
**Hollis Tibbetts, 66.** Taught English and drama at the regional high school for thirty-five years (she taught Tess, Shannon, Mattie's mother, half the town). Runs the Merrow Players at the opera house and is its volunteer house manager — keeps every key on a ring the size of a dinner plate; runs the light board from the balcony booth for every show. Short, sturdy, a white crop of hair, reading glasses on a beaded chain, fingerless wool gloves (the opera house is always cold; she's always sewing), men's cardigans with stuffed pockets, a bark of a laugh. Quotes Shakespeare and Gus's book with equal ease. Gardener: a famous fall border of dahlias and tall blue monkshood ("the deer won't touch it"). First person in Merrow to welcome Archie; gave him the keys; talked him out of canceling the séance ("Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house."). Dear friends with Vivienne within a week. Godmother to Shannon's Rosie.

**On the page:** warm, funny, practical, beloved, present in many scenes as stagehand/friend. Never spotlighted, never sinister, never "mysteriously sad" — she is the town's beloved aunt. She's a widow, as many harbor-town women are; that's ordinary here.

**Voice.** Brisk, warm, teacherly, dry: "Sit up straight, Sergeant, I can still give you detention." Quotes Shakespeare when moved. Never sentimental.
