## Ch 29 — The Method (Wed Oct 29, ~10 p.m., carriage house)
- **The rehearsal platform** (invented): Lenny built it in the first week from plywood and four-by-fours, the same height as the opera-house stage. It has a practice trap the size of Gus's, 2 ft × 4 ft, with two leaves hinged at the center. Underneath: three wooden steps, a counterweight on a rope, a sandbag for Lenny to sit on. The practice chair is a **ladder-back taken from the Fairweather kitchen**. The show chair at the opera house is **bentwood**, as Ch 37 has it, and Ch 31 matches.
- **THE METHOD (canon for Ch 31, 37, 40):** an oval of spring steel ("a very good coat hanger") is sewn into a channel in the muslin sheet about 2 ft below the top. It catches on the chair-back and holds the cloth out at shoulder width, so the draped chair *already looks occupied*. The sheet is long at the back and pools on the floor. The chair stands on the front leaf. The back leaf drops on its counterweight, felted and silent. The person comes up the steps behind the chair, inside the sheet, and sits. That takes about 8 seconds. **Gus's count lasts 11 seconds**: the patter stretches "one…two…three". The performer **looks at the audience for the whole count and never at the sheet** ("Where you look, they look"; "You can't check"). The reveal pulls the sheet up and back, hoop and all, throws it behind, and never looks at it again.
- **Finale plan as stated:** Vivienne comes up into the chair. Archie stands by the chair, drapes the sheet and holds the corner. June does the patter and the count and pulls the sheet.
- **Gus's patter opening:** "every trick was a promise". Ch 40 has June quote this. In her perfect run June starts with Gus's line, then makes it her own: the chair came out of this kitchen, and many bad breakfasts were eaten on it, mostly cooked by her father. Ch 40's "his own patter… she'd changed them" fits this.
- June wears Lenny's work gloves ("nobody touches my trap with bare hands"). She has found a 1979 video of Gus's Chair, filmed from the balcony "by somebody's uncle", with a man audibly unwrapping a sandwich.
- The wrong run: June counts too fast, and Archie is caught on hands and knees with one leg in the trap. Lenny: "Ankle." Second run: she counts slowly but looks at the sheet, and Archie hears it from inside. **The perfect run uses Lenny under the sheet**, with Archie watching from a bucket ("from the house"). Lenny appears in three sweaters, glasses on his forehead, "a man waiting for a bus". Archie walks out without being able to speak.
- **New backstory (invented — please check against Ch 17/28):** at Gus's last show in 1988, ten-year-old Archie sat in the 2nd row on the left, on the aisle, eating peppermints "somebody's husband" bought him at the Galley. **Vivienne was under the stage and was the person revealed in the Chair**, in her old sequins. The whole hall stood up; they hadn't seen her on that stage since she was 24. During the count Gus looked straight at Archie. Afterward Archie waited in the green room because Gus had said he'd walk him home, and fell asleep on a pile of coats. This is consistent with Ch 35 (Hollis ran the board for the Chair in 1988) and Ch 40 (Vivienne "coming up through Gus's trapdoors since she was twelve").
- "I think he did the trick." / "Then where did he go?" / "I don't know." Archie refers back to telling Dale on Monday (Ch 15) that he was in the second row.
- June asked to do the Chair "that afternoon, at the foot of the scaffold" (Ch 28). Vivienne-in-the-chair was "settled over lobster rolls" at Paint Day. **Ch 28's drafter should confirm this staging.** Archie has paint under his nails and a stripe of it in his hair; no color is named.
- Exit: Dale calls from his kitchen. The AG is filing murder on Ivy tomorrow, and Okafor has been calling the station all evening and wants Archie at the jail at 10.

## Ch 30 — Visiting Hours (Thu Oct 30, ~10 a.m., Knox County Jail)
- **New minor character:** CO **Doucette**, who confiscates Archie's "very ambitious hairpin" (a lock pick). Archie's pocket contents: keys, wallet, a deck of cards, two half-dollars, peppermints.
- **Okafor (details invented):** fifties, compact, black wool coat, flat shoes, close-cropped hair silver at the temples, a briefcase older than her client, a fountain pen. The wedding was **eleven years ago**. She asked Archie to vanish the cake because her mother-in-law chose it and it was lemon; the hotel kitchen staff ate it, and she sent a thank-you note on letterhead. Archie is paying her invoices. Her ground rules: no discussion of his statement or the scarf. Her purpose is a third party.
- Ivy wears an orange jail sweatshirt and a rubber band instead of a hair elastic; no scarf.
- **Ivy tells the act:** she was 12, in a motel in Nashua, practicing with a hairbrush in the dark. Two squeezes, then let go one finger at a time. The rod is under the cloth at her place, with a wet glove on the end. The remote is up her left sleeve. "Teddy calls it the static. Teddy has always known." She has wiped the horn after every séance since she was 12. "I wasn't hiding anything on Saturday night except her."
- Archie's read draws on Kev's Trumpet Hiatus (Ch 21): "You drop a routine for two reasons. It's stopped working, or it hurt somebody." He has "sat with forty" trumpets. Then: "What happened in 2003?"
- **Ivy's memory:** she was 7, it was raining, the car was parked **outside a church in Rockland**, and she sat in the back seat with a coloring book. Lorelei came out with a newspaper under her coat and cried with the wipers off. "She said a man had died." Ivy asked about McDonald's. Years later, drunk in a hotel bar in **Phoenix**, Lorelei said she never did the horn on a private sitting again "after **the man in Maine**." Ivy doesn't know who he was.
- **The books:** thirty years of appointment books in Lorelei's own shorthand, in a hatbox in **Pru's office safe**, moved there on Sunday as "Mother's private things". Okafor writes the release on her legal pad ("one hatbox, contents, from the custody of Prudence Haskell"). Ivy signs and Okafor countersigns with her bar number.
- Ivy: "Read them. Then you'll know what she was." Archie: "I already liked her. That's the problem."
- **Exit:** in the corridor Tess, with an unnamed trooper and a thick file, braid wet from rain that has started up again, glances at the half-open paper showing two signatures. Neither speaks. Okafor goes off toward the interview rooms, which sets up Ch 34, where Okafor tells Tess about "a man in Maine, 2003".

## Ch 32 — The Hatbox (Thu 9:30 p.m. → Fri ~3 a.m.)
- **The hatbox** is round and green-striped, from a Newbury Street (Boston) milliner, with a cord handle. It holds **thirty cheap drugstore notebooks**, one per year from 1995 to last year, with rubber bands. **No years are written in them.** Entries are dated as weekday + month/day (e.g. *Th 3/9*).
- **The code:** it's from **Chapter nine of *The Dead Don't Whisper*, "The Billet and the Blue Book"** (invented), a Spiritualist-camp shorthand from the 1890s that Gus printed as a fold-out table. Consonants only; numbers for relations; ○ = passed; ~ = water; a barred T = trumpet; a check = "it landed". Archie memorized it at nine "I thought there'd be a test". This pays off Lorelei's Ch 6 line that every medium learned from Gus's book.
- **June's win #1:** she dates every book by working out which years a weekday/date pair falls in, cross-checked against towns and prices, with yellow sticky notes. **June's win #2:** she spots that every Mrs. seems to have lost a *wife*, meaning Lorelei shifted every relation number by one to keep sitters who'd read Gus from reading her notes.
- Sample human entries: Mrs. Albert P., Biddeford ("did he suffer", $40). An old man in Saco worried his wife is still mad about the dog ($25, ten given back, paid in quarters). A man in Bangor looking for the will: "look in the freezer (It's always the freezer.)". A 2000 entry: "I., four today. Pancakes. No sittings."
- **The Rockland books** cover 2001–2003: three books, 41 Thursday sittings. Raw first entry: *Th 10/4. Rcklnd, Pk St. — P.L., Mrrw. Lbstr. 6○ D. 15. ~ qry, 8/01. W. H. dsnt kno — NO calls hse. $350.* After that: December, "Son spoke through T. 'Dad.'"; "special sittings" at $2,500. June totals it on an envelope (the figure isn't stated on the page) and Archie turns it face down. The checked lines are "It's warm here", "I'm not cold anymore" and, in Oct 2003, "Come see me on the water". Then: *11/20. P.L. lost at sea. Saw it in the paper.* / ***God forgive me.*** (the only plain longhand in thirty years) / *No more T for privates.* This matches Ch 38's quotation.
- **The click:** Archie connects the memorial name from Ch 3 (he recites the list). June connects H. Langley 12/03 on the card ("I've signed under it three times"). Archie remembers Ch 17: Monday night, chowder, Malvolio on his knees, the photo of a boy on a dock, "Danny. My son," a velvet waistcoat, talk of buttons. He doesn't say any of it. **June names Mrs. Tibbetts herself, then argues it away:** she's Tibbetts, not Langley; there are fifty H's in town; "lots of people are named Danny"; it's a coincidence to check; Deb opens at 9 and the *Packet* is on microfilm. She says "We'll check it. In the morning. Both of us." He says only "Goodnight, June." He tells no lie, but keeps her in the audience. This matches Ch 37's breakfast line "Did you check the Danny thing?".
- June wears pajama pants and Archie's old **Harvard Square sweatshirt**. Vivienne went up at nine with a hot-water bottle. Fog came in after supper.
- Exit: Archie winds the watch at about 2 a.m. and sits up with his hand on "God forgive me" until it's time for the Galley.

- Archie is wearing the **buffalo-check coat** (Ch 35 has it on Friday too; whoever drafts Ch 10–28 needs to get it back from Ivy). Bev won't unlock until 5:00 exactly. The marine forecast calls for fog, visibility under a mile, seas two to four feet.

Danny died at the quarry in **August '01, at 15, off the high ledge with the other boys**. **At 12 he ate a whole blueberry pie on Archie's stool on a $5 bet with Roland**, and Roland paid. Peter went out in November, about two years later, alone, in a gale warning, mid-afternoon, on **the *Hollis T.***; she fetched up on Shag Ledge and he was found two days later. Hollis sat in the back booth every morning that winter and wouldn't eat; Bev fed her anyway.
