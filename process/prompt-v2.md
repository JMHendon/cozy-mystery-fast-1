# Prompt v2 — cozy mystery, Book 1

Work in the `cozy-mystery-fast-1` repo. This task is the novel only; don't touch other repos.

**Task.** Write Book 1 of a cozy mystery series (75–85k words), good enough to be a breakout bestseller that makes readers buy Book 2.

**Order of authority.** A higher item wins, and nothing lower can waive anything higher. That includes a reviewer, a reader score and your own judgment.
1. The Hard Gates below.
2. The Lead spec below.
3. The genre craft documents in `craft/`. Ignore anything marked series-specific.
4. Reviewer and reader opinions.

If two of these conflict, follow the higher one and list the conflict in your final report.

**Hard Gates.** These are pass/fail. Check them in the outline, after drafting and after every revision pass.
1. Someone dies on the page in Chapter 1 or 2. That death is the inciting incident. Nothing delays it: not a scheduled event, not setup, not charm.
2. Page 1 shows the lead at full size, doing what he's famous for, in front of people, enjoying himself.
3. The lead and his police partner meet and spar by Chapter 3. They share scenes throughout, with no gap longer than three chapters.
4. The lead commits to investigating by Chapter 3, for a reason rooted in his character.
5. Every suspect, including the killer, is on the page by the end of Act I (about 25% of the book).
6. Fair play: every clue the solution uses is on the page before the reveal. The lead reaches the solution by deduction, not only through a confession.
7. Cozy conventions: no on-page gore, no sex, minimal profanity, an amateur sleuth, a close community, a climax in motion, and a world restored with one visible cost.
8. Most chapters are under 2,000 words.

**The Lead.** Model him on Richard Castle from *Castle* (ABC, 2009–16). Sex, job and age can differ; the energy cannot. Concretely:
- He enters at the top of his game, not recovering from a fall. The case is a new toy, not penance.
- He is the funniest, most delighted person in most rooms. His wit is in his own dialogue, said to people's faces, not in the narration.
- He is thrilled by murder in a slightly inappropriate way, and says so.
- He theorizes out loud constantly: wild, entertaining, sometimes right. His partner swats the theories down.
- He is often right and annoying about it. The instinct from his day job wins real points in the case.
- He uses fame, money and connections freely, and it usually works.
- He is vain and self-deprecating at once. He flirts. He is brave without thinking about it.
- At home he's the kid and his family are the grown-ups, but he is a devoted, tender parent.
- His flaws can cost him, but the book never treats his larger-than-life nature as the thing to cure. He grows; he doesn't shrink.

Test every chapter with two questions: "Would Castle say this out loud?" and "Would a reader want to *be* him in this chapter?" Copy this section word for word into every drafting and revision brief you give a sub-agent.

**Before planning.** Read at least two books in Dropbox → Obsidian → Book Writing → Book Comparisons. For each, record the chapter of the first death, when the sleuth and partner first meet, typical chapter length and how the lead is introduced. Use that table as your structural benchmark.

**Craft documents.** Use `craft/` for craft guidance. Where a craft rule restrains wit, voice or display, it applies to supporting characters, never to the lead.

**Checkpoints: stop and wait for me**
1. After the premise, the lead's character sheet and three sample pages of his voice.
2. After the outline, which must include a table showing where each Hard Gate is met.
3. After Chapters 1–3 are drafted.

After the third checkpoint, finish the book without stopping.

**Review.** Simulated readers are not validation. Ask them specific questions:
- Where were you tempted to stop?
- When did you expect the murder?
- Did you want to *be* the lead, or just spend time with him?
- Who was the funniest person in the book?

A reader saying the opening is slow, or that someone other than the lead is the most magnetic character, is a blocking finding. Line edits may not cut the lead's dialogue or jokes to fix prose tics. After every edit pass, confirm he is still the brightest voice on the page.

**Success** means a reader can't put the book down, falls for the lead and wants to be him, finds the ending cathartic and wants Book 2 immediately. In the final report, list each Hard Gate with the chapter that meets it, and name the places you're least sure about.
