## The premise (carried over; framing is yours)

During Halloween week in Merrow, Maine, America's most beloved TV medium, Lorelei Crane, films a séance in the town's 1899 opera house. The opera house was recently bought by Archie Fairweather, the world's most famous magician and debunker of psychics, whose grandfather vanished from the same stage on Halloween 1988. In the dark, with every hand in the circle supposedly held, Lorelei dies, poisoned through her own spirit trumpet. The killer is the town's beloved retired English teacher, Hollis Tibbetts, avenging a husband the medium talked into the sea twenty-two years ago.

**The structural problem you must solve first:** the séance *is* the death, so the séance must happen in Chapter 1 or 2 (gate 1). Anything the old book staged before it (Lorelei's arrival, the challenge to her, Archie's history with her, Shannon's readings, Hollis talking Archie out of cancelling) now has to be delivered around the death, inside the séance chapters, or afterward through the investigation, or cut. Archie appears within the first three pages (gate 2). The book may open on the séance itself.

## The lead

**Reference character:** Richard Castle from *Castle* (ABC, 2009–16). Archie's job, age and setting differ; the energy does not.

**What that means on the page:**
- He enters at the top of his game. Leaving Las Vegas was his choice and he's having a wonderful time: he bought a theater, a town and a hearse. The case is a new toy, not penance.
- He is the funniest, most delighted person in most rooms. His wit is in his own dialogue, said to people's faces, not in the narration.
- He is thrilled by murder in a slightly inappropriate way, and says so.
- He theorises out loud constantly: wild, entertaining, sometimes right. Tess swats the theories down.
- He is often right and annoying about it. A magician's eye for method and misdirection wins real points in the case.
- He uses fame, money and connections freely, and it usually works.
- He is vain and self-deprecating at once. He flirts (with Tess, who doesn't want him to). He is brave without thinking about it.
- At home he's the kid and his family are the grown-ups, but he is a devoted, tender father to June.
- His flaws can cost him, but the book never treats his larger-than-life nature as the thing to cure. He grows; he doesn't shrink.

**Tests for every chapter:** "Would Castle say this out loud?" and "After this chapter, would a reader want to spend a day with Archie, or be like him?"

**Copy this whole "The lead" section word for word into every drafting and revision brief you give a sub-agent.**

## The partner / official foil

Detective Sergeant Tess Calloway, Maine State Police Major Crimes (`carryover/ensemble.md`), is the Beckett to Archie's Castle: competent, unimpressed, the one person he can't charm, and the one whose rare laugh he lives for. She is on the page no later than the chapter after Archie commits to the investigation (gate 4). The partnership is the engine: they spar from their first meeting and share scenes throughout, with no gap longer than three chapters. Slow burn; no kiss in Book 1.

## Review of Chapters 1–4

- Run the gate check first. Gates 1–4 must PASS in the prose itself, not only in the outline.
- Get at least one blind cold reading of Chapters 1–4 from a separate sub-agent. Give it only the chapters, no plan or solution, and tell it nothing about what the book intends. Ask it:
  - Where were you tempted to stop, and why?
  - When did you expect something to happen that hadn't happened yet?
  - Who was the funniest person?
  - Which character would you most like to spend a day with?
  - Which character would you most like to be like?
  - What do you think happened, and who do you suspect?
  - Would you read Chapter 5 right now?
- Simulated readers are not validation. Blocking findings, to fix before finishing: a reader saying the opening is slow, or readers consistently naming someone other than Archie in answer to *both* of the last two questions.
- Run the lead-presence check before and after every revision pass. Line edits may not cut Archie's dialogue or jokes to fix prose tics. After every pass, confirm Archie is still the brightest voice on the page.

## Success means

1. A reader can't put it down.
2. A reader falls in love with Archie (and the ensemble) and wants to spend time with him or *be* him.
3. The ending is cathartic.
4. The reader wants Book 2 immediately.

For this run: Chapters 1–4 make a reader desperate for Chapter 5, and the plan credibly delivers 1–4 above for the whole book.
