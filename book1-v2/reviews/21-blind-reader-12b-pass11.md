## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never wanted to close the book, but I coasted in three places.

**The set-up block in Chapter 1.** The scene is moving, then it stops for: "Half an hour ago, with the house lights up and two cameras on him, he had searched everything Lorelei had brought…" The flashback carries the rules (the million, the tea, the trumpet) and the seating chart. I didn't have a clear picture of the table. "Kev, on Archie's left," "Warren Gilley, two seats to Archie's right, past Vivienne," Ivy "at her left elbow," Shannon at the right. I rebuilt the circle on scratch paper to see who could have a hand free. The whole mystery rests on that geometry, and the book makes the reader do the work.

**The run of debunkings.** The toe, the free hand, the rod, the glove, the remote each get the same treatment: trick, then "Archie murmured," then instant solution. "You owe me ten dollars." It's delightful for three beats. By the glove I wanted him to be wrong once. The trumpet voice rescues it by breaking the pattern.

**The kitchen in Chapter 3.** This is the slackest stretch. He explains the funnel for the third time (to the table, to Calloway, to June). The "Don't say gift" / "I was going to say *puzzle*" exchange is a sitcom button. The rules negotiation with June ("Vegas. 2019." / "That was a *cloak.*") is warm, but it only reassures me that he's a good father, which I already believed. The vase is the only thing in the scene that moves the plot.

A bigger worry is that I've now been stonewalled about six times. Sparrow, the trunk, the Glass Box, Calloway's sticker, Warren's whisper and Lenny's silence each get a deflection: "Go to bed, Archibald." "Go to bed, darling." "Someday." "Don't." After the third, I saw the machinery.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **The voices.** Archie explains the toe, the bell, the glove and the static, then says "I know how everything *she* did was done. I don't know how this was." He never accounts for the young man's voice "off a boat." That's the only trick that mattered, and I expected it by Chapter 3.
- **The trunk.** "Seven letters" sits next to a nickname with seven letters, S-P-A-R-R-O-W. I expected someone to say it. Archie is a man who lifts a cop's watch and a nurse's card, and the ink vial is only for people who "forced it." Nothing stops him from trying a word except his mother sitting on the lid.
- **The footage.** Brannock "watched the footage at two this morning." That footage presumably has a dead woman saying "The trunk, Sparrow." Calloway never asks Archie about it, in the corridor or at the Galley. I expected that question in Chapter 4.
- **Cost for being right.** Archie is correct about every trick, then about the poison, then about the scarf. The first real price is the sticker. I kept waiting for another.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Funniest: Archie in Chapter 1, and only there.** "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" is the best joke in the four chapters. "Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down." and "It's a spirit, not an audit" are close. After that he turns into a quip dispenser, and the quips get weaker. "Smart. Most people only count their fingers" doesn't parse. She said she'd count her fingers, so how is that the usual behavior?

**Most magnetic: Lorelei.** In about eight pages she gets "Brother Amos is with us. He senses a skeptic," the refused search, the tea she takes anyway, and "the corner of her smeared red mouth moved." Killing her at the end of Chapter 2 hurts the book. It has removed its best antagonist before she's had twenty lines, and Archie telling me "I liked her" doesn't replace her.

**Close behind:**
- **Bev:** "Everything that comes back gets washed." And "You did," said Bev. "As a protest."
- **Calloway:** "You were a lot more pleasant at nine." / "I was nine." She's best when she says almost nothing.
- **Vivienne:** "It is a *centerpiece*," and "A woman with a tin horn doesn't get to change it… I have lived on that rule." That's the strongest emotional writing in the four chapters.
- **Lenny:** "Is not complicated."

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Spend time with him, in doses. The narration is his biggest fan: "Hardest-working woman in show business," "Archie nearly loved him," "He could make forty thousand people believe a woman had vanished." I'm being told he's magnetic more often than I'm shown it.

The moments I leaned toward him are all places where the act cracks:
- The sticker: "he saw that he'd got it wrong, and wrong somewhere that hurt."
- Shannon: "He should have said he was sorry. He knew it while he was opening his mouth."
- Her reply when he defends her: "I don't need you to do that."
- June: "You're doing the hands."

I'd rather be Calloway or Bev. They have something at stake and don't announce it. Archie has a fortune, an adoring daughter, a loyal inventor, a building and a hearse, and the only thing the book says he wants is: "Secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would [collect]." I'd like to see that wish tested.

## 5. What happened, and who

**Working theory.** Someone dosed the narrow end of the trumpet after Archie's raspberry at 7:36, or got it into Lorelei another way. The thermos also stays live, since Calloway fixates on it, and Archie's "She was fine for forty minutes" is too quick a dismissal. Someone inside Merrow fed Lorelei's team local material ("A girl called me in April"). Lorelei's team then used it to draw out the Gus material and "Sparrow." The killing probably silences her before she says more about Gus.

One clue I liked: "The answer's in the—" cut off, and Archie reads it as stagecraft. I suspect it was the poison starting, not a fade. The text half-confirms this when he berates himself in the hospital: "he had heard her voice start to go, and admired the craft."

**Ranking (low confidence):**
1. **Ivy.** She has means and behavior: the false "She held my hand. The whole time," the wipe, the coat she won't take off. But she's the obvious pick and that's the book's tell. I'd bet she's covering something (the fakery, the family business) rather than murdering her mother. Keeping a poisoned scarf in her pocket for hours isn't what a killer does.
2. **Warren.** This is the only specific motive on the page. Lorelei whispers, Warren goes "translucent," cousin Alden drives to warn them, and there's a "clause" in the sale. Two problems: that whisper isn't in Chapter 1's account of the tea scene, so it feels like an unfair retroactive clue, and Warren was hand-held on both sides during the séance. The loud public accusation makes Warren a likely decoy, though.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** "We had a *plan*." What plan? He pushes the million-dollar angle ("Technically, something just happened in that room you can't explain"). His grief for "Lori" reads as real, so maybe a stunt went wrong.
4. **Hollis Tibbetts.** She runs the lights and knows every trapdoor and every story about Gus. She appears briefly, helpfully, and is never examined. That's why I'd keep an eye on her.
5. **Vivienne.** She isn't a killer, but she cut monkshood that afternoon, she knows who Sparrow is, and she's the person most likely to be withholding the actual secret.

Archie is off the list because of the POV. Chief Pinkham comes in "from the back of the hall," and I suspect the book likes him too much.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 now?

Yes, mostly for Calloway. Specifically, I want:
- **The Brannock interview** at nine, with Margaret Okafor in the room and Calloway watching Archie fail to be charming.
- **A pointed question about Sparrow and the trunk** from someone with the footage.
- **Shannon's "April" lead chased down.** That's the best clue so far, and Archie asked the right question to get it.
- **What the footage actually shows** between 7:36 and 8:00.
- **A mistake with consequences.** The Warren accusation has to cost him more than a call from the town attorney.
- **Lenny, not just the Glass Box tease.** Lenny leaves the room in silence.

I want fewer "go to bed" deflections and at least one answer.

## 7. What I had to reread, couldn't picture or didn't believe

- **Reread:** "Smart. Most people only count their fingers." It's muddled at the point where it's trying to be the flirt.
- **Reread:** the watch theft. When did he take it? She moves him by the elbow and he later holds out the watch, and the handoff isn't staged. I liked the beat once I worked it out.
- **Couldn't picture:** the table (see #1), and how the voices were produced.
- **Didn't believe:** that the poison-obsessed Archie lectures a kitchen on aconite for five minutes with a vase of monkshood in the center of the table. It's funny, though, and the "centerpiece" payoff earns it.
- **Didn't believe:** that Archie obeys his mother about the trunk. It works as a character moment, but it's out of key with the man who steals watches.
- **Didn't believe:** that Calloway, with the "trunk" and "Sparrow" footage in hand, doesn't ask about them.
- **A pattern, not a flaw.** Every chapter ends on a button line: "Nobody touches that trumpet," "a voice in the dark saying *Sparrow*," the bell buoy, "Then I'd better be right." By Chapter 4 I could predict the cadence. Skip one.
