I never wanted to put it down, but I got itchy in a few places.

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I wasn't tempted in Chapter 1. It's the best thing here, and I'd have said so at the first page break.

**Chapter 3's kitchen reenactment is the first real stall.** I'd just watched Archie narrate every trick live ("Toe." "Two." "Three. I'm sorry, Mother."), and then he walks June through all of it again with a spoon, a dish glove and a funnel: "Seven people. All holding hands. The medium here, where I am… Three raps. That's her toe." Reading it, I felt the book catching up to itself. The funnel adds one idea: she talked through it for ten minutes before her mouth went numb. The rest is recap, and the whole of Chapter 1 had already made it clear.

**The Galley in Chapter 4 is a roster.** Within a page I was handed Bev, Pinkham, Shannon, Hollis, the liars' table and the co-op men. Bev and Hollis do the same job: gruff, tender, thermos-owning women who tell Archie to eat ("Eat something." / "She's right. Eat."). I had to stop and sort them.

**The Spindrift riff is a joke that goes nowhere.** "Captain's house, widow's walk, signal lamp, tunnel to the—" doesn't pay off, and the pie bit that follows is better.

The stakes also feel soft by Chapter 4. The state police sergeant's response to a man who poured the victim's drink on camera is a voluntary interview at nine. Nobody is scared of anything.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **SPARROW.** The trunk has "Seven brass letter-dials." Sparrow is seven letters. I had the combination by the end of Chapter 1, and then Archie types "S. Then P. Then A." and stops on principle ("Not from you."). I liked the principle, but I was waiting for the dials to turn, and the book made me wait while I was ahead of him. I also wondered why he's been failing "every night since August" when Lenny, who "built every illusion Archie had ever performed," is in the carriage house. Why not just cut it open?
- **The November 15 deadline.** "The town would hand him the keys on November fifteenth" was planted once and never touched again.
- **Detectives acting on the monkshood.** He texts her a photo of the murder weapon in his kitchen at 12:40 a.m. and gets "Don't touch it." In Chapter 4, at 6 a.m., no one has collected it. I expected a patrol car.
- **Calloway's care home.** The "SEA VIEW" sticker and "Don't" is a promise. I'm waiting for it to be paid.
- **Gus's voice.** Archie says he knows how everything was done, yet the old man's "*Viv… Vivi… Sparrow*" is the one thing he can't explain. I expected him to chase it harder than he does.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Lorelei Crane**, and she's dead by page nine of Chapter 2. "Forgive him. He's from Las Vegas." "Honey, are you going to talk through my whole séance?" She's the one performer who beats Archie in the dark. Killing her is a bold choice, but the book is now a little emptier than its first chapter, and her "Not the spirits, honey" is the last great line in it.

**Close behind:**
- **Vivienne**, who "had been sawn in half four thousand times and had never once screamed." "It was a very nice glove." "I didn't know it was a murder weapon, Archibald. I thought it was a *flower*."
- **Bev**, who has the best dry rhythm in the book. "There's no third pie." "Then the word is order eggs." "You did. As a protest."
- **Calloway**, who is funny through withholding. "Find a different occupation." "Don't be." "I was nine."
- **June**, with "Vegas. 2019." / "That was a *cloak.*"

Lenny's funnel line ("Is not complicated") and Teddy's "We do not say that word" are good one-offs.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Spend time with him, and mostly in Chapter 1. "Having the time of his life" made me want to be him. That fades.

He's almost never wrong. He nails the toe, the bell, the remote, the glove, Calloway's sticker and the aconite, and the people around him mostly exist to be impressed or briefly hurt. The hurt gets forgiven fast. Calloway's "Don't" gets an apology and a half-smile. Shannon's "You took him away from me" is the most real thing in Chapter 4, but Hollis follows it with "You were wonderful, and then you were kind," which tells me how to feel. I like the man who can say "I'd arrest me." I'd like him better if he had something to lose beyond Nov 15, a mistake that costs him, or a sign of what's under the patter besides June's "You do the hands."

## 5. What I think happened, and my suspects

Aconite reached her between about 7:36, when Archie's raspberry into the trumpet was safe, and 8:17. Only the tea or her own mouth fits. In the dark, only Ivy and Lorelei had a free hand. The tea is the cleaner story, and the book has just had the one container that could prove it scrubbed.

1. **Ivy Crane.** Pure evidence: the scarf, the lie to Calloway ("She held my hand. The whole time."), the dry-eyed coat she won't take off. I distrust it, because the book pointed at her twice. Her "Okay" and the collapse felt real to me. She's either the killer or protecting a fraud, and I lean toward the second.
2. **Hollis Tibbetts.** She wrote to Lorelei's people in May and pushed Archie in September ("Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house."). She runs the booth and the keys. A lifelong townie and Players director would also know the family nicknames that "Gus" used. That's the best explanation for how Sparrow got into Lorelei's mouth. Her speech to Archie ("I'm the one who wrote to those people") reads like someone pre-confessing a lesser sin.
3. **Vivienne.** "Don't you dare… Not yet." She seemed to know what was coming. She also cut the monkshood that morning. I suspect she's hiding something, not committing murder.
4. **Teddy Marchand.** He's the only one who mentions money, ratings and "a *plan*," and he runs the monitors in the wing. But his grief looked real, and a dead star doesn't obviously help him.
5. **Bev.** The washing is too loudly planted ("Everything that comes back gets washed") to be the answer. But the book also gives Hollis a lookalike thermos ("HOLLIS on its masking tape"), so I suspect a swap.
6. **Lenny.** He refused to come, and he's the one with the skills and the carriage house.
7. **Shannon, Kev and Warren.** Shannon has no plausible motive. Kev and Warren are comic relief.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 now?

Yes. What's pulling me is Calloway, Archie and the 9 a.m. interview, not the puzzle. What I want next:

- The interview with Margaret Okafor and the thermos on the table. I want Calloway to make Archie uncomfortable and to cost him something.
- Archie being **wrong** about something. He's ahead of everyone in every scene.
- Vivienne confronted about Sparrow.
- Ivy's scarf and the cups going to a lab.
- A reason the deadline matters.
- Someone besides Archie who's actually scared.

## 7. Rereads, pictures and disbelief

- **The seating.** Seven seats, a circle, and nobody in a seven-seat circle sits "exactly opposite." I couldn't build the chart, and Archie's "probably her left thumb" for Ivy's remote confused me. Ivy sits on Lorelei's left, so her free hand should be her right, and her left is holding Kev's. I also wasn't convinced Ivy had enough hands for the bell, the rod, the glove and the remote. In a magician's book, that's the one place I'd expect fair play.
- **The monkshood on the kitchen table.** A bouquet of the murder weapon, cut that morning, in the suspect's own house, is a coincidence that reads as frame-up. The comedy covers it, but I noticed.
- **A hospital hearing "aconite" from a magician.** Fine as charm, but it's quick: the nurse folds, and the doctor checks Archie's pulse like he's a patient.
- **Calloway's position.** She's a Major Crimes sergeant who grew up in Merrow, is on a first-name basis with the chief and the diner owner, and is leading the case. That's a conflict of interest the book doesn't seem to notice.
- **The card.** Archie "lifted" Calloway's card from the charge nurse, but I never saw it left there. Small, but I reread to check.
