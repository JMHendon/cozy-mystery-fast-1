# A reader's report on Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never really wanted to quit. Chapter 1 earns a lot of goodwill. But my attention sagged in three places.

**June's recap in Chapter 3.** She ticks off on her fingers what the reader already knows: "You poured her a drink on camera. You told the police what poisoned her before any doctor did. You put your mouth on the trumpet. You bet her a million dollars." That's a plot summary in dialogue, and I was ahead of it. The scene's real payload is the "Dad. You're the suspect" turn, and the book spends two pages getting there. The "promise me you'll stay out of it" handshake is also the most predictable beat so far. Everyone knew he'd promise and then do the hands.

**The repeated bit.** Chapter 3 has "Hear me out, though. The producer. Teddy." Chapter 4 has "Hear me out, though. The producer. Teddy." That's the same sentence, the same suspect, and the same deflation, one chapter apart. The "I made that up *thirty seconds ago*" gag works once. By the second time, the book is running a pattern: Archie floats a theory, a woman tells him to eat his eggs. I started to feel the rhythm instead of the people.

**Chapters 2–4 as one long aftermath.** Hospital, kitchen, diner. Each is people talking about the séance. Nothing new has been *done* since the trumpet hit the floor. Each scene is funny, but I noticed I was waiting for a scene that wasn't a conversation.

## 2. What I expected that hadn't happened

- **Ivy and the scarf.** At the end of Chapter 2, Archie hands Calloway the most damning fact in the book: the wipe, the pocketed scarf, and "She hasn't taken the coat off since." Calloway's response is to ask Dr. Rao to flag the blood. In Chapters 3 and 4 nobody mentions the scarf again. Neither does Calloway, who goes after the *thermos*. I expected a search warrant, or at least a scene where someone asks Ivy for the coat. The silence reads as the author parking a thread, not the detective being clever.
- **The warning about the clause.** Pinkham says Warren is on the phone about "the kind [of clause] with your name in it." It's a planted threat to the November 15 closing, and nothing follows.
- **Sparrow.** Chapter 1 said "The answer's in the—". Chapter 3 gives us the trunk and a cute beat where he refuses SPARROW, since seven letters means it's bait. I liked that move. But I expected the *voice* to be explained. How did a TV medium get a Maine-accented Gus, and Gus's pet names for his daughter?
- **Lorelei.** I expected her to matter longer. See below.

## 3. Most magnetic and funniest

**Lorelei Crane, and it isn't close.** She's on the page for about one chapter and she out-charms everyone in the book. "Amos doesn't have hands, honey." "Forgive him. He's from Las Vegas." "Honey, are you going to talk through my whole séance?" Her last line is "Not the spirits, honey," said while dying, and it's the best line in the four chapters. The structural problem is that the book kills its most alive character and then has to run on grief for a hundred pages. I missed her by Chapter 3.

**Close behind, for the surviving cast:**
- **Bev.** "Then the word is order eggs." "There's no third pie." "You did. As a protest." She's a single-note joke, but the note is always dead-on. She's also the only person who gets to refuse Calloway.
- **Vivienne.** "Don't be. It was a very nice glove." and "I didn't know it was a murder weapon, Archibald. I thought it was a *flower*." She's the only person whose deadpan has a secret under it.
- **Calloway.** "Find a different occupation." / "I would." She's magnetic because she's the one person Archie can't out-talk, and because he hurts her with the sticker and has to say sorry.
- **Hollis.** "You look like something the cat wouldn't." Funny, and a bit too warm, which makes me suspicious of her.

A caution on all of these: **the comic voices are too similar.** June, Lenny, Bev, Calloway, Hollis and Vivienne all do flat, dry comebacks of the same length. If you cover the names, I can't always tell who's talking.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

**Spend time with him.** In Chapter 1 I wanted to *be* him. The running commentary in the dark ("Right big toe… She's wearing ballet flats. I checked.") is a real pleasure. It's a man having the time of his life at the thing he's best at. The breaker-trip when Shannon says "Tommy?" is the best moment in his arc.

After that, I only wanted to sit near him. He's never really wrong and never really hurt. Everyone tells him he's wrong, but the book never makes it cost him anything. His theories are bad ("I made that up"), yet his big calls are right, including aconite, Ivy, and the funnel. His mistakes are charming and his wins are real. That's comfortable, and it makes him less interesting than the sticker moment, where he's cruel without meaning to and says "Sorry… Occupational hazard." That's the Archie I'd follow. I'd also like to see him fail at something that isn't a joke.

## 5. What happened, and who I suspect

My read is that the poison is on the narrow end of the trumpet, put there after his 7:36 raspberry. He says it's clean at 7:36, and Lorelei's symptoms start only once she speaks through it. I'm not fully buying "it's on the mouthpiece, not the tea." Aconite can take a while to work when swallowed, and forty minutes after the chamomile fits the tea just as well. That's why Calloway wants the cups. I think the thermos is the book's red herring, and a clumsy one.

Ranked:

1. **Ivy Crane.** She lied on the record ("She held my hand. The whole time"), wiped the narrow end with her scarf, pocketed it, and kept her coat buttoned to the throat in a hospital. She's also the one who "doesn't drink anything I don't pour," so she had the tea. She's *too* obvious. The wipe could cover for a device or a trick, not poison, but she's clearly hiding something. I don't think she's innocent. I suspect she's either the killer or someone protecting a bigger secret.
2. **Hollis Tibbetts.** The warm helper is a suspect in this genre. She holds the keys to the building and runs the lights, and her keys "jangled up there like a ring of bells." She personally brought the TV people in ("I'm the one who wrote to those TV people in May"). And she told Archie not to cancel: "Beat her. In Gus's house." She knew Gus. Someone fed Lorelei Gus's voice, and she's a plausible source.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** He has the "medical event" panic, the policy, and the "Static, static, love it" line. The retirement rumor ("She did… Fan page said so") hands him a motive. But "Oh, *Lori*" and "looked exactly his age" make his grief feel real. I think he's the decoy.
4. **Warren Gilley.** If Archie is charged, the sale might fall through, and the clause has "your name in it." That's a motive, but he's a "Shh" character and I don't feel him.
5. **Vivienne**, not as the killer but as the person who *knew*. "Don't you dare… Not yet" is said *before* Gus's voice appears. How did she know it was coming? And she cut the monkshood that morning. I think she knows who Sparrow is, and that's the thread. If the book makes her the murderer, I'll feel cheated. If it makes her the keeper of the real secret, I'll be satisfied.

**Wildcard: Lenny.** He built every illusion Gus used, he lives in the carriage house, he has access to the garden, and he "refused to come to the séance on the grounds that it was 'a waste of a perfectly good Monday.'" Staying home from the event is a tell.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, but because of a handful of threads, not because I'm gripped. What I want:

- **The 9 a.m. interview with Brannock and Okafor.** The book has promised a collision between Archie's charm and someone immune to it. Don't skip it.
- **Ivy actually talking.** She has about forty words so far. I want her to push back on Archie, lie to him, or surprise him.
- **The scarf, handled.** Either Calloway has it or Archie finds out why she doesn't.
- **A clue that isn't a recap.** The funnel and the monkshood are the only new physical facts, and both were delivered by Archie in his own kitchen.
- **Stakes beyond a stern word.** Brannock, the clause, the fifteenth. Right now the fear of Archie going to jail is told, not shown.
- **Fewer people telling Archie to stop.** June, Calloway, Hollis, Bev and Vivienne have each now said some version of it.

## 7. What I had to reread, couldn't picture, or didn't believe

- **The hand logic in Chapter 1.** A circle of seven, Ivy's hand "let go," and then four effects credited to Ivy's lap: the bell on a rod, the damp glove on the same rod, the remote under her right hand, plus her other hand presumably holding someone in the chain. I tried to place the seating (Kev on Archie's left, Vivienne on his right, Warren two seats right, Lorelei across, Ivy at her left, Shannon at her right) and I couldn't make it add up. Archie says the daughter's hands are doing a lot of work, but a reader can't check it.
- **The float.** The trumpet is described as rising and "swinging slow arcs above the red lamp" in the middle of the table. Archie then says Lorelei is holding it with her lips at the narrow end. Her mouth is at the edge of the table, and the lamp is at the center. I couldn't picture a trumpet reaching both. And in pitch dark, how does Archie *know* her left hand has it? He asserts it as an inference, and the book treats his assertion as proof.
- **"Sixteen years ago he had spent a month learning how the Borgias killed people."** The Borgias are associated with arsenic, not aconite. A reader who knows that will pause. Separately, a numb mouth and tingling face fit several poisons, so his instant certainty ("Aconitine. Monkshood.") reads as the author rewarding him, not as deduction.
- **The monkshood in the kitchen.** He studied poisons, he was shouting "aconite" at the chief, and then he doesn't notice a vase of hooded dark-blue flowers on his own table for hours. The text half-acknowledges this ("where he'd walked past it a dozen times"), but I still didn't believe he'd miss it, and it's the most convenient coincidence in the four chapters.
- **Calloway on the phone with Archie.** He "lifted" her card from the charge nurse, texted her photos of evidence at 12:40 a.m., and she answered in under a minute. I can accept that she's amused, but I didn't believe a State Police sergeant would reply to a person of interest that way. That is, until the sticker scene explained she's partly tracking him.

I'm in, but not at full commitment. Chapter 1 sold me a book that Chapters 2–4 haven't quite delivered yet.
