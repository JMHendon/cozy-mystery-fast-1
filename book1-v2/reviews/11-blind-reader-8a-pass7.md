# Reader's report

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I wasn't tempted in Chapter 1. It's the best thing here. I got restless at three other points.

**Late Chapter 1: the debunk loop.** The pattern is an effect, then Archie explaining it, then another effect, then another explanation. It runs on a metronome of timestamps: "At eleven minutes past…", "At fourteen minutes past…", "At seventeen minutes past…". By the camera blackout ("It's a pocket remote. Ivy's, in her lap… Hardest-working woman in show business") I could see the machinery. Archie is right every time, without friction. The Tommy turn rescues it.

**Chapter 2, the Calloway introduction.** The prickly, competent cop arrives, he does a party trick with her watch, and then a Sherlock read of her visitor sticker. Lines like "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward" sound like lines. This is where the book felt like a book I'd read before. The "medical event… in front of a *superfan*" bit with Teddy is also stock.

**Chapter 3, the kitchen.** This is where I'd actually have put it down. Archie re-explains the trumpet, which we already saw, with a funnel ("The tea was at twenty to eight… somebody filmed it"). June then restates the case from the cop's side ("You're the suspect"). Then comes the rules negotiation ("No cameras." "No show." "Vegas. 2019." "That was a *cloak.*"), which is sitcom cadence. For about four pages nothing new is learned except the monkshood. After the vase, the mother gives "Go to bed, Archibald" and then "Go to bed, darling" again on the stairs. The second time it stopped feeling like mystery and started feeling like withholding.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't arrive

- **The footage.** Everyone invokes it: "somebody filmed it" and "Lieutenant Brannock watched the footage at two this morning. He'd like to meet you." Nobody on the page watches it. I kept waiting for the scene.
- **Vivienne's "Not yet."** In Chapter 1 she grips his hand and says "Don't you dare… Not yet," *before* Gus's voice speaks. The text says, "He didn't know what she meant until the trumpet swung toward them." That implies she knew the message was coming. Archie, the noticer, never asks her about it. He asks "Who's Sparrow?" three times and never "How did you know to stop me?" That's a hole in his intelligence.
- **The check and the trunk.** Both are set up and neither pays off. The check was promised "in the morning." The trunk is guarded, and then the chapter ends.
- **The interview.** Chapter 4 sets up the nine o'clock meeting with Brannock and stops short of it.

The one place expectation was met immediately was the Warren outburst. Alden walks out and the consequence lands within a page. More of that.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Funniest: Archie, in Chapter 1 only.** "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" is the best joke in the four chapters. It's also a demonstration of skill, and it gets the whole circle laughing. "Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down" is nearly as good.

**Most magnetic: Lorelei Crane**, and that's a structural problem. She's dead by the end of Chapter 2. I get her through a laugh ("low and surprised and real"), "Not the spirits," and the line "I haven't finished beating you," after which "the corner of her smeared red mouth moved." That's more life than anyone else has. I wanted a whole book of those two competing.

**Close behind:**
- **Bev:** "Everything that comes back gets washed." And, about the pie, "You did. As a protest."
- **Lenny:** "It's a funnel… Is not complicated." And "It's both. Don't touch it."
- **Vivienne:** "It is a *centerpiece*."
- **June:** "Don't say gift."

A criticism: nearly everyone has Archie's snap. Margaret ("I'm going to have you killed"), Teddy, Pinkham and Bev all deliver quippy one-liners in similar rhythms. Even Archie's "Only emotionally," to a doctor checking his mouth for poison an hour after he watched someone die, reads as a reflex rather than a character choice. Maybe that's intended. I couldn't tell.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

**Spend time with him, in short doses.** I wouldn't want his life. He performs constantly, even on a yellow line outside a resuscitation. The narration also keeps telling me he's lovable ("He adored her," "He liked her immediately") rather than letting me find out.

I liked him most where he failed. One moment is "Amos, you're breaking up. Try standing by the window," said while a woman was dying and he thought she was acting. The other is the Galley, where his Warren theory costs him a town. Everything else he's been right about: rod, glove, remote, scarf, aconite. A detective who is never wrong is more admirable than interesting.

The June scenes are where he's warmest. "You're doing the hands" is the best detail in the book, a tell I can picture. That's the part of him I'd actually want to be around.

## 5. What happened, and who

**My read of the mechanics.** Archie says the poison was on the mouthpiece. He blew through it at 7:36 and was fine, so it was applied afterward. The only person he knows had a free hand is Ivy.

I distrust that tidy story. Archie's "ten minutes" of talking through the trumpet is shaky. The tea at 7:40, with onset around 8:20, fits aconite at least as well. The book also keeps lighting the thermos: Ivy's "She doesn't drink anything I don't pour," Bev washing it, Calloway taking it in a paper bag. Archie poured Lorelei's cup himself, on camera, which is exactly how you'd frame him. The monkshood on his mother's table points the same way.

**Ranking:**

1. **Ivy Crane.** She lied ("She held my hand. The whole time"). She wiped the narrow end with her scarf, hid it, and kept the coat on. She didn't cry. She has the most evidence against her, which is why I give her only about a third of the blame. I suspect she's covering for something, either poison or her mother's fakery. Archie says himself that a medium's assistant can wipe a trumpet "that has nothing to do with poison."
2. **Warren Gilley.** The whisper that turned him "translucent" is the one concrete unexplained event, and there's a clause in the sale. The book punished Archie for saying it aloud, which in this genre often means "right direction, wrong detail." I can't weigh it fairly because the whisper is only reported and never dramatized, and Chapter 1's narration, which logs everything to the minute, never shows it.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** He was in the wing as the cameras died ("Static, static, love it, keep going"). He was nervous about the "medical event." Someone from the production called Shannon in April. His motive is unclear, and his "Oh, *Lori*" seemed real.
4. **Lenny.** He refused to attend ("a waste of a perfectly good Monday"), built every illusion Archie performs, and left the kitchen without a word. This is a long shot, but he's the type the book hides in plain sight.
5. **Kev.** Superfans are suspect by genre, and he had a logo and a viral clip ready within hours. I don't believe it, though.
6. **Shannon and Bev.** They are the least likely.

**Not suspects, but people hiding something:** Vivienne, who knew what "Not yet" meant and cut the monkshood that afternoon. Also Calloway's Sea View sticker, which reads as a planted thread. I'd bet it connects to whoever fed Lorelei the "Sparrow" material.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, mostly for "Sparrow." The murder is a puzzle, but Lorelei somehow knowing something from inside Gus's family is the hook. What I want:

- **The footage, on the page.** Show what Brannock sees, and whether it clears or damns Archie.
- **A scene with Ivy that isn't filtered through Archie's deduction.** After two chapters she's a cipher. Let her say something surprising.
- **Lorelei again,** even in a TV clip. Give me her voice.
- **Calloway ahead of Archie once.** Right now she mostly reacts to him.
- **Stop withholding.** Someone should answer a question, or Archie should finally ask Vivienne about "Not yet."
- **Fewer recaps.** Chapter 3 re-explained what Chapter 1 showed.

## 7. Rereads, things I couldn't picture, things I didn't believe

- **The seating.** I reread the table paragraph twice and still couldn't build the circle. It's seven people, "close enough to bump knees," with Kev at Archie's left and Warren two seats to his right "past Vivienne." The whole deduction depends on who is holding whose hand.
- **The glove on Warren's neck.** A rod from Ivy's lap is supposed to brush "the back of Warren's neck" and then Vivienne's cheek, at a small table with someone between. I didn't believe it. I also couldn't see how Ivy runs a six-foot rod, a bell, a glove and a remote with one hand while the other holds Kev's.
- **Everyone playing along with the live commentary.** Lorelei laughs, and Kev stays rapt, while Archie narrates every trick aloud. It's charming, but it strains.
- **Tommy's and Gus's voices.** The text says "the voice was not Lorelei's." Is that a recording, an impression, or something else? Archie says he knows how Tommy was done but not Sparrow. A real Gus recording would explain why Vivienne reacted.
- **Small snags.** "He'd bought the whole building from the town in September, and on November fifteenth it would be his" made me reread. Hollis is called "Hollis" and then "her," and I'd assumed a man. "His notebook of failures" appears once with no explanation. Maine is mostly furniture so far: liars' table, "from away," bell buoy, lighthouse watercolors.
- **The promise to June.** It lasts about seven hours. Breaking it is funny. I just can't tell whether the book knows it's funny.
