# A cold read of Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never quit, but I slowed down in three places.

**The middle of Chapter 3 (June's terms of surrender).** After the funnel, the monkshood and the Sparrow beat, the book stops to negotiate house rules. "No cameras." "You don't do a press conference in a cape." "Vegas. 2019." "That was a *cloak.*" The cloak joke is fine, but it is the second time in the chapter that June tells Archie what he's doing before he does it. We already got that with "Don't say gift" and "You're doing the hands." The rules also change nothing. He agrees to everything and then goes to the turret to do exactly the thing she didn't ask about.

**Archie's "Hear me out, though."** He uses it on his mother's kitchen table about Warren ("Hear me out, though. Warren Gilley. Twenty minutes before...") and again at the Galley about Teddy ("Hear me out, though. The producer. Teddy."). Both times the book deflates the theory, first with "Warren goes that color at select board meetings," then with "I made that up *thirty seconds ago*." That's a good gag once. The second time it felt like the book repeating itself, and the Galley scene is where I most wanted a page-turn. Chapter 4 is the slackest of the four. The sequence of Bev's rules, the liars' table, Shannon's accusation, Hollis's cameo and then the thermos is a lot of town to meet while the central question sits still.

**Chapter 1's running scoreboard.** "One... Two... Three... Four... Five." It's charming at first. By "Five" it's a tic. The narration is also so busy explaining each trick that the séance stops being scary until Shannon's husband speaks. That moment ("*Shan… it's warm here*") is where it finally got me.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **Sparrow.** This is the hook, and I expected at least a nibble by the end of Chapter 4. All we have is Vivienne's face "did something he had never seen it do in forty-seven years, and then it stopped doing it" and "Go to bed, Archibald." Four chapters in, I'm now waiting on the book, not Vivienne.
- **The check.** Archie sets a 9 a.m. reminder to pay a million dollars. In Chapter 4 the reminder is just gone, and 9:00 is the interview. I expected either the payment or a reason it didn't happen.
- **The sale clause.** "Something about a clause." "The kind with your name in it." The November 15 closing is a ticking clock that Chapter 1 loaded and that nobody has fired yet.
- **The SEA VIEW sticker.** Calloway was visiting someone at an obvious care home, and Archie's read of her ("Don't.") was the best character beat for her. I expected it to be picked up again. It wasn't.
- **The ink vial in the trunk.** It was introduced as stakes and then immediately defused by Archie typing S-P-A and stopping. I wanted more cost to it.

## 3. The funniest, most magnetic person

**Lorelei Crane**, and she dies by the end of Chapter 2. "Not the spirits, honey" is the best line in the book. Her reaction to Archie's heckling ("a low laugh, surprised and pleased") makes the first chapter a duet. Once she's gone, the book has lost its best scene partner and doesn't replace her.

Among the living, the order is:

1. **Bev.** "Everything that comes back gets washed." "There's no third pie." "You did. As a protest." She's the funniest person who isn't trying. Her deadpan is also load-bearing for the plot, which is rare.
2. **Calloway.** "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward." / "I would." And "Don't be." She's drier than Archie, and she's the only person in four chapters who isn't straight-lining for him. She is closest to magnetic, because I want to know what's behind the sticker.
3. **Vivienne.** "It is a *centerpiece*." And her grip turning to iron in the dark.
4. **Lenny.** "It's a funnel. Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated."
5. **June.** "That's three words, and one of them is a contraction."

Archie himself is funny. The best joke in the book is his, "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on,*" but he is mostly the engine for other people's lines.

## 4. Did I want to *be* him?

For the first ten minutes of Chapter 1, yes. The joy is real: "having the time of his life," the pleasure at the float ("Lorelei, that's the best float I've ever seen"), a man loving someone else's craft while beating her at it. That's an attractive trait, and it's the reason I'd follow him.

After that I mostly wanted to spend time with him, for two reasons:

- **He's right about everything.** Rod, glove, remote, aconite, the sticker on Calloway's coat, the hospital sequence. The one thing he can't do is explain the voice, and the book keeps him on a pedestal for the rest. When the narration says "She knew he wouldn't" (Vivienne) or "Absolutely not" (June), everyone around him is a reaction shot. A reader doesn't envy someone who never pays for his mistakes.
- **The patter reads as armor, which the book knows.** "Only emotionally." The quarter walking over his knuckles when he's about to lie. His best moments are when the patter fails: "I liked her," and "That's her husband... Enjoy your eggs," which immediately gets shut down by Shannon ("I don't need you to do that"). I wanted *more* of him being wrong at the Galley.

## 5. What I think happened, and who I suspect

**What happened.** Someone poisoned Lorelei with aconite, probably in the tea or on the narrow end of the trumpet, and the "Gus" voice either was part of a setup to make the séance memorable or was the thing that went wrong. There is a real hole in Archie's reasoning here. He argues the mouthpiece because "the tea was at twenty to eight" and she was "fine for forty minutes." Forty minutes is entirely consistent with ingestion, and he drank from the same thermos, only different cups. I don't believe his trumpet theory yet, and the book shows signs of doubting it too: Calloway grabs the thermos, not the trumpet.

**My ranking:**

1. **Hollis Tibbetts.** She's the one who told him to hold the séance ("Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house."). She runs the lights, alone in the booth, and the cameras went dark while she was up there. She's an English teacher who knew Gus and probably knew the name Sparrow. And she's the first person who knocked on his door with a pie and the keys. She's also *too* warm and too helpful, and she exits Chapter 4 in a gust of confident exposition. The warm local who handed him the building is my pick, partly on evidence and partly because the book is a mystery.
2. **Ivy Crane.** She wiped the narrow end and hid the scarf, and she said "She held my hand. The whole time," which was false. She is the obvious suspect, and for that reason I suspect it's a *different* crime. Maybe she was cleaning up a stunt, or she knew something was in the trumpet and was protecting her mother's act. She is doing something, but I don't think it's murder.
3. **Vivienne.** She knew who Sparrow was. She said "Not yet" to stop him from exposing the séance. She cut the monkshood that afternoon and put it on the table. I don't think she poisoned anyone, but she is the person I most believe is hiding a story. The monkshood centerpiece is either an alibi for her or someone's frame.
4. **Teddy Marchand.** There's an insurance policy and a retirement announcement, which Archie invented and Pinkham confirmed from a fan page. His grief ("Oh, *Lori*") seemed real, and "A *medical event*" reads like a producer, not a killer. Still, he sat out of reach in the wing during the "static."
5. **Warren Gilley.** The whisper that turned him "the color of skim milk." The book has told me twice it means nothing, which is why I haven't dropped him.
6. **Bev.** The thermos was washed. The book draws attention to it three times, which makes me think it's a red herring. She'd be a heartbreaking answer, and I doubt it.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 right now?

**Yes**, mainly for Calloway, Margaret Okafor and Lieutenant Brannock in the select board room. What I want:

- **A conversation about Sparrow.** Either Vivienne says something concrete, or Archie finds out she's been lying to him for years.
- **The lab result, and a hole in Archie's theory.** I want the tea, the cups and the trumpet to come back messier than he predicted.
- **Ivy and the coat.** What was on the scarf? What did she say when pushed? Even a half-answer would help.
- **The trunk.** One real clue, not another failed combination.
- **Less patter.** He's at his best when someone makes him stop.

## 7. What I reread, couldn't picture, or didn't believe

- **The seating.** Chapter 1 says "seven of them round a small oak table." I had to count: Lorelei, Ivy on her left, Shannon on her right, Archie across, Kev on his left, Vivienne on his right, Warren past her. That works, but I couldn't hold the circle in my head, and when the lights come up it says "six people at a round oak table." It reads like a miscount, but I think it's Archie on his feet. A one-line diagram in the prose would help.
- **Ivy's free hand.** Archie says Ivy is running the bell, the wet glove, the camera remote and the rod with "one free hand, one lap." A single hand holds the rod, wets the glove and triggers a pocket remote, all at once? I didn't believe it.
- **"Ray." / "Tess."** The guard is Ray, and she says it first. It took me a moment to realize Calloway was addressing him, not Archie.
- **"That was how the detective first saw him."** A stray omniscient shift in a book that's otherwise in Archie's head.
- **"Wolfsbane. For the name."** I couldn't parse what she meant. Is it the plant's name, Gus's joke, or the Fairweather name?
- **The million-dollar check.** The challenge says if "anything happened in this room tonight that Archie couldn't explain," he'd pay. Then he decides to pay the next morning, before a lawyer is in the room and while he's an obvious suspect. Margaret arrives at 6:30 as if none of that was discussed. That felt like a character gesture the plot hadn't earned.
- **The ER.** Archie walks through the swinging doors, is escorted out, and within an hour has a state detective ordering a targeted screen on his say-so. It's fast, but I'd forgive it because he's the only person who got it right. It does feed the problem in question 4.
- **The "Gus" voice.** Archie performs old-lady voice throwing himself in Chapter 1. When Gus's voice speaks, I wasn't clear why that's the unexplained part. The *content* ("Sparrow," "the trunk") is the mystery, not the voice. Chapter 3 says "I couldn't explain the voice. The Gus one," and it should say "the knowledge."
