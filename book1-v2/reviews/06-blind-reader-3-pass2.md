# A cold read of Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

Never in Chapter 1, which is the best thing here. I started to drift in three places.

**The kitchen in Chapter 3, from the funnel to "Dad. You're the suspect."** I had just watched the trick done. Archie then re-performs it for Lenny, and the only fresh beat is Lenny's "It's a funnel. Sound goes in the small end… Is not complicated." June's "You're the suspect" is the book explaining its stakes to me.

**The third telling that Archie enjoys the corpse.**
- Chapter 2: "It's a gift. You'll want to put a bow on it."
- Chapter 3, June: "Don't say gift."
- Chapter 3, Archie: "I was going to say *puzzle*."

It's a good joke once. By the third time the book is managing me.

**Bev and the thermos in Chapter 4.** The moment Bev says "Everything that comes back gets washed," I knew the scene's punchline. Then it took two more pages to arrive: "The Galley went completely silent." Archie also has to be careless at exactly the moment the plot needs it. He is told "Don't wash it," says "I have never washed anything in my life," and hands it to the one woman in Maine who washes everything. A mind that notices ring clicks would not do that.

A smaller sag is Teddy: "A *medical event*, Jen. We do not say that word." He's a sitcom producer I've already met.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't come

- **The million-dollar check.** Chapter 1 makes it the stakes: "If anything happened in this room tonight that Archie couldn't explain… a million-dollar check." Something happened that he can't explain. Teddy and Kev would raise it within the hour, and nobody mentions it in three chapters.
- **SPARROW on the lock.** "S. Then P. Then A. He stopped." He has knelt there "every night since August" with a "notebook of failures," and he refuses the best lead in thirty-seven years on a hunch. His reasoning is clever ("hand the mark a word that fits his lock"). I still felt the author withholding the payoff.
- **The sale clause.** Pinkham says "Something about a clause… The kind with your name in it." We never learn what it says.
- **Warren's whisper.** Archie's best lead is dismissed in one line: "Warren goes that color at select board meetings."
- **Ivy's coat.** The judge is "asked for" and nothing follows.

## 3. The funniest, most magnetic person

**Lorelei Crane, easily.** Examples:
- "Amos doesn't have hands, honey."
- "Honey, are you going to talk through my whole séance?"
- "Not the spirits, honey."

She out-banters the hero and she's dead by 9:40 p.m. Chapters 2–4 feel like a party after the best guest left.

Close behind:
- **Bev:** "There's no third pie." and "You did. As a protest."
- **Vivienne:** "It was a very nice glove." and "It is a *centerpiece*."
- **June:** "Vegas. 2019." / "That was a *cloak.*"
- **Calloway:** the most magnetic live character, because she says so little. "I would." is her whole answer to the handshake joke.

## 4. Be him, or just spend time with him?

In Chapter 1 I wanted to be him: the count ("Four."), the joy, the room. After Lorelei dies I only wanted his company, for three reasons.

**He is right about everything checkable.** The toe, the ring, the rod, the remote, the scarf, and the aconite all check out. Dr. Rao even confirms it: "It was the rhythm you said it would be." His only wrong ideas are the ones he invents as jokes. Nothing he does costs him.

**His flaw is stated, not shown.** June tells me he "does the hands." Shannon tells me he took her husband away. Both are good scenes, but the narration lets him off both. "She was dying" settles the Shannon argument, and "You passed" settles the June one.

**The narrator keeps telling me he's charming:**
- "with real joy"
- "delighted"
- "enchanted"

I'd rather see it land on someone else.

The best moment for him is "That's her husband. Enjoy your eggs," and it's a little too clean.

## 5. What happened, and who I suspect

My theory is that the aconite was on the narrow end of the trumpet. It went on after Archie's clean blow at 7:36 and before Lorelei blew at about 8:15. Archie thinks the voice "smeared" because she was staging a fade-out. I think that was the poison starting: "The voice thickened as if the old man's mouth were full of cotton." The cut-off line, "The answer's in the—", suggests someone wanted her stopped before she finished it. If that's right, the killer is tied to Gus and the trunk, not to TV.

Two odd details:
- **Ivy's coat.** I think it holds the rig (rod, glove, remote), not poison.
- **The lipstick.** It's mentioned twice, "dark-red," and I distrust it.

My ranking:

1. **Ivy.** She lied on the record ("She held my hand. The whole time."), wiped the trumpet, and kept the scarf and coat. I'm giving her only moderate odds, though. I suspect she's hiding the fraud, not the murder. Archie says as much himself: "reasons a medium's assistant wipes a trumpet that have nothing to do with poison."
2. **Warren Gilley.** He went pale after Lorelei's whisper, and the sale and its clause touch him. But his hands were held the whole time, so he needs help.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** Pinkham confirmed the retirement ("She did… Fan page"), which turned Archie's invented theory into fact. Teddy is also the only one with an obvious money motive. His panic is too loud for me to believe, which usually clears a suspect in this kind of book.
4. **Hollis.** She pushed him to run the event ("Beat her. In Gus's house"), has keys to the building, and runs the lights. She's the helpful mentor with pie.
5. **Lenny.** He skipped the séance, built every Gus illusion, knew the monkshood on sight ("It's both"), and walks out silently. I put him last, but only because I can't see his move yet.

Vivienne isn't a suspect. But she knows who Sparrow is, and she is the person I most want to catch lying.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 right now?

Yes. Sparrow and Calloway pull me forward more than the murder mechanics do.

What I want:
- **The 9:00 interview.** It needs friction, an actual Brannock instead of a name, and Okafor doing more than scowling.
- **Vivienne forced to answer.** I'd take her lying and Archie catching it.
- **Archie wrong in a way that costs.** He accuses someone aloud and has to eat it.
- **Calloway bad at something.** So far she is unflappable.
- **What the sale clause says.**

## 7. Rereads, pictures I couldn't form, things I didn't believe

- **"He had never wanted anyone to collect so badly."** I reread it. It reads as the opposite of what's meant.
- **The sale.** "He owned this one. Very nearly: the sale closed on November fifteenth." Who is selling to whom? Hollis's "Don't cancel her" then implies the town wanted the séance canceled. I couldn't build that backstory.
- **The seating.** The plot runs on who could reach whom and whose hand was free. Warren is "two seats to Archie's right," Vivienne sits between them, and the rod comes "from Ivy's lap" to touch Warren's neck and Vivienne's cheek. I tried to draw the table and couldn't. A one-line cue would fix it.
- **The sticker deduction.** "You grew up in Merrow" comes from the chief using her first name on the radio. That is thin for the confidence Archie shows.
- **The voices.** Lorelei does a young boatman and then an old Maine showman, convincingly, through a funnel-like cone. I'll accept it as a skill, but it's a lot to ask.
- **Dialect.** Pinkham's "Ayuh. Lord. Ayuh." is stage Maine. Bev and Hollis sound like real people, so he stands out.
- **Voice sameness.** Strip the tags and I couldn't tell June from Calloway from Bev. They all use the clipped deadpan exchange: "That's three words." / "Complete, though." It's attractive and starting to flatten.
- **The "Don't" refrain.** Women tell Archie "Don't" (June, Calloway ×3) and he does it anyway. By Calloway's "Don't be," I could see the template.
- **Name load.** About ten named people in the first few pages.
