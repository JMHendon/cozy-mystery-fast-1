# A cold read of Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

**The five-debunk run in Chapter 1.** I'd say the middle stretch is the weakest of the first chapter. Archie solves each effect aloud as it happens: "Telescoping rod. Six feet of it, from Ivy's lap. Two. You owe me ten dollars." Then "A damp glove on the same rod," then "It's a pocket remote," then "Five." By the third tally I was watching a man explain a show, not watching a show. It also drains the séance of any uncanny feeling, so I never felt what Kev felt.

**The kitchen in Chapter 3, from "Hear me out, though. Warren Gilley…" to the vase.** It's a long run of family banter three hours after a death. The structure is: Archie floats a theory, a local deflates it ("Warren goes that color at select board meetings"). Chapter 4 does the same thing with Teddy ("Hear me out, though. The producer…"), and Pinkham confirms the guess: "She did… I follow the fan page." "Hear me out, though" is now a tic. The loop was charming once and is a pattern by the second time. A third time and I'm gone.

**Hollis's entrance in Chapter 4.** She comes in, says "Sit up straight. You look like a boy who's lost his homework," carries off a pink pie box and leaves. She delivers one useful line, "Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house." The rest is texture. The scene has a name-dump problem, and so does the book. By the end of Chapter 4 I'd met about 18 named people (Jen, Mattie, Liam, Rosie, Ray, Brannock, Okafor…) and was tracking them, not enjoying them.

**The closer.** "Plenty of time to solve a murder" is a quip-button, not a hook. It let the air out of a chapter that ended well on Bev handing over the thermos.

## 2. What I expected that hadn't happened

- **The footage.** Archie says "there were cameras on that table from half past seven… somebody filmed it." Calloway watched it at 2 a.m. Four chapters in, nobody has described what's on it. The withholding feels coy, not suspenseful.
- **The million-dollar check.** Chapter 1 sets up "Secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would [collect]." That is the best emotional premise in the book. Chapter 3 gives it one exchange with June, and Chapter 4 mentions it in a phone call. It hasn't cost him anything.
- **Any investigating.** Four chapters in, Archie has theorized in a kitchen and a diner. His only real act was the aconite call, and it was right. I expected him to be wrong about something by now.
- **A response to "Sparrow."** Vivienne says "Go to bed, Archibald" and then "Go to bed, darling." The same refusal twice in one chapter reads as stalling.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Funniest: Bev.** "The word is eat your eggs." "There's no third pie." "Everything that comes back gets washed." Her lines are short, dry and in character.

**Close behind:**
- Vivienne: "It is a *centerpiece*."
- Lenny: "It's a funnel… Is not complicated," and "It's both. Don't touch it."
- Calloway: "I was nine."

**Most magnetic: Calloway.** The watch beat works because she doesn't perform. He holds up her watch, she takes it back without a word, and "for the first time something in the gray eyes was paying attention." The peeled SEA VIEW sticker, "Don't," and the unexplained anger carry more intrigue than anything Archie says.

Archie is funniest in Chapter 1 ("*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*"). After that his patter is on repeat.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Be him for seventeen minutes in Chapter 1 and then no. Spend time with him in small doses.

In Chapter 1 he loves the craft. "Oh, that's *gorgeous*… that's the best float I've ever seen" is the warmest moment in the book. After that he is never wrong, never loses anything and never pays for being clever.

His worst moment is also the book's most honest one. In the ER he tells a stranger "You grew up in Merrow. You were visiting someone tonight," and she walks away. It shows he treats people as puzzles. June names it ("Don't say gift"), but the book keeps rewarding the behavior. He lifts her watch, then lifts her card, and both times it's presented as charming.

I'd rather be June or Calloway. They're the ones who see him clearly.

## 5. What happened, and who

**My theory.** The poison was on the narrow end of the trumpet. It went on after Archie's "clean at seven thirty-six" blow. Whoever applied it had a hand that was legitimately free in the dark. The only free hands were Ivy's and Lorelei's, so the culprit is probably inside the act, not an outsider.

I'm not confident about the mouthpiece. The tea is also a candidate: forty minutes is plausible for aconite, and Calloway's pen stopped at "Different cups." Archie's "It's on the mouthpiece" is stated with more certainty than the evidence supports. June is right to be suspicious of him.

**Ranking:**

1. **Ivy.**
   - She had the free hand and the scarf.
   - She also has a handy motive: Lorelei was retiring.
   - But she's the person Archie flags twice, and a mystery rarely lets the sleuth fingering the culprit be right by Chapter 2.
   - She may be wiping the trumpet because she knows what's on it, and who put it there.
2. **Teddy.**
   - He is a producer with, presumably, a policy on her.
   - He was at the stage-right monitor when the cameras went dark at 8:14 ("Static, static, love it, keep going"). That's suspiciously fast for a man who didn't expect it.
   - Against him: his panic about the word "poison" reads as a comic-relief red herring.
3. **Warren.**
   - The whisper that turned him "translucent."
   - The mystery "clause" with Archie's name in it.
   - A man with a stake in the sale falling through.
4. **Hollis.** My reason is thin and it's mostly genre instinct. She pushed Archie into the séance ("Beat her. In Gus's house"), ran the lights, and is introduced with a lot of affection.

**Not suspects:** Shannon (grief is too raw), Bev (thermos washing is the book's moral anchor), and Archie. The monkshood on his table is a frame or a coincidence, and it's obvious enough that I don't take it as a clue.

**Vivienne** is the one I'm most curious about, but as the keeper of a secret, not a killer. "Don't you dare… Not yet" suggests she wanted to hear Gus.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, but from momentum, not hunger. The ER feet and "Nobody touches that trumpet" earned it.

**What I want:**
- **The footage, frame by frame.** I want a moment Archie can't explain, shown, not asserted.
- **Ivy speaking.** She's been dry-eyed and monosyllabic for two chapters, and I want to hear her.
- **Archie wrong, with a cost.** The Brannock interview and Margaret Okafor walking in would be a good place.
- **Calloway's Sea View.** She's the better mystery.
- **Vivienne telling him something.**

## 7. Rereads, pictures, doubts

**The seating and hand logistics in Chapter 1.** I had to rebuild them. Kev is on Archie's left and Vivienne on his right. Warren is "two seats to Archie's right, past Vivienne." Lorelei is across, with Ivy on her left and Shannon on her right. The glove touches both Warren's neck and Vivienne's cheek from "six feet" of rod in Ivy's lap. Ivy is the only one with a free hand for the rod, glove and remote, and I couldn't work out who was holding her other hand. A one-line seating diagram would help.

**Vivienne's trunk logic.** "Thirty-seven years, and nobody has opened it, so he isn't gone." I reread it twice. It works as grief logic and fails as logic, and the book treats it as both.

**The seven-letter lock.** SPARROW is seven letters. I had the combination from "Seven letters," so the trunk is no longer a mystery. The only open question is whether anyone will open it.

**"I made that up thirty seconds ago."** Pinkham confirms Archie's guess about Lorelei retiring. It's a good gag, but the world agreeing with his improv is a cheat in a mystery. It also hands Teddy a motive without Archie earning it.

**The ER.** Archie walks into a resuscitation and lectures a doctor, and no one stops him for long. Dr. Rao says "Got it" almost at once. I went along because the tone is farce, but that's a lot of leniency.

**June.** She's charming, but she's a knowing-child type: "I just wanted to see if you'd lie to me." I couldn't tell her age. "Debate," "school" and "the hearse" suggest 15 or 16, but the text never says. I also noticed that her mother is absent without comment.

**"Not the spirits."** Lorelei says it while dying, and "something wry" is in her eyes. It's a sentimental line for someone who can't feel her face, but it's also the best clue she gives. I'd like to know why she was so sure.

**Style tics.** "The way she'd looked at him… as if he were a statement she hadn't finished reading" appears twice, and "gray eyes" keeps coming up. The color of Lorelei's lipstick is mentioned three times.
