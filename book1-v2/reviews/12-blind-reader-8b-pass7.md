# Reader's report, Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never really wanted to quit, because the chapters are short and each ends on a hook. But I slowed down in three places.

- **The middle of Chapter 1.** Archie calls and debunks one trick after another: "Right big toe… ballet flats," "Telescoping rod. Six feet of it," "A damp glove on the same rod," "It's a pocket remote." The first one is delightful. By the fourth, I could predict the shape: effect, then Archie's whisper, then a button line ("Hardest-working woman in show business"). The scene also keeps telling me he's adored: "even Lorelei had laughed," "Archie nearly loved him." I'd rather be shown.
- **The back half of Chapter 3.** After the monkshood reveal, the kitchen turns into a rules negotiation: no cameras, no cape ("Vegas. 2019." "That was a *cloak.*"), no hearse. It's cute, but I'd already got "June is the family's conscience." The line "for an instant she was forty years old" is the author doing my work for me. "You did the hands." / "That's why you passed." is the most writerly beat in the book, and it felt like a TV-episode button.
- **The Warren theory in Chapter 4.** "Twenty minutes before the lights went out, Lorelei leaned over and whispered in his ear, and Warren went the color of skim milk." I didn't see that in Chapter 1. Warren sits two seats from Archie, Lorelei is across the table, and everyone is holding hands. A clue the reader never saw is a cheat. It also made Archie, who is careful everywhere else, look careless.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't come

- **The camera outage.** At 8:14 "the red standby lights on both cameras went dark at once." The trumpet rises three minutes later. Archie waves it off as Ivy's remote. In Chapter 3 he says "there were cameras on that table from half past seven. If somebody touched that trumpet after I did, somebody filmed it." I kept waiting for someone to say that the window between 8:14 and 8:17 is exactly when a poisoner would need cover. Nobody does.
- **The impossible crime.** Chapter 3 sells a locked-room puzzle: "I know how everything *she* did was done. I don't know how this was." But I can't see what is impossible. Ivy had a free hand, the cameras went dark, and Lorelei refused a search. I'm waiting for the part Archie can't explain.
- **The million-dollar check.** It's promised in Chapter 3 ("Her charity gets the check in the morning") and again in Chapter 4 ("You are *not*"). It's still hanging.
- **The 9 a.m. interview with Brannock and Margaret Okafor.** I expected to get there by the end of Chapter 4, and didn't.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Funniest: Archie, in Chapter 1.** "Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on." is the best line in the book. "Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down" is close behind.

**Most magnetic: Calloway, by a mile.** She says almost nothing and owns every scene.
- "Do you," with no question mark.
- "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward." / "I would."
- Her eyebrow goes up "a sixteenth of an inch."
- "Don't be."

**Close behind:**
- **Bev.** "Everything that comes back gets washed." And "You did. As a protest."
- **Vivienne.** "It is a *centerpiece*."
- **Shannon.** The best human moment in the four chapters is hers: "He talked to me… For six minutes Tommy was there. And you stood up and yelled for the lights."
- **Lorelei.** She was magnetic for ten pages, then died. Killing the one person who could trade patter with Archie costs the book something.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Spend time with him. I wanted to be him for about fifteen minutes of Chapter 1. His pleasure in the craft is lovely: "Oh, that's *gorgeous*… Lorelei, that's the best float I've ever seen." After Lorelei dies, I only wanted his company.

- **He's never wrong.** The aconite, Ivy's scarf, the Sea View sticker, and the stage tricks are all correct. His deduction about Calloway ("You grew up in Merrow") skips the reasoning. A first-name greeting from a guard doesn't tell you where someone grew up, and the book rewards him anyway. Shannon is the first person to make him look foolish, and he recovers within a page.
- **His inner life is told by others.** I learn about his wound (Gus, the trunk, "Sparrow") from his mother's face, not from him. His only stated want is "secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would" collect the million. The best line he has is "I liked her."

## 5. What I think happened, and my suspects

My working theory is that aconite got onto the narrow end of the trumpet, or into Lorelei's mouth some other way, during the 8:14–8:17 camera gap. The Cranes had researched the town: Shannon says "a girl called me in April." Lorelei's séance was meant to push the Fairweathers toward the trunk. Someone who feared the trunk made sure she never finished the sentence. The delivery is still open, though. The text keeps three routes alive: the tea, the trumpet, and the "dark-red lipstick" it mentions three or four times.

1. **Ivy Crane** has the most access (the only free hand) and she lies on the record: "She held my hand. The whole time." But the book spotlights her so hard ("Nobody else was looking at her") that I distrust it. Her buttoned coat with both hands in the pockets could be hiding the rod, glove and remote, so her behavior may be fraud-guilt, not murder-guilt. She's first on access, but I'd only give her about a third of the probability.
2. **Warren Gilley.** He has the whisper, the board chair's stake in the sale, and the skim-milk face. But Archie accuses him loudly in a diner, and in a mystery that usually means he's a red herring.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** He called her "Lori," and "Static, static, love it, keep going" is a strangely calm thing to say as the cameras die. He also benefits from a televised death. I can't place him near the trumpet, though.
4. **Vivienne** is a separate case. I don't think she's the killer, but she knows something. "Don't you dare… Not yet" comes *before* the trumpet swings toward them, as if she knew Gus's voice was coming. And she cut the monkshood that afternoon.
5. **Hollis, Kev and Shannon** are at the bottom. Hollis has no characterization yet, and Archie placed Shannon himself.

I also have a hunch about Calloway, though not that she's a suspect. "You were a lot more pleasant at nine." / "I was nine," plus the Sea View sticker, suggests her visit and her history tie into the Gus mystery.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, immediately. What I want:

- The Brannock/Okafor interview, with a scene where Archie loses a round.
- The camera footage, specifically 8:14–8:17.
- Archie and Ivy alone, with Ivy wanting something from him.
- One piece of hard evidence about delivery, such as the lab result on the tea or the lipstick.
- Vivienne pressed on "Sparrow" and not just sent to bed.
- Fewer explained tricks and one thing Archie genuinely can't explain.

## 7. Rereads, blurry pictures, doubts

- **The table.** Seven people at a "small oak table" with a teacup-sized lamp. Yet Ivy's six-foot rod reaches Warren's neck and Vivienne's cheek, the bell walks across the table, and the trumpet swings "wide arcs." I reread twice to work out who held whose hands. Seat everyone once, plainly.
- **"He'd bought the whole building from the town in September, and on November fifteenth it would be his."** Bought, but not yet his? I reread it.
- **The cups.** Pinkham says the State took "the cups" from the table. Then Bev washes "the two little nested cups" off the thermos and hands them over. They can't be in both places. Also, since Archie drank from the same thermos and is fine, the washed thermos shouldn't be a bombshell. "The Galley went completely silent" overstates it.
- **The camera logic.** See question 2. Either Archie is blind to it, which I'd like the book to acknowledge, or it's an oversight.
- **Gus and the trunk.** I had to reread "Thirty-seven years, and nobody has opened it, so he isn't gone." Is Gus dead, missing, or something else? Also, who is "Sparrow"? The voice says "Viv… Vivi" and then "Sparrow," which implies a third person.
- **The search.** A man who runs a million-dollar challenge lets the medium's assistant keep her coat and pockets unsearched. I half-believe he wanted the show to run, but it undercuts "I'm going to beat her."
- **Convenient expertise.** Archie recognizes aconite from numb lips in a dark room, and the doctor accepts it within seconds. It's plausible for this kind of book, but twice in two chapters his niche knowledge is the key.
