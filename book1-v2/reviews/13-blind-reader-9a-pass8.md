# Reader's report, Chapters 1-4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I was never close to quitting in Chapter 1. The first line and the closing "Nobody touches that trumpet" hold it up. These are the dips I felt:

- **The debunk loop in Ch. 1.** Archie explains the bell, then the glove, then the remote, each time in nearly the same shape: "Telescoping rod. Six feet of it, from Ivy's lap," then "A damp glove on the same rod," then "It's a pocket remote." After the third one the wonder had become a routine. It only recovers when the trumpet rises and he stops being able to narrate.
- **The front of Ch. 3.** I already had the case against Archie. Then the funnel demonstration and June summarize it again: "You poured her a drink on camera. You named the poison before any doctor did. You put your mouth on the trumpet." That is the book restating its own plot to me. "Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated" is a nice Lenny line, but the scene around it is a recap.
- **The Galley in Ch. 4.** It adds a lot of cozy furniture: the "liars' table," "Ayuh," "You're from away," the "walrus mustache" (used twice), "feed caps." It's pleasant but familiar. Chapter 4 also ends on a quip ("Then I'd better be right") rather than a hook. Chapters 1-3 each ended on an image or a question, so this was the weakest ending.
- **Names.** By Ch. 4 I was juggling about 20 names. Ray the guard and Dr. Rao sit two paragraphs apart. Bev and Lenny both get "reading glasses pushed up." Hollis, Bev and Vivienne blur as older women.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **The trunk.** "The answer's in the—" is the strongest hook in the book. By the end of Ch. 3 the trunk is on the page with its ink vial and seven dials. I expected someone to try it by Ch. 4. Nobody has, though it's clearly the Chekhov's gun of the book.
- **The footage.** Calloway says "Lieutenant Brannock watched the footage at two this morning." Archie said the cameras would show the answer. I expected to learn what they showed. The standby lights had gone dark at 8:14, so Archie's claim in Ch. 3 that "somebody filmed it" is already shaky.
- **The check.** Archie sets a nine a.m. reminder, and Margaret Okafor is already in the car to stop him. I expected that conflict to land.
- **Ivy speaking.** She's been in four chapters and has said "Okay," "Me. On her left," and "She held my hand. The whole time." I want her in a scene.
- **The Warren whisper.** I did not see it in Ch. 1. Archie says in Ch. 4 "I was standing right there," but Ch. 1 gave Warren only "Shh" and the parking joke. I felt held out on, as if the book had a clue it didn't show me.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Funniest per line: Vivienne.** She beats Archie.
- "Don't be. It was a very nice glove."
- "It is a *centerpiece*."
- "A woman with a tin horn doesn't get to change it."

**Funniest overall: Archie**, though his hit rate is uneven. These land:
- "It's a spirit, not an audit."
- "Only emotionally."
- "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward."

Others are filler, like "Tell Amos I'll be watching his hands. And if he hasn't got any, I'll be watching yours."

**Most magnetic: Lorelei Crane, and she's dead by the end of Ch. 1.** Her pull comes mostly from Archie's reactions ("low and surprised and real"), because she has few lines. "Not the spirits," with "something wry" under the fear, is the best thing she does. I wanted the duel and didn't get it. That is effective for grief and a bit unfair to the reader.

**Close behind:**
- **Calloway.** Her economy of words works: "Don't touch it." / "Don't be." But she is a familiar guarded cop. The sticker moment is what gives her depth.
- **Bev.** "As a protest" and "Washed it" are the best comic beats in Ch. 4.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Spend time with him, and only some of the time.

In Ch. 1 I did want to be him. He is delighted by the people trying to fool him, and the first line sells that. Once he becomes the detective, he gets less lovable. He wins nearly every beat and talks in a fixed rhythm.

What humanizes him:
- He is wrong about the sticker: "he'd got it wrong, and wrong somewhere that hurt."
- "I liked her."
- He is grateful for June's "Are you okay?" and "couldn't answer it."
- Shannon rebuffs him: "I don't need you to do that."

What diminishes him:
- He accuses the select board chairman of murder in a diner, two stools from the chief of police, minutes before he phones his lawyer. The text excuses this with "because the coffee was hot and the room was full and he had not slept." That is a flaw I'd believe from a lesser man.

## 5. What happened, and who I suspect

My theory is that the trumpet is the decoy. Archie blew into the mouthpiece at 7:36 and is fine. Aconite taken by mouth can act in under an hour, which fits tea at 7:40 and collapse at about 8:20. The line "She doesn't drink anything I don't pour" is shouted at me, and then Lorelei drinks Archie's pour anyway. The cups came from Bev's thermos, and the one place the evidence would die is Bev's sink. "Everything that comes back gets washed" is too neat. I suspect the tea and the cup, and the scarf-wipe is misdirection.

Ranking:
1. **Ivy.** She had the free hand, the remote, the trumpet, and the coat she won't take off. She also lied: "She held my hand. The whole time." I only half believe it, because Archie hands her to me within one chapter. The wipe could be fraud cover, hiding a voice device, or a routine habit. She's either the culprit or protecting her mother's secret.
2. **Warren Gilley.** He has the whisper, the sale clause, and a cousin who can be sent in four minutes. He knows something about Gus or the building.
3. **Vivienne.** I don't think she's the poisoner, but she knows who Sparrow is. "Don't you dare. Not yet" means she knew the voice was coming. Her monkshood is on the table, probably as a frame or a red herring.
4. **Lenny.** He's the only one who skipped the séance ("a waste of a perfectly good Monday"), and he knows every mechanism. He walks out silently when the million comes up. I like him too much, which in this genre is a warning.
5. **Teddy Marchand.** The cameras die and a voice from the wing says "Static, static, love it, keep going." He calls her "Lori." But his panic reads as brand management.
6. **Bev and Shannon.** Both had access to the thermos. Shannon seems too wrecked, and Bev seems like Calloway's red herring.

There is also the unseen "girl who called me in April." That is premeditation by someone inside Lorelei's operation, probably with a voice source I'd bet is cloned or archival. If so, the question of how they knew "Sparrow" matters more than the poison.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, immediately. The reasons are Sparrow, the trunk, and Vivienne's face. Here is what I specifically want:
- The 9 a.m. interview with Brannock, Margaret and Calloway, and what the footage shows.
- Archie wrong in a way that costs something. He has been wrong once, and it cost a feeling, not a case.
- Ivy talking.
- Fewer Archie explanations and more things happening to him.
- A reason Calloway's sticker matters. The memory-care mother is planted too carefully not to. My wild guess is that she knew Gus or is Sparrow.

## 7. Reread, couldn't picture, didn't believe

**Reread**
- The Ch. 1 seating. I drew it: Archie, Vivienne, Warren, Ivy, Lorelei, Shannon, Kev. I had to, to follow who held whose hand and the "one free hand" claim.
- "He'd bought the building... on November fifteenth it would be his." Bought or not yet?
- Ch. 3 says she "talks through *this* for ten minutes." On the page it's about three.
- A stray stutter in dialogue: `"...I don't know how this was." "And she said Sparrow..."`

**Couldn't picture**
- How a trumpet floating in Lorelei's left hand produces a young man's voice while she also holds the circle.
- The luminous trumpet and the bell "walking" at the same time.

**Didn't believe**
- The ER. A guard drags in a loud magician, and a doctor says "Wait. Say that again" and immediately flags it.
- The trunk. In 37 years nobody has tried it, a professional magician has a "notebook of failures," and Lenny could drill it in four minutes. The ink-vial threat feels like a premise rule rather than a character reason.
- June as "forty years old" with a debate-style negotiation. "That's why you passed" is charming but a little too cleanly wise.
- The million. Archie is mid-purchase on a theater and is writing a million-dollar check at breakfast with no hesitation. "Rules are rules" is a nice line, but I want to see the stakes.
- Calloway's text replies at 12:40 a.m. are in character, but he lifts her card and she lets it go.

**Overall:** the voice and the setup are good. Chapter 1 is a very good chapter. The book tapers from "I can't look away" to "I'm comfortable and mildly informed," so Chapter 5 needs to put something at risk.
