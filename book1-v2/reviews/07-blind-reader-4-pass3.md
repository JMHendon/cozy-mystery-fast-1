I read it straight through. Chapter 1 is a lot better than the three after it.

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never came close to quitting, but I skimmed in three places.

- **The seating chart in Ch. 1.** "Lorelei Crane sat across from him, her daughter Ivy at her left hand, then Warren Gilley, then Vivienne at Archie's right; on his left, Kev, then Shannon Mayo at Lorelei's right." That's the book's first real information dump, and I couldn't picture it without sketching it. Everything after depends on who is adjacent to whom, so it matters, but a reader can't absorb it in one sentence.
- **The same deduction told three times.** Archie explains that Ivy let go at 8:07 and wiped the trumpet in Ch. 1, again to Calloway in the corridor in Ch. 2, and again with the funnel in Ch. 3. I knew it each time, and the funnel demo adds the least. Ch. 3 is where I felt the book idling.
- **The June negotiation.** "Vegas. 2019." / "That was a *cloak.*" is a good beat. The Montreal threat, the hearse, and the handshake keep going after it has landed. It's charming, but nothing in it moves.

By Ch. 4 the rhythm is also predictable. Archie does a bit, then a dry woman deflates him and has the last word: "Don't be." / "You did the hands." / "Go to bed, Archibald." / the unordered pie. It's a good engine, but I noticed it was an engine.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't come

- **The million-dollar clause.** "If anything happened in this room tonight that Archie couldn't explain before the lights came up…" Gus's voice saying *Vivi… Sparrow… the trunk* is exactly that. Teddy brings it up once ("Technically, something just happened in that room you can't explain"), and the book drops it. I expected it to bite.
- **Trying SPARROW on the lock.** He types S-P-A and stops. "Nice try. Not from you." Then he tries SEANCES. This man keeps a "notebook of failures" and has tried everything, so refusing to test the one word he was handed doesn't feel like him. It feels like the author holding the plot back. His "she'd have counted" reasoning is clever, but it works as an excuse.
- **Ivy.** She's the most interesting person in the book and she's mute. "Staring at the lighthouse over the coffee machine as if it owed her money" is my favorite line about her. Then she declines to hand over her coat and vanishes. I expected a reason, or at least a scene from her side.
- **Calloway's "Sea View" sticker.** Archie opens the question ("whoever you were visiting—") and she shuts it. I'm waiting for the payoff.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

- **Lorelei Crane** is the most magnetic, and the book killed her in Ch. 2. "Brother Amos says there's a skeptic in the circle. He forgives him." / "Not tonight, honey" / "Not… not the spirits, honey." I missed her immediately. That works for the story, but it means the best scene partner Archie has is gone.
- **Archie** is the funniest in Ch. 1: "It's a spirit, not an audit." / "Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down."
- **Bev** is the funniest per word. "Everything that comes back gets washed." / "There's no third pie."
- **June** comes close with "That's three words, and one of them is a contraction."
- **Calloway** is the closest to magnetic. "Do you," with "no question mark on it anywhere." / "I would." / "Don't be." Her flaw is the intro, "a braid that had clearly been a professional decision," which is stock-cop description.
- **Lenny** gets "It's a funnel… Is not complicated" and little else.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

Spend time with him. In Ch. 1 I wanted to be him, because competence at that level is a pleasure and the prose lets you sit inside it. After that he's closer to a charming guest. He's never wrong in a way that costs him. His wrong theories are throwaways ("Warren went the color of skim milk"), and June, Bev and Calloway absorb every consequence.

The moment I liked him most is the one where he stops performing. "I can see exactly how it looks. I'd arrest me." Shannon's "You took him away from me" is the other. I'd like more scenes where his wit fails to protect him.

## 5. What happened, and who I suspect

I don't trust Archie's mouthpiece theory. He's alive after blowing a raspberry into it at 7:36, but he never establishes that the poison was on the trumpet and not in the tea. He drank the same chamomile from a different cup. Ranking:

1. **Ivy.** She ran the rod, glove and remote, she didn't cry, and she wiped the trumpet. But she did it with Archie at floor level, in plain light. "She doesn't drink anything I don't pour" is a planted line. I half suspect she was wiping off a fraud (a voice device in the mouthpiece) rather than a poison. She's my top suspect and my least confident one.
2. **Warren Gilley.** He's the only one with a concrete motive that hurts Archie: the sale "clause with your name in it" and the whisper in his ear. The book mocks him constantly, which reads as red-herring behavior. He's still second.
3. **Vivienne.** I don't think she's the killer, but she's the biggest liar in the book. "Don't you dare. Not yet" came *before* the trumpet turned to them, so she knew it would say her name. She also cut the monkshood the same afternoon and won't say who Sparrow is.
4. **Teddy Marchand.** Insurance, "she was going to retire," and a panic about the special. It's all too neat and comes from Archie's "I made that up" riff.
5. **Dark horses:** Hollis ("Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house" put him in that chair) and Lenny (skipped the séance).
6. **Shannon.** I'd be angry if it were her, which is probably why I'm suspicious of the book for making her so sympathetic.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, right now. The 9 a.m. Brannock interview is the best hook so far. What I want next:

- Margaret Okafor in person. "I'm going to have you killed" is the book's best phone voice and she needs a body.
- Brannock as a real antagonist with an actual theory of Archie.
- Warren in that board room, with the clause on the table.
- One thing Archie gets wrong that costs him.
- A real step on Sparrow and the trunk, from someone other than Archie reading the air.
- Ivy, or Vivienne and Ivy together. That hand on her back, and Ivy letting it stay, is the most interesting gesture in the four chapters.
- Less retelling. Give me new information.

## 7. Rereads, unpicturable, unbelievable

- **Reread:** the seating, as above. Also *Sparrow*. The voice says "Viv… Vivi… Sparrow. The trunk, Sparrow." It reads like another pet name for Vivienne, but Archie asks "Who's Sparrow?" as if it's a third person. I can't tell whether that's deliberate ambiguity or confusion.
- **Didn't believe, Shannon:** Archie recognizes "which newspaper the name had come from," so he knew about Tommy. Why is a woman widowed in February sitting at the table of a séance he's about to publicly debunk? Whoever invited her did something cruel, and the book doesn't say who.
- **Didn't believe, the sticker:** Archie says "You grew up in Merrow" from a visitor sticker. Nothing shown supports that. Pinkham calling her Tess is the only clue, and it reads like a lucky guess that happens to be right.
- **Tone:** Archie laughing "half horror and half delight" at the monkshood a few hours after a death is a risk. June flags it ("Don't say gift"), which helps. Shannon's scene and "I liked her" are what keep me on his side.
- **Texture:** "Ayuh," the walrus mustache, the liars' table and "from away" feel like casting, not observation. Repeated tics also stood out: Calloway's face "did nothing at all" / "didn't change" / "something moved at the very corner of her mouth," and "which was an answer."
