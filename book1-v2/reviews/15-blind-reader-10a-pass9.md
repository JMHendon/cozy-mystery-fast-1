I'd read on, and I was never close to quitting. Here is where it sagged and where it got ahead of me.

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

Nowhere seriously, but I felt three soft spots.

- **The debunk loop in Chapter 1.** Each spirit event is followed by Archie explaining it, and the pattern repeats five times: the rings, the bell, the glove, the remote and the trumpet. The bell ("Telescoping rod. Six feet of it, from Ivy's lap. You owe me ten dollars") is fun. By the glove and the remote ("It's a pocket remote. Ivy's, in her lap. One free hand: the rod, then the glove on the rod, now the remote") I could predict the beats. The *rules* paragraph (the search, the refused chair, the thermos, "She doesn't drink anything I don't pour," "The tea truce") was also too much to absorb while the scene was moving.
- **The ER in Chapter 2.** Teddy with a phone at each ear ("A *medical event*, Jen. We do not say that word") is a sitcom producer. A bystander barging into a code and getting "Say that again" from the doctor is cartoonish. It worked only because it was brief.
- **The middle of Chapter 3.** This was the longest stretch with no new information. Archie re-explains the trumpet to Lenny and June ("It's on the mouthpiece. And there were cameras on that table from half past seven"), and we already know all of it. Then comes the negotiation over no cameras, no hearse and no cape. It's charming, but it's a rule-setting scene for rules the book will clearly break. The only payoff is the vase.

The scarf also gets restated about four times: seen in Ch. 1, described to Calloway in Ch. 2, replayed in Archie's head, and raised again in Ch. 4 with "And Ivy's scarf?" By the last mention I was ahead of the book.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **The trunk.** Vivienne is sitting on it like a gargoyle, Lenny "could have had it open in four minutes with a drill," and Archie arrives holding "his notebook of failures." I expected an attempt on it, a break-in, or at least Archie trying the dials. He just leaves.
- **Sparrow.** Archie asks his mother three times in a row (Ch. 1, then twice in Ch. 3), and she deflects each time with "Go to bed." By the third ask the engine stalls. I wanted him to try anyone else, or try something else.
- **Ivy's lie.** "She held my hand. The whole time" "went into Archie like a splinter," and then he says nothing in the room. When he tells Calloway privately, all she does is write. I expected her to react.
- **The million.** The reminder is set for 9 a.m., and in Ch. 4 he tells Margaret "I'm writing a million-dollar check," and she says "You are *not*." The check is the biggest open promise. It collides with the 9:00 interview, and I expected it sooner.
- **The voices.** The funnel explains how the sound got out. It doesn't explain how Lorelei knew Tommy's voice, Gus's cadence ("a hint of Maine under a lifetime of stage diction"), or the word "Sparrow." Archie admits this, and I'm waiting for it.

## 3. Funniest, most magnetic

- **Funniest:** Archie, narrowly. His best line is the voice from under the table: "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" "Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down" is also good.
- **Most magnetic:** Calloway, and it isn't close. She's on the page for maybe 1,500 words and has the book's best exchanges: "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward." "I would." Then "Don't be," and "Then I'll take it from you, Bev." She works because she's spare, and her economy makes Archie's patter funnier by contrast. The sticker moment ("You were visiting your mother." "Don't.") is the only place in four chapters where Archie is actually wrong and it costs him something.
- **Close behind:**
  - Bev, who gets the best closers ("You did. As a protest." and "I was nine").
  - June ("Dad," she said. It was the whole sentence; "That's three words").
  - Lenny ("It's a funnel... Is not complicated").
  - Pinkham ("He drives fast for his age").
- **The one I miss:** Lorelei. "Brother Amos is with us. He senses a skeptic" was the best antagonist entrance in the book, and she's dead by page 40. The book now has to replace her.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

I wanted to be him for about six pages. The opening, "holding his mother's hand in the dark and having the time of his life," makes the room's delight his gift. The sentence that actually made me like him is "Secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would [collect]."

After that I'd rather sit next to him than live in his head. The patter doesn't stop for a dying woman, a grieving family or a coroner. "Only emotionally" is a quip said to a doctor checking him for poisoning. Calling the murder "the best show I've ever seen" and "a puzzle" is gross, though June calls it out. He's also right about nearly every mechanism: the toe, the rod, the remote and the aconite. The omniscience drains suspense.

The moments I liked him most are the ones where the act fails:

- "Not the spirits, honey... I haven't finished beating you."
- "I liked her."
- "I can't," to June.
- The Calloway sticker.

I want more of those and less of him winning.

## 5. What happened, and the suspects

**Theory.** Lorelei's act was a machine: cold-calling locals ("A girl called me in April"), researching the dead, and producing voices. Someone inside or near that machine poisoned her during the window between 7:36, when Archie blew through the clean trumpet, and 8:17, when it rose. The cameras were dark from 8:14, which fits that window. The most likely route is the mouthpiece. I'd also flag the **lipstick**, which is mentioned three times ("dark-red lipstick looked suddenly too bright," "smeared red mouth," "smeared up one cheek"). Ivy wiped the narrow end of the trumpet, which could be about the lipstick rather than a fingerprint. The thermos is a second route that Bev's washing may have destroyed.

The "Sparrow... the trunk... the answer's in the—" message cut off mid-word. Archie read that as craft, but it could be the poison landing. If so, the killer didn't control the timing, or wanted that sentence unfinished. I suspect the death and Gus's trunk are connected.

**Ranking:**

1. **Ivy Crane.** She has access, a documented lie, and evidence-handling. But the scarf has been flagged so often that either it's the answer or a decoy. She may be protecting the *fraud* rather than a murder, since admitting she let go of her mother's hand exposes the act. I'd put her at about 35%, because no motive has been shown.
2. **Teddy Marchand.** He controls the cameras and the production's research. He said "Static, static, love it, keep going" while the cameras died, and "Oh, *Lori*" suggests history. A dead medium on a Halloween special is also worth money.
3. **Warren Gilley.** He went "the color of skim milk" after Lorelei whispered to him. He has a cousin rushing to protect him and a "clause in your sale." He reads as a man with a 1988 secret more than as a poisoner. He was in the circle holding hands, though as board chair he may have had keys to the building.
4. **Hollis Tibbetts.** She is the house manager and has the keys, the booth and the lighting board. She has no inner life yet, which in a mystery is itself a flag.
5. **Vivienne.** "Don't you dare. Not yet" gave me a chill, and she knows who Sparrow is. I think she's concealing rather than killing, but she may have known what the trumpet would say.
6. **Follow the million.** Nobody has asked who benefits from the *Challenge payout* or what "the charity of her choice" is. Killing Lorelei inside the Challenge, on camera, guarantees the check. Teddy's lawyers raise it immediately. I'd look at the charity before Warren.

I'd rule out Shannon Mayo and Bev. Shannon is the one person Archie was "sure wasn't in on it," and the book sat her in the murder seat, which makes me slightly wary. Still, her grief is the strongest feeling in the book.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, straight away. The chapter-ending lines are strong ("Then I'd better be right," the bell buoy ringing twice). What I specifically want:

- **The 9:00 interview with Brannock and Margaret Okafor.** I want Archie outplayed by someone in a room, because right now he's the smartest person in every scene.
- **Calloway with real friction.** Archie pocketed her card and texted her the vase. I'd like her to be furious about it, or to catch him leaving something out.
- **Ivy as a person.** She has been a face and a coat so far, and I want her to speak at length.
- **What Lorelei whispered to Warren.**
- **The check:** does he write it, and what does it set off?
- **Someone other than Vivienne answering the Sparrow question.**
- **Calloway's own story.** The memory-care sticker and "I was nine" suggest she's local and has a stake. I guessed the person at Sea View is connected to 1988, which may be wishful.

## 7. Rereads, things I couldn't picture, things I didn't believe

- **The seating.** Seven people at a "small oak table," and the whole mystery turns on who is holding whose hand. I had to work it out on paper: Archie, Vivienne, Warren, then Ivy or Shannon, Lorelei, the other of Ivy and Shannon, Kev. That is solvable, but it needs a cleaner sentence.
- **Warren's whisper timeline.** In Ch. 4 Archie says: "Twenty minutes before the lights went out, Lorelei leaned over and whispered in his ear, and Warren went the color of skim milk... I was standing right there." Then: "fifty minutes later, she's—". Those can't all be true. Twenty minutes before the lights went out is about 8:00, in the dark, with Archie seated, Lorelei across the table, and Warren not beside her. Fifty minutes later would be after she was already in the ER. It's also a major event introduced only in dialogue. I didn't believe Archie saw it.
- **The Ch. 3 timeline.** He says she was "fine for forty minutes" and then talked "ten minutes" through the trumpet. The trumpet rose at 8:17 and the symptoms came within a couple of minutes, so neither number fits.
- **The notebook of failures.** I didn't understand what it was.
- **Archie accusing Warren aloud in the Galley.** I believe it of his character, but not minutes after June's "No show." It's funny ("I said *hear me out*"), but it contradicts the man who just promised his daughter he'd stay out of it.
- **Believing it:** the trauma-room intrusion needs the doctor to behave less conveniently.
