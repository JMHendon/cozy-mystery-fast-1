# Reader's report, after Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never really wanted to quit, but I slowed down in three places.

**The bell, glove and remote run in Chapter 1.** The pattern is a gasp, then Archie explains the trick, then a joke: "*Telescoping rod. Six feet of it, from Ivy's lap. You owe me ten dollars.*" Then "*A damp glove on the same rod*." Then "*It's a pocket remote. Ivy's, in her lap. One free hand: the rod, then the glove on the rod, now the remote…*" By the third round I was ahead of him. I knew the trumpet was coming, because "having the time of his life" is a flag that the floor is about to drop. One round could go.

**The first half of Chapter 3.** The goulash, the funnel and "Don't say gift" mostly repeat what I already had. The trumpet's narrow end goes to the mouth, and "*It's on the mouthpiece*." Then comes the negotiated rules list (no cameras, no hearse, no cape, "*Vegas. 2019.*" / "*That was a* cloak."). It's charming and I liked the "hands" tell, but it's a family-sitcom stretch sitting where the plot should be moving. Archie says "*I'm the alibi*" and June says "*You're the suspect*," and in Chapter 4 he says "*I'd arrest me*." That's the same beat three times. The third is the best, so cut the first two.

**Rhythm tics, which wear on you by Chapter 4.**
- "*which was worse*" appears twice in Chapter 3 ("*She waited, which was worse*" and "*She didn't say anything about it, which was worse*").
- The beat "He could X. He could Y. He didn't." recurs: "He could stop it…", "He could have argued…", "several charming things… lining up behind his teeth."
- Similes arrive in clusters. Some are great ("as if it owed her money," "a face like a split cedar shingle"). Some aren't. Two cups "like a pair of clean teeth" made me stop and try to picture it.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't come

- **SPARROW.** The trunk has "*Seven brass letter-dials*." The voice says "*Sparrow*." Sparrow is seven letters. Archie says "*Seven letters*" and Vivienne says "*I know how many letters it is*," and then nobody says the word. I assumed someone would try it that night. If the book wants me ahead of its detective, fine. If I'm supposed to be puzzled, I'm not.
- **The sticker.** Archie gets Calloway's mother wrong and it clearly wounds her: "*Don't.*" I expected it to come back in Chapter 4, even as a glance. It doesn't. Her one real moment of vulnerability gets no echo.
- **The million-dollar check.** It's the premise of the first page and the "best or worst thing you've ever done" moment. In Chapter 4 it's a phone aside ("*You are* not*.*"). The stakes, and the November 15 closing on the building, are named and then parked.
- **Warren's whisper.** Chapter 4 says "*Twenty minutes before the lights went out, Lorelei leaned over and whispered in his ear*." I don't remember seeing that in Chapter 1, and Archie narrates everything there. It feels like a clue that wasn't planted. Also, "*three months of failures in his own writing, one word a night and some nights forty*" is a mystery about Archie that appears once and vanishes.
- **The Brannock interview and the footage.** We're told Brannock "*watched the footage at two this morning*" and wants Archie in. The chapter ends before it. That's fair, but it's the thing I most wanted to see.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Archie is funniest in Chapter 1 and only there.** "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" and "*Warren once chaired a meeting about parking. He's never come down.*" are real laughs. After Chapter 1 the patter turns into reflex, and the people around him win by saying less.

**Close behind:**
- **Vivienne.** "*Don't be. It was a very nice glove.*" and "*It is a* centerpiece*.*" Her lines are the best-written per word in the book.
- **Bev.** "*Washed it.*" / "*You did. As a protest.*" / "*You were a lot more pleasant at nine.*" / "*I was nine.*" She also needs the fewest words.
- **June.** "*That's three words.*" and "*You did the hands.*"

**Most magnetic is Calloway.** "*Do you,*" she said, "*with no question mark on it anywhere.*" Her stillness is the only thing that made Archie shut up. The sticker scene is the best scene in four chapters.

**Biggest waste is Lorelei.** She's vivid in about forty lines ("*Brother Amos is with us… He senses a skeptic*") and then she's dead. I'd have paid for more of her.

## 4. Be him, or just spend time with him?

Spend time with him. In Chapter 1 I did want to be him: "*the cold delight he'd been chasing since he was four*" and reading the table like a map is a great fantasy. After that, he's almost never wrong. The sticker is the one miss, and he's right about the scarf, the rings, the tea and the hands. Within an hour of Lorelei's death he's laughing until his eyes run at the monkshood. The book tells me he's sorry ("*I liked her*") more than it shows it.

The one place I liked him better was Chapter 4. He lectures the liars' table about Shannon's husband and she shuts him down: "*I don't need you to do that.*" That's the first time his cleverness costs him something. I want more of that and less of "*terrible, terrible puzzle*" delivered with relish.

## 5. What happened, and who I suspect

I don't think Archie's mouthpiece theory is the book's answer. Calloway goes straight for the thermos, and "*Chamomile. Different cups.*" stops her pen. Brannock wants to see Archie after watching the footage. I suspect the tea is the real route, or at least that it's in play. I'm also suspicious that Archie is so sure of "*It's on the mouthpiece*."

**Ranking:**

1. **Ivy Crane.** She's the only one with hands-on evidence tampering, a flat lie to police ("*She held my hand. The whole time.*"), and zero visible grief. She also said "*She doesn't drink anything I don't pour*" and then let her mother drink Archie's tea. I only half-believe it, though. A poisoner who wipes the trumpet in front of seven people is odd. I suspect she's at least covering for her mother's fraud, maybe a gimmick in the mouthpiece.
2. **Warren Gilley.** The skim-milk whisper, the building sale and the "clause" give him the only concrete motive on the table. The cousin gag makes him look like a joke, which is why I don't clear him.
3. **Vivienne.** The key line is "*Don't you dare… Not yet.*" She says it before the trumpet swings toward them. How did she know the voice was coming? She cut the monkshood that afternoon, and she's guarding the trunk. I don't think she's the killer. I think she's the one hiding what "Sparrow" means, and the book may be nudging me toward her anyway.
4. **Teddy Marchand.** He was in the wing during the camera blackout ("*Static, static, love it, keep going*"), and his people are the likely source of the "girl" who called Shannon in April. His panic about the word "poison" cuts against him, though.
5. **Lenny.** He skipped the séance, built every Gus illusion, and knows how the Glass Box worked. He's a source for how a trumpet could carry Gus's voice, not a murderer.

I'd clear Bev, Shannon, Kev, Hollis and Pinkham for now. The monkshood sitting in a bouquet in the hero's kitchen narrows the poison's source to the house, and I don't like that at all.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 now?

Yes, because of the unanswered questions: Sparrow, the trunk, Brannock, the footage and Ivy's coat. I'm not reading for Archie's jokes.

**What I want:**
- The Brannock interview, with Margaret Okafor in the room, where Archie's cleverness doesn't help him.
- A real answer on mechanism: tea or trumpet, and what the cameras show about who touched either.
- Calloway and Archie in a room again, with consequences from the sticker.
- Ivy saying something that isn't flat.
- No new named characters for at least a chapter. By Chapter 4 I'm tracking about twenty names.
- Lorelei back in some form (the footage, a flashback), because she's the best character the book has killed.

## 7. Rereads, things I couldn't picture, things I didn't believe

- **The watch trick (Chapter 2).** "*He held up a plain steel watch… and held it out to her*" doesn't tell me it's hers until "*She looked at her bare wrist.*" I also never saw him take it. The follow-up, "*I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward*" / "*Smart. Most people only count their fingers.*", doesn't parse as a joke. I reread it twice.
- **The seating and hand-holding in Chapter 1.** It works, but I had to diagram it. Archie, Vivienne, Warren, Shannon, Lorelei, Ivy, Kev fits, and so does Ivy having one free hand while Lorelei floats the trumpet with the other. It's a lot of load for a reader in the dark.
- **Kneeling sightline.** Archie sees Ivy wipe the trumpet "*at the level of everybody's shoes*" while he's checking Lorelei's pulse. Believable for a magician, but a stretch.
- **The trauma bay.** Archie barges into a resuscitation, shouts "*huge admirer of medicine*," and the doctor says "*Got it.*" It's cozy-mystery logic, and I accepted it, but barely.
- **Calloway's tolerance.** She lets a witness and likely suspect hand her instructions ("*Ask her about her scarf*"), then text her photos of evidence. Fun, but she's not that kind of cop in her other scenes.
- **The voice in the trumpet.** "*An old man's voice now, dry and fond, with a hint of Maine under a lifetime of stage diction*" is coming out of a woman's mouth, with no word about whether she's an impressionist. It matters, and it's glossed over.
- **A typo.** In Chapter 3: `I don't know how this was." "And she said Sparrow…` has a stray quote mark that looks like a dropped paragraph break.
