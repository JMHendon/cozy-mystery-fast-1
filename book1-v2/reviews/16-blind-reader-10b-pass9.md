# A reader's honest response to Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where was I tempted to stop?

Never in Chapter 1. That chapter has the best voice in the book. I first felt the pull to skim in the middle of Chapter 3.

- **June summarizing the plot back to me.** "You poured her a drink on camera. You named the poison before any doctor did. You put your mouth on the trumpet." I had just read all three events, and the book was explaining its own suspense to me through a teenager.
- **The funnel demonstration.** It re-teaches the trumpet mechanism I already had, and Lenny's "Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated" doesn't earn a second pass at it.
- **The bargaining.** "No cameras." "No show." "Vegas. 2019." "That was a *cloak*." It runs longer than the information it carries. The "hands" tell is also explained twice ("You do the hands when you're about to say something that's not true," then "You did the hands." "That's why you passed."). The first time was charming and the second was the author patting the joke.
- **The first page of Chapter 4.** "Five old lobstermen in feed caps," the "liars' table," "You're from away," "Ayuh." I recognized every piece of this furniture. I kept going because Archie was in the room, not because the room was new.

## 2. When did I expect something that hadn't happened?

- **Warren's whisper.** In Chapter 4 Archie says: "Twenty minutes before the lights went out, Lorelei leaned over and whispered in his ear... I was standing right there."
  - Chapter 1 never shows this. Everyone is seated in the dark holding hands, and Warren's only line is "Shh."
  - The arithmetic doesn't work either. "Fifty minutes later, she's—" doesn't fit a collapse at about 8:20 and a death at 9:40.
  - The book is leaning its accusation on a clue it didn't plant. That reads as cheating, not mystery.
- **Ivy being held.** Archie watches her wipe the trumpet and pocket the scarf. The police chief is thirty feet away. Ivy then sits in a waiting room for two hours with the scarf in her coat.
  - She tells Calloway "She held my hand. The whole time," which Archie knows is false. Calloway "writing, glanced at him once, sideways," and that's it.
  - I expected a follow-up question.
- **The trunk.** "Seven brass letter-dials" plus "Sparrow" is seven letters. I expected someone to try it by the end of Chapter 3. Instead Vivienne sits on the lid. Between Chapters 1 and 3, "Who's Sparrow?" gets "Go to bed, Archibald," and then "Go to bed, darling." By the third deflection the withholding feels mechanical, not mysterious.
- **The million dollars.** It's the stakes in paragraph five of Chapter 1, but so far it's only a quip.

## 3. Who was the funniest, most magnetic person?

Archie, in Chapter 1, and it isn't close. "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" and "Tell Amos I'll be watching his hands. And if he hasn't got any, I'll be watching yours."

The most magnetic person, though, was Lorelei, and she's dead by page fifteen. "Brother Amos is with us. He senses a skeptic." Archie's "I haven't finished beating you," and "the corner of her smeared red mouth moved," made me want her book, not his. Killing the best foil is probably the point, but it costs the book.

Close behind:
- **Vivienne:** "It was a very nice glove." And "It is a *centerpiece*."
- **Bev:** "You did. As a protest."
- **Calloway:** "I would."
- **June:** "Don't say gift."

I worry that everyone, including Bev, Pinkham and Calloway, speaks in the same dry deadpan. Calloway's "Don't." / "Don't be." / "Don't touch it." is a good running gag, but it makes the cast sound alike.

## 4. Did I want to be him or spend time with him?

In Chapter 1 I wanted to be him: the cold delight, the running commentary ("Hardest-working woman in show business").

After that I just wanted to spend time with him, and mostly when he's wrong.
- The memory-care sticker misread, which Calloway shuts down with "Don't."
- "He should have said he was sorry. He knew it while he was opening his mouth."
- "I can't," to June.

When he's right he's a bit insufferable. He lifts a cop's watch within a minute of meeting her. He publicly dismantles Kev's joy ("You owe me ten dollars"). He's right about nearly everything else, including the aconite, Ivy's hand, and the rod. I want him to be fallible in a way that costs someone other than him.

## 5. What happened, and who do I suspect?

1. **Ivy.**
   - The lie about the hands, the wipe, the one free hand, and "A girl called me in April" all fit.
   - She's too obvious, though. My bet is she's guilty of fraud and possibly of concealing something, but not necessarily the poison. The wipe could be hiding the trick, such as a speaker.
2. **Vivienne.**
   - I don't believe she's the killer, but she knew something was coming. "Don't you dare. Not yet" comes *before* Gus's voice, and she cut the monkshood the same afternoon.
   - She says "he isn't gone. That's the rule," which implies she thinks Gus is alive. She's the key to the Sparrow secret and may be protecting someone.
3. **Hollis Tibbetts.**
   - She's the dark horse. She runs the lights and the building, which is being sold out from under her, and she's given a "sharp and calm" voice and no motive.
   - Calm minor characters with no stated motive usually matter.
4. **Warren.** Sale clause, whisper, and the color of skim milk. The book waves him so hard that I assume he's hiding something unrelated.
5. **Lenny.** He's absent, he builds everything, and he leaves silently when the million comes up. He's too neatly placed as "the one who could."
6. **Shannon.** Archie says she's "the one person at the table he was sure wasn't in on it." When a narrator vouches for someone, I flinch. Her anger at him feels real, though, so she's low.
7. **Teddy and Kev.** Teddy's "Oh, *Lori*" reads as genuine, and Kev just loves the show.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 now?

Yes, but for Archie and Calloway and the trunk, not for the plot's cleverness. What I want next:
- The cameras. What the footage from 7:30 to 8:14 shows, and the blank after it, with Archie watching frame by frame.
- Archie in front of Lieutenant Brannock with Margaret Okafor at his side.
- One scene where Calloway is ahead of him, and the sticker gets repaired.
- Ivy as a person, not a grief tableau.
- A real answer to one question, either the whisper or Sparrow, so I'm rewarded for waiting.

## 7. What I reread, couldn't picture, or didn't believe

- **The seating.** In a book where "who had a free hand" is the key clue, I had to count seven seats and reconstruct the ring myself from "Kev, on Archie's left" and "Warren... two seats to Archie's right, past Vivienne." Who holds Kev's other hand is never said.
- **Hollis.** She's "Hollis Tibbetts" in the booth, then "the house manager" coming down the stairs. I briefly thought those were two people.
- **"It's on the mouthpiece."** Archie asserts this in Chapter 3, but he himself poured Lorelei's tea, and she took it despite Ivy saying "She doesn't drink anything I don't pour." That's an obvious, deliberate clue that gets dropped. Why is the tea out?
- **The million.** A murder isn't an unexplained paranormal event, so paying out for it reads as character, not logic.
- **Procedure.** A State Police Major Crimes detective lets a suspect text her a photo of the likely weapon at midnight. I'll accept that as cozy convention, but it's a stretch.
- **A glitch in Chapter 3:** `"...I don't know how this was." "And she said Sparrow..."` has a stray quote break in the middle of Archie's speech.
