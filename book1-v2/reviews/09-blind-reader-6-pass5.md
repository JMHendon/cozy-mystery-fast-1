I'm reading as a stranger, so I don't give anyone credit for what I assume the author is building toward.

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never came close in Chapter 1. "Archie Fairweather was holding his mother's hand in the dark and having the time of his life" is a real hook, and the pivot to Shannon is the best thing in the four chapters.

The first real sag is the start of Chapter 3. Chapter 1 has just shown me the trumpet and the voices. Then Archie demonstrates with a funnel ("Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated"), and I'm being re-told what I saw. The banter that follows ("Don't say gift." / "I wasn't going to say gift.") is fine, but it's the first time the book is treading water.

The second sag runs from there into the diner. The three chapters after the séance are three versions of one scene:
- Archie riffs.
- A wry authority figure deflates him (June, Bev, Hollis, Calloway).
- Someone says a short, flat line.

By Bev's "Then the word is order eggs," I could predict the beat. The June negotiation ("You don't do a press conference in a cape." / "Vegas. 2019.") is charming but has no teeth. They shake on it, nothing is at stake, and the book moves on.

The diner also runs a casting-call parade. Each arrival gets a costume paragraph ("A short, sturdy woman in a man's oilskin... fingerless wool gloves"). Hollis's scene mostly exists to be a line of dialogue I now suspect is a clue.

## 2. Where I expected something that didn't come

- **Sparrow.** Ch1 raises it. At the end of Ch2 Archie is waiting for his mother in the parking lot, and I expected him to press her in the hearse. Ch3: "Who's Sparrow?" gets "Go to bed, Archibald." Then he nearly types it into the lock and doesn't. That's three deflections, and it's starting to feel like stalling rather than mystery.
- **Gus's voice.** Archie says "I know how everything *she* did was done," but he never explains the old man saying "Vivi" and "Sparrow." He explains Tommy's voice (the funnel) and leaves the one that matters. I wanted him either to say he couldn't or to try.
- **The trumpet's chain of custody.** The poison was clean at 7:36 and lethal by 8:17. Who touched that trumpet in between? Two cameras were rolling for part of it. Nobody mentions the footage.
- **Consequences.** He pays a million dollars with a phone alarm and a line. I expected Margaret or June to treat that as an admission, and neither does.
- **Stakes for Archie.** June says "You're the suspect" and the Galley scene implies it. But I never feel a door closing on him.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

The funniest moment is Archie's: "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" It works because Lorelei laughs "low and surprised and real."

The most magnetic person is **Lorelei Crane**, and she's dead by the end of Chapter 2. Her best lines are "Brother Amos is with us. He senses a skeptic" and the wry look "one performer gives another across a stage when the scenery falls down." Her last words are "Not the spirits." The risk is that the best chemistry in the book was Archie and Lorelei, and I'm now asked to transfer it to Archie and Calloway.

Close behind her:
- **Calloway.** "I'd shake your hand, but you look like you'd count your fingers afterward." / "I would." Then "You were a lot more pleasant at nine." / "I was nine." She's the only person who gets the better of him.
- **Margaret Okafor.** One phone call: "If this is not about a wedding cake, I'm going to have you killed." I want her on the page.
- **Bev.** She's the funniest supporting character: "You did. As a protest."
- **June**, for "You did the hands."
- **Vivienne**, for "It is a *centerpiece*."

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

I'd spend an evening with him, but I wouldn't be him. He's wonderful when he's delighted ("Oh, that's *gorgeous*... best float I've ever seen"). When he's wrong, he's wrong in ways I liked. Shannon's "I don't need you to do that" is the best correction he gets.

Two things keep me at arm's length:
- **The patter doesn't stop for anything.** He laughs at the monkshood centerpiece right after the death, and then gets "Plenty of time to solve a murder" as an exit line. The grief ("I liked her") is real but lasts about a page, and he's right back to theories.
- **He's never wrong.** Aconite is confirmed by the doctor ("It was the rhythm you said it would be"). He "knew exactly... which newspaper the name had come from" and shows me nothing. A hero who's always right gets less sympathetic.

The moment I wanted to be near him was the hands: "He could make forty thousand people believe a woman had vanished. He had never once been able to lie to his daughter."

## 5. What happened and who I suspect

The poison was probably on the narrow end of the trumpet, applied between 7:36 and 8:17. Lorelei's mouth went numb at 8:19 or so, and the message was cut off mid-sentence, either by accident or because she'd gone off-script.

Ranking, with low confidence:

1. **Ivy.** She has the best opportunity and the strongest tells. She lied: "She held my hand. The whole time." She wiped the narrow end with the scarf and pocketed it. She kept her coat buttoned and wouldn't hand it over. But Archie himself says "There are reasons a medium's assistant wipes a trumpet that have nothing to do with poison," and her behavior is fully explained by protecting the act. I think she's involved, but I don't think she's the poisoner. She's the red herring or the accomplice.
2. **Hollis.** She runs the lights from a booth nobody's watching. She was the one who said "Don't cancel her. Beat her. In Gus's house," and she brings it up unprompted at the moment he wonders whether he should have cancelled. She's calm: "Ambulance is coming, Archie!" And she's old enough to know Gus-era nicknames. Her scene reads like misdirection.
3. **Warren.** He has a motive: Lorelei whispered something that turned him "translucent." He also has a clause with Archie's name in it. A conviction or scandal around Archie would void the sale. But I can't see how he got to the trumpet.
4. **Teddy.** Archie floated the insurance theory first, and in books like this that usually means no. His grief ("Oh, *Lori*") also reads as real.
5. **Vivienne.** She's not a killer, but she knows more than she says. In Ch1 she says "Don't you dare. Not yet" before the Gus voice appears, so she knew something was coming. She cut the monkshood that afternoon. That's the biggest tell in the chapters.

I can't place **Lenny**, who skipped the séance as "a waste of a perfectly good Monday" and then left the kitchen silently.

The monkshood from Gus's fence is either someone using the Fairweathers' garden to frame them or someone in the family. I can't tell which.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5 right now?

Yes, but because of Calloway and the cast, not the plot. What I want:

- **The 9:00 interview** with Lieutenant Brannock, Archie and Margaret in the same room.
- **Ivy speaking.** She has maybe three lines in four chapters, and I need to hear her voice.
- **Some trace of Lorelei** (a notebook, a dressing room, a voicemail) so the loss hits me more.
- **The footage.** Who touched the trumpet between the search and the séance?
- **A real setback for Archie**, something that costs him.
- **Fewer theories.** He's guessed at Warren, Teddy and "a very upsetting whisper" with no basis. I'd like one deduction I can check against the page.

## 7. Rereads, pictures I couldn't make, things I didn't believe

- **The lock.** *The lock.* "Seven letters. Of course it was seven letters. She'd have counted... 'Nice try. Not from you.'" This is the one thing I flatly didn't believe. A man who has tried every word since August and keeps a "notebook of failures" would try the one word that fits, since it costs nothing and it's a test. Refusing to feels like the author keeping the trunk shut until Chapter 6.
- **The watch.** I couldn't see when Archie took Calloway's watch. She "didn't offer her hand," and there's no contact described. He steals it off-page in a book whose pleasure is Archie explaining tricks.
- **Timing.** In Ch3 he says she "talks through this for ten minutes." In Ch1 the trumpet rises at 8:17 and the cough comes within a page or two. Shannon later says "six minutes." The numbers don't match what I read.
- **The rod geometry.** A telescoping rod "from Ivy's lap" reaches the bell mid-table, then the back of Warren's neck, then Vivienne's cheek. I reread the seating twice and still couldn't picture it.
- **"Dale!"** In the paragraph where Archie shouts it, narration about Pinkham is spliced into the middle of the speech, so I wasn't sure who was saying "Call it in" until the next line.
- **Procedure.** A state detective is told at 9:45 p.m. that the daughter wiped the weapon with a scarf in her pocket, and the scarf stays in the pocket overnight. "Without a judge, it's her coat" is accurate, but the delay of hours with a murder suspect free reads as a plot convenience.
- **"Tess."** Archie deduces she grew up in Merrow because "the chief had called her Tess on the radio." That exchange never appears on the page, so the clue isn't fair to the reader.
