**Overall:** Chapter 1 is excellent, and Chapters 2 to 4 are pleasant aftermath. Nothing physically happens after the collapse. It's three rooms of people talking (hospital, kitchen, diner) until the last page of Chapter 4.

## 1. Where was I tempted to stop?

I never seriously put it down, because every chapter ends on a hook ("Nobody touches that trumpet," "a voice in the dark saying *Sparrow*," "Then I'd better be right"). I came closest in four places.

- **Chapter 1, the debunk run.** The rod, the glove, the remote and the bell each get the same beat: trick, Archie's commentary, a button line. By "Hardest-working woman in show business" I admired the jokes but felt I was reading a list. Archie also explains away each effect almost as it happens, so the uncanny has no room to build.
- **Chapter 2, the hallway.** Teddy is a stock TV exec with two phones: "A *medical event*, Jen. We do not say that word." The ER barging, with a doctor who says "Wait. Say that again," is convenient.
- **Chapter 3, the weakest chapter.** The funnel demonstration re-explains what Chapter 1 already showed ("Narrow end at her mouth…"). Then comes the sitcom bargaining with the wise teenager: "No cameras." "No show." "Vegas. 2019." "That was a *cloak.*" June is "forty years old" for an instant, Lenny grumbles in three sweaters, and Archie is the genius dad who needs supervising. I recognized these people instead of discovering them.
- **Chapter 4, before Calloway walks in.** There's a lot of local color (the "liars' table," "Ayuh," the parliament) before the story moves. "Washed it," said Bev. "The Galley went completely silent" is where it wakes up.

## 2. When did I expect something that hadn't happened?

- **The footage.** Archie says "If somebody touched that trumpet after I did, somebody filmed it." Three chapters later, all I get is "Lieutenant Brannock watched the footage at two this morning." I assumed someone would tell me what's on it. It's the biggest unspent promise.
- **"Not yet."** Vivienne says "Don't you dare… Not yet" before her father's voice appears. I expected her to be pressed on it. She isn't. She says "Go to bed" twice instead, and the repeat made me impatient.
- **Archie's hope.** "Secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would [collect]." And later, "wanted it, so much his chest hurt." This is the best emotional hook in the book. Chapters 2 to 4 drop it for puzzle-solving.
- **Detective work.** Archie does none beyond noticing. I expected him to question Ivy or work the problem, but he spends Chapter 4 stirring up a diner.
- **Lenny.** He built every illusion and could open the trunk "in four minutes with a drill." I expected him to react to "Sparrow." He just leaves.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

- **Funniest line:** "*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on.*" Archie throws his voice for it, and it's the peak of the book so far.
- **Most magnetic:** Vivienne. She's funny ("Don't be. It was a very nice glove." "It is a *centerpiece*.") and she's withholding. "Not touching her, which for Vivienne was a form of prayer" is the best character line in the four chapters. She's the only person who makes me want to know the answer to a question about her.
- **Close behind:**
  - Calloway, for the sticker scene. Her "Don't" and "I would" land hard.
  - Bev: "You did. As a protest."
  - Lenny: "Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated."
  - Pinkham: "He drives fast for his age."

Lorelei is a problem. Archie says "I liked her" and "She was so good." But she gets about four lines ("He senses a skeptic"), mostly while being admired. I was told to mourn her and didn't, and her death costs Archie his best foil.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

At the start I wanted to be him ("having the time of his life"). By Chapter 4 I only wanted to sit near him.

- **Why I stopped wanting to be him:** he's right about nearly everything. He calls every trick, names the poison, and spots the scarf. The narration also tells me he's brilliant ("He could make forty thousand people believe a woman had vanished") more than it shows me.
- **When I like him best:** when the act fails. He gets the sticker wrong and sees "there was no trick in the world for taking it back." There's also "I'd arrest me," and "Not the spirits, honey… I haven't finished beating you."
- **The detail that humanizes him:** "three months of failures in his own writing, one word a night and some nights forty." I want to know what he's writing.
- **What I didn't like:** he lifts a cop's watch, and then her card, in a hospital.

## 5. What happened, and who do I suspect?

I'm unclear on the delivery method. Archie's "alibi" ("I blew through that thing at seven thirty-six and I'm alive") covers the trumpet. It doesn't cover the tea he poured, which is obviously the worse problem for him. The doctor's question, "You drank something with her?", and Calloway's pen stopping say as much.

My ranking, loosely held:

1. **Ivy.** She lied ("She held my hand. The whole time"), kept her coat buttoned, didn't cry, and wiped the mouthpiece. But the wipe was "quick and practiced," which may mean routine hygiene. Archie concedes "There are reasons a medium's assistant wipes a trumpet that have nothing to do with poison." The book hands her to me too readily, so I treat her as guilty of the fraud, and maybe the murder.
2. **Vivienne, as the one who knows, not necessarily the killer.** "Not yet" came before the voice. She cut the monkshood the afternoon of the murder, she guards the trunk, and she blanks on "Sparrow."
3. **Warren Gilley.** Lorelei's whisper made him "translucent," and he now has a lawyer and a "clause in your sale." I distrust him because Archie shouted his name in a diner. That reads like a red herring.
4. **Teddy Marchand.** He controls the footage and has the narrative spin ("We do not say that word"). He has no motive on the page, but he's the one with the most control over the evidence.
5. **Lenny.** He's too unbothered, and he knows the house and the trunk.
6. **Shannon.** She's the one Archie vouches for ("the one person at the table he was sure wasn't in on it"), so the genre makes me suspicious. But the grief reads real. The "girl" who called her in April shows the Cranes were working Merrow six months ahead. That means premeditation.

Calloway also nags at me. Bev says "You were a lot more pleasant at nine." She says "I was nine," and I wondered whether she was a kid in that theater in 1988. Given the sticker, I'd bet her Sea View connection ties to Gus. That's a guess, not a suspicion.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, but because of three questions, not momentum. I want:

- the 9 a.m. interview with Brannock and Margaret Okafor, as a real verbal duel;
- what is on the footage;
- Vivienne actually pressed on "Not yet" and "Sparrow";
- the lab result: tea or trumpet?
- one scene where Archie is wrong about a trick, not just about a person.

I'd also like some new event. A fourth chapter of reaction scenes would lose me.

## 7. Rereads, pictures, disbelief

- **Seating and hands (Chapter 1).** I rebuilt who held whom three times. I could place everyone eventually, but the "free hand" logic (Ivy has one hand for rod, glove and remote) strained me.
- **Warren's whisper (Chapter 4).** "Twenty minutes before the lights went out… and then, fifty minutes later, she's—" I couldn't find that whisper in Chapter 1, where Archie notices everything. The times don't add up either: the collapse was around 8:20. It reads like a clue invented afterward.
- **"Ten minutes."** Archie says she "talks through *this* for ten minutes." Chapter 1 gives about three minutes between the trumpet rising (8:17) and her collapse.
- **The handcuff line.** "Smart. Most people only count their fingers." I reread it twice before the watch gag made sense.
- **The monkshood centerpiece.** Archie's laugh is great ("a bouquet of the murder weapon"). But it's a coincidence engineered to implicate his family, and I'll believe it only if it isn't one.
- **The million-dollar check.** It's a character gesture I like more than I believe: a murder suspect writes a million-dollar check the morning after.
- **Prose tics.** The similes pile up ("like a kettle," "like a breaker tripping," "like owls," "like a cork," "the way you'd move a coat rack," "the build of a vending machine"). The "Not pale. *Translucent.*" construction and the italics for emphasis recur too often.
