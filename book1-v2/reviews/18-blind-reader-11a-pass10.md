# A cold read of Chapters 1–4

## 1. Where I was tempted to stop

I never came close to quitting, but I slowed down in two places.

**Chapter 1, the middle.** The rod, the glove, and the remote arrive as a list. Each time, the trick happens, Archie names it, and someone reacts. Rod: *"Telescoping rod… Six feet of it, from Ivy's lap. You owe me ten dollars."* Glove: *"A damp glove on the same rod… Sorry, Mother."* Remote: *"It's a pocket remote. Ivy's, in her lap."* By the third one I had the pattern. The chapter recovers when the trumpet rises, but that stretch is explanation standing in for tension.

**Chapter 3, from "There's goulash" to the vase.** This was the slackest stretch. The funnel demonstration repeats what Chapter 1 already showed ("Sound goes in the small end. Comes out the big end. Is not complicated"). June's "Don't say gift." / "I wasn't going to say gift." / "You were going to say gift." is a familiar sitcom rhythm. The rules negotiation ("No cameras." / "Vegas. 2019." / "That was a *cloak.*") works as a list of promises I assumed he'd break. I also felt the "Warren went the color of skim milk. Not pale. *Translucent.*" line was one adjective too many.

## 2. Where I expected something that hadn't happened

- **Archie getting sick.** He drank the tea and put his mouth on the trumpet. Rao asks, "Any numbness? Tingling?", and Archie answers, "Nothing. I'd tell you." That plants a bomb. Nothing in Chapters 3 or 4 follows it up.
- **The footage.** "If somebody touched that trumpet after I did, somebody filmed it." Two cameras ran the whole time, and Brannock "watched the footage at two this morning." Nobody in the text sees it, and Archie never demands to. The three-minute gap between the cameras dying at 8:14 and the trumpet rising at 8:17 is the obvious window, and the book just leaves it there.
- **"Sparrow."** Chapter 4 sets up the Galley as a flinch-detector: *"if you wanted to know who in Merrow had something to hide, you said a name out loud in the Galley and watched who flinched."* Archie says "Warren Gilley," not "Sparrow." That felt like a wasted beat.
- **Hollis Tibbetts.** She gets a name, a booth, and a calm voice, and then she vanishes. I kept waiting for her to come back.
- **The Nov. 15 deadline.** It's in the first page and doesn't matter until Warren's lawyer call in Chapter 4.
- **Any turn at all.** Every chapter after the first ends on a soft note: "Sparrow," the bell buoy, "Then I'd better be right." By the end of Chapter 4 I know about Shannon's "April" call and Warren's pallor, and not much else beyond what I knew at the hospital.

## 3. Funniest and most magnetic

**Vivienne.** She's the best thing here:

- "*Don't be. It was a very nice glove.*"
- "*It is a* centerpiece."
- "*Go to bed, Archibald.*" Her face does something Archie has never seen in forty-seven years.

She's funny and has a secret, and she's sitting on a trunk.

Close behind:

- **Bev.** "Everything that comes back gets washed." Then "Washed it" lands in total silence, and later: "You did. As a protest." The thermos scene is the funniest sequence in the four chapters.
- **Calloway.** She's funny by withholding: "Do you," with no question mark, then "I would" (counting her fingers), then "Don't be." She's also the only person who makes Archie lose a round.
- **Lorelei Crane**, who is the problem. *"Brother Amos is with us. He senses a skeptic."* gets her three lines. The smear of red on the corner of her mouth at "I haven't finished beating you" is a great beat. Then she's dead. Her death is the engine of the book, and I never got to enjoy her. Archie's "I liked her" asks for grief I hadn't earned yet.

Archie is the funniest person in Chapter 1 ("*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on*" is a real laugh), but he's a different kind of funny from the others.

## 4. Be him, or spend time with him?

In Chapter 1, I wanted to be him. The first line sells it: *"holding his mother's hand in the dark and having the time of his life."* His joy at being fooled is the best thing about him, as in *"Secretly, embarrassingly, he hoped someone would"* and *"Kev… such pure joy that Archie nearly loved him."*

After that I just wanted to spend time with him, and in small doses. The quip density never drops, even at the hospital and the diner. Almost every scene ends on a Fairweather exit line ("I'd arrest me," "I'm going to be very helpful," "Enjoy your eggs"). The text calls him a show-off, but the costs are small: one bad sticker guess, one rude question to Shannon. Both are the book's best moments for him, because they're the only times he's wrong.

I'd also say he's a bit unbelievable as an observer. He sees Ivy wipe the trumpet and says nothing to Pinkham, who's twenty feet away, for roughly two hours. He waits to deliver it as "a free sample." That reads as the author wanting a better scene, not as something he'd do.

## 5. What I think happened, and the suspects

**Method.** I'd put the poison on the trumpet's narrow end at about 55%, and in the tea at about 40%. Archie's alibi reasoning is weaker than he thinks. "She was fine for forty minutes" doesn't exclude the tea, and his "clean at 7:36" doesn't exclude him. If it's the trumpet, the 8:14–8:17 camera blackout is when it happened.

**Suspects, ranked:**

1. **Ivy Crane.**
   - She lied to the sergeant ("She held my hand. The whole time"), though Archie heard the rings.
   - She had a free hand and the blackout window.
   - She wiped the trumpet before anyone had named a poison.
   - She hasn't taken her coat off.
   - Lorelei's line about "anything I don't pour" implies a woman who controls her mother's consumables.
   - Counterweight: she's so obviously guilty-acting that she's either the answer or the decoy. Archie himself offers the alternative: "There are reasons a medium's assistant wipes a trumpet that have nothing to do with poison." The wipe could be hiding a gimmick.
2. **Warren Gilley.**
   - Lorelei whispered something that made him go "translucent," and his cousin ran to warn him.
   - He chairs the board that sold the theater.
   - By genre rules, though, the man Archie loudly accuses in Chapter 4 is usually a secret-holder and not the killer. He probably knows something about 1988.
3. **Teddy Marchand.** He's the producer with money riding on Friday's special, and he says "Oh, *Lori*" like someone who's lost an asset. "Static, static, love it, keep going" was cheerfully delivered from the wing right when the cameras died. He had access to the setup for hours.
4. **Lenny.** Dark horse. He built every illusion Archie owns, knows the trunk, and skipped the séance "on the grounds that it was a waste of a perfectly good Monday." He was also the only one who didn't react to "Sparrow."
5. **Hollis Tibbetts.** She's the house manager and runs the booth. She gets suspicion only by being introduced and then ignored.
6. **Shannon Mayo.** Archie put her at Lorelei's right hand "because she was the one person at the table he was sure wasn't in on it." That's a red flag in any mystery, but I don't believe it emotionally.
7. **Kev, Vivienne.** I don't think either did it. Vivienne is the book's real question: *"Not yet,"* she said when Archie went to stand, and she cut the monkshood that afternoon. She knows what "Sparrow" means.

## 6. Would I read Chapter 5?

Yes, but on the strength of Calloway, Vivienne, and "Sparrow," not the murder itself. Specifically, I want:

- **The nine o'clock interview**, with Brannock as a genuine opponent and Okafor as a weapon. I also want the footage watched, with the dark minutes on screen.
- **Ivy cornered**, and not another reported summary of her.
- **Archie wrong in a way that costs him**, not one more clever exit line.
- **Someone besides Vivienne answering "Sparrow"**, even if the answer is a lie.
- **Lorelei again**, through her old tapes, a flashback, or a rival's grudging praise. Otherwise the dead person is a rumor.
- **The check.** The book says it's happening at 9 a.m. I want to see him actually write it. It's the best decision he makes ("Rules are rules, and they're my rules"), and it's off-screen.

## 7. Rereads, pictures, belief

- **The seating.** I couldn't draw it. Warren is "two seats to Archie's right, past Vivienne," Ivy is at Lorelei's left, Shannon at her right, and Kev at Archie's left. I tried several layouts, and in the ones that fit Warren is next to Ivy, which would mean Warren holds Ivy's hand. If so, that's a clue nobody mentions. If not, the glove-on-Warren's-neck beat and Ivy's "one free hand" don't line up cleanly.
- **June and the hands.** "*You did the hands.*" / "*But I didn't* lie." / "*That's why you passed.*" I read it twice to work out what the tell means.
- **Typo.** In Chapter 3 there's a stray quote break: *"…I don't know how this was." "And she said Sparrow…"*.
- **The sticker.** "You were visiting your mother" from a SEA VIEW sticker and the age of the woman is a guess presented as deduction. I believe it as a flaw, since the book treats it as one, but the ease of it ("Easy") made me roll my eyes before the payoff.
- **Believability.**
  - The ER sequence: a stranger walks into a code and is only collared at the second try.
  - "Unattended death" for a woman who died in a hospital.
  - A man being investigated for murder accuses a select-board chairman in a diner before his lawyer arrives, right after promising his daughter he'd stay out of it.
  - Paying a million-dollar challenge in the morning of a murder investigation. I liked it as character, but not as procedure.
