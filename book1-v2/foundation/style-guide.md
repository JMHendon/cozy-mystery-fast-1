# Style guide

## Basics

- **Spelling:** US throughout (theater, color, gray, center, traveled, skeptical, mustache). Exception: proper names keep their own spelling ("Merrow Opera House").
- **Viewpoint:** close third person on Archie, past tense, every chapter, no exceptions. The reader knows what Archie sees, hears, thinks and remembers, and nothing he doesn't. This is a fair-play book: what Archie plainly sees goes on the page, even when he doesn't yet see what it means.
- **Narration:** companionable, quick, physical, opinionated through Archie's eye. The narrator gives orientation and picture; **jokes belong in Archie's mouth**, said to people. Ration narrator aphorisms and manner clauses (line-edit protocol tests 1 and 7). The narrator never explains Archie's psychology or the theme.
- **Numbers:** spelled out in dialogue and running prose; times in dialogue as people say them ("quarter to eight"); chapter headers carry the day and time in numerals.
- **Chapter header format:** `# Chapter N` then an italic line: day, date, time, place. Example: *Monday, October 27 · 8:00 p.m. · The Merrow Opera House*
- **Chapter length:** one scene, under 2,000 words (gate 7). End on a turn, decision or live question.

## Profanity

Essentially none. Permitted: "God," "Lord," "hell" (rare, adults under real stress), "damn" (rare). Archie never swears: "Holy *Houdini*," "Great Scott," "wicked" (Boston, only under stress), sparingly and varied. Lenny mutters in Hungarian ("Istenem"). Bev says "Christ on a bike" once in the book, at most. No slurs, no crude sexual language.

## The cozy contract on the page

- The death is witnessed as a collapse; no clinical detail beyond what Archie's knowledge names (numb mouth, tingling, slurring, the heart). No gore.
- Hollis's water scene at the climax is a rescue, not a suicide on the page. Her intent stays plain and uncertain ("I hadn't decided"), and the scene's energy is Archie's.
- Malvolio the cat survives everything and gets fed.

## The lead (no ceiling)

Archie's lines are the product. He talks most, best and funniest. Patter, theories, boasts, nicknames, callbacks, demonstrations, showman's address ("Ladies and gentlemen—" to one detective) all live in his dialogue at full size. He never swears, is never cruel, never punches down, never makes speeches about the theme or explains his own wound. His **sincerity drop** (the patter stops; one plain sentence) is rare and lands. Tests for every chapter: "Would Castle say this out loud?" and "Would a reader want to *be* Archie in this chapter?"

## Recurring physical facts

Archie: six-one, heron who does Pilates; dark hair silvering, uncut; fast dark eyes; hands always busy (coin, card, pen); velvet jacket, brocade waistcoat, Henley, Gus's eight-foot emerald scarf, expensive sneakers, vintage watch he winds when thinking, red-and-black buffalo-check coat. Tess: five-seven, braid, gray eyes, navy MSP fleece or charcoal coat, steel watch, gray Interceptor parked exactly between the lines. The hearse: 1958 Cadillac Miller-Meteor, eleven miles to the gallon, every dog barks at it.

## Maine procedure (keep honest)

MSP Major Crimes leads homicides outside Portland and Bangor; the OCME handles the body; the AG's office charges murder. Interviews are voluntary unless in custody. Warrants come from a judge. Chain of custody matters: when Archie hands over evidence, there is friction about how he got it. Forensic turnarounds faster than real life are labelled as convention in the plan and never stated as medical fact on the page.
