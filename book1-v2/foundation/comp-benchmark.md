## What the benchmark tells this book

1. **The death opens the book.** The one true cozy in the set (*Still Life*) has the body in its first sentence; *Magpie*'s inner novel opens on a funeral. That matches gate 1. This book goes further than either: the death happens in a scene the reader watches, in Ch 1, and is confirmed in Ch 2.
2. **The lead arrives at once.** *Still Life* puts its detective in paragraph two; *Gaudy Night* puts its heroine on page one. *Magpie*'s inner detective arrives late (Ch 9) and humbled (a diagnosis), which is exactly the shape the house standard and this book's brief forbid. This book opens with Archie in sentence one, at full strength, winning.
3. **Chapter length is the big departure.** Two comps run 5,000–10,000-word multi-scene chapters. Only *Magpie*'s inner novel works in short single-scene chapters (~1,500 words). This book follows the *Magpie* inner-novel rhythm because gate 7 requires one scene under 2,000 words. Justification: the house gate outranks the comps, and the short unit is the page-turner engine the brief asks for.
4. **Foil timing.** *Still Life* meets its team in Ch 2. This book matches it: Tess meets Archie in Ch 2 at the hospital, before he has committed, so the partnership is a fact before the investigation is.
5. **Commitment.** *Still Life* commits in Ch 2 (duty); *Gaudy Night* in Ch 5 (asked); *Magpie* late. This book commits in Ch 3 for a reason rooted in appetite and family (the trunk, his grandfather's theater, "Sparrow"), which is gate 3 and faster than two of the three comps.
6. **No comp has a Castle-type lead.** Gamache is calm and wise; Pünd is dying; Harriet is guarded. None of them is the delighted showman this book needs, so the comps benchmark structure only. The lead's register is benchmarked against the brief's reference character, not against these books.
