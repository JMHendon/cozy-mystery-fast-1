## 2. Comp benchmark and departures

From `foundation/comp-benchmark.md`:
- *Still Life*: body in sentence one; detective in paragraph two; meets his team Ch 2; chapters ~7,600 words.
- *Magpie Murders*: the inner novel opens on a funeral; the detective arrives in Ch 9, humbled; meets the inspector around Ch 19; inner chapters ~1,500 words.
- *Gaudy Night*: not a cozy and has no murder. Heroine on page one; the partner arrives Ch 14; chapters ~6,900 words.

**Departures:**
- Chapter length follows *Magpie*'s inner novel, because gate 7 requires it.
- The death is witnessed in Ch 1, earlier and more direct than any comp's on-page murder.
- The foil meets the lead in Ch 2, matching *Still Life*.
- The lead is introduced at full strength, the opposite of *Magpie*'s humbled detective.

No comp has a Castle-type lead, so they benchmark structure only.

## 3. The acts (summaries; full maps in outline.md)

**Act 1, Beginning Hook (Ch 1–11).** Archie is beating America's favorite medium in the dark when she dies through her own trumpet. He names the poison and forces the screen. He commits at midnight on his daughter's terms. By morning he is the honest suspect (the tea on tape, the washed thermos, the monkshood in his mother's vase), and his public theory about Warren Gilley has put the opera-house sale at risk.
- *II* death (1–2) · *TP* Warren's motion and Brannock's public summons (10–11) · *Crisis* step back or investigate in public (11) · *Climax* he commits before the town (11) · *Resolution* Tess lets him demonstrate the method (12).

**Act 2A, Middle Build I (Ch 12–22).** Archie learns that the whole town learned the trick in Mrs. Tibbetts's senior English. He is wrong about Warren and makes amends with the right gift. He tells Shannon the truth and coaxes Kev toward honesty. Tox confirms murder.
- *II* the hearse séance (12) · *TP* aconitine positive (19) · *Crisis* hand over Kev's footage, which clears him and sinks Ivy (21) · *Climax* he hands it over (22) · *Resolution / midpoint* poison inside the small end; the question becomes who knew where her lips would go; police turn to Ivy (22).

**Act 2B, Middle Build II (Ch 23–33).** The warrant at the inn; Vivienne keeps "Sparrow"; Archie hauls a trap and learns Teddy lied. A famous favor gets the B-roll, which narrows the case to forty unfilmed seconds. Archie proves his raspberry went through the horn; the AG charges Ivy anyway.
- *II* the warrant (23) · *TP* Ivy charged (30) · *Crisis* do the show and quit, or keep going (30–31) · *Climax* he takes Tess's rule-breaking help; June reads the code (31–32) · *Resolution* "P.L., Merrow" (33).

**Act 3, Ending Payoff (Ch 34–44).** "Langley? That's Hollis." Archie tells Tess and plays his Halloween show with June in the Empty Chair. The booth is empty. He chases Hollis across the fogbound harbor and goes into the water at Shag Ledge. Her confession confirms his deduction. Ivy is freed and the town resets around an empty light booth. Archie, June and Vivienne open the trunk: SPARROW, and a 1996 postcard from Key West in Gus's hand.
- *II* the click (34) · *TP* the empty booth (35) · *Crisis* wait for the Marine Patrol or go (36) · *Climax* the rescue (37–38) · *Resolution* 39–44.

## 5. What the blind readers said, and what changed

Seventeen independent cold reads, each given only the four chapter files (table in `reviews/22-revision-log-and-reader-verdicts.md`; full reports in `reviews/`).

- **Tempted to stop:** never before the crime. The slow spots in late reads were the method-calling run mid-Ch 1 and the first half of Ch 3's kitchen.
- **Expected but missing:** Sparrow answered; the footage; Ivy speaking; a cost for Archie. The plan delivers the first three in Ch 5, 21–23, 28 and 31.
- **Funniest:** early reads named Lorelei. From pass 3 on, nearly every read named Archie ("*Amos, it's your mother. Put a sweater on*").
- **Most magnetic — BLOCKING, UNRESOLVED:** Lorelei in reads 1–6. After that a mix: Lorelei, Calloway, and Vivienne. The stated reason never changed: the most magnetic character is the one who withholds, or the foil the book kills.
- **Be him or spend time with him:** every reader said "spend time with him," and wanted to *be* him in Ch 1.
- **Suspects:** Ivy is first in almost every read. Hollis fell from 1st–2nd (early drafts) to 4th–unranked.
- **Read Ch 5 now:** yes, every time.

**What I changed:**
- Archie now co-stars the séance (the rap duet, the bet, the ventriloquism).
- His jokes and reads cost him:
  - "You're breaking up" while she's dying.
  - The wrong guess about Tess's mother.
  - The Warren accusation that triggers the clause.
  - The tactless question to Shannon.
  - He's thrown out of the trauma bay.
- His wound is on the page: he was ten, in the second row, in 1988.
- He makes a generous, costly choice: paying the Challenge.
- He takes Lorelei's last line.
- Supporting cappers and narrator flattery were cut. Two quips were moved off grave moments.
- Hollis is out of Ch 4. Her two Ch 4 facts moved to Ch 5 and Ch 11.
- Vivienne guards the trunk.
- Recaps cut; seating and hand logic clarified.

No Archie dialogue was cut to fix other tics, and his size is intact.

**Status:** the finding is not fixed by simulated readers. I stopped because the remaining lever readers point to is making Archie withhold and say less, which the lead card forbids ("He grows; he doesn't shrink"). Recommend an author's or human cold read before Ch 5. If a human also names someone else, the structural lever is in the plan's Ch 5 and Ch 20 interviews: let Archie's cleverness fail in public, and lose there.

## 6. Conflicts resolved by the order of authority; carryover staging changed

1. **Gate 1 vs pre-séance canon.** The book opens at 8:00 in the dark. The controls, toot and tea come back as a recalled clause in Ch 1 and as evidence on tape in Ch 5. "Beat her" moves to Ch 5 (Archie quoting Hollis). "Wrote to the TV people" moves to Ch 11. Shannon's readings arrive in Ch 4 (the April call) and Ch 14.
2. **Lead card vs reader requests to "stop performing."** The lead card outranks reader opinion. I answered with costs, not silence.
3. **Lead card ("thrilled by murder") vs readers who found the gift/puzzle beat tasteless.** Kept.
4. **Canon trunk (SPARROW; Archie has forgotten the nickname) vs readers calling his refusal stalling.** Canon kept. Staging changed: Vivienne sits on the trunk and refuses, which matches her canon refusal to try it.
5. **Hollis's drafting rule (never spotlighted).** Early readers flagged her, so I changed her staging (see §5). No canon was changed.
6. **Forensic pace.** The five-day calendar uses three labelled conventions (screen ~48 h, trumpet ~68 h, scarf ~14 h). None is stated as medical fact.
7. **Prompt blocking rule vs lead card.** The remaining reader-suggested fix would shrink the lead. I kept the lead and report the finding open (§5). This is the one place I did not satisfy a prompt requirement, and I am flagging it rather than claiming a pass.
8. **Not a canon change, but a new invention:** Archie pays the Fairweather Challenge. It is recorded in the Ch 3 and Ch 5 briefs.

## 7. Least sure about, ranked

1. **The lead's magnetism.** Seventeen simulated readers never named Archie most magnetic, though most named him funniest. A human read may differ, but this is the book's product, so it is the top risk.
2. **Length margins.** Ch 1 and Ch 2 sit within about 35 words of the 2,000 cap including headers. Any future expansion has to cut first.
3. **The five-day calendar.** An AG murder charge on Day 5 rests on three compressed lab turnarounds. It is honest as convention, but a procedural-minded reader may balk.
4. **Hollis in Act 2.** Ch 11, 14, 16, 26 and 28 each give one ordinary fact. Genre-savvy readers flagged the warm helper early in drafting. The spread is designed, but untested beyond Ch 4.
5. **The Shag Ledge climax.** Rescuing a killer whose intent is "undecided" in 52-degree water is the darkest beat in the plan. Archie's buoyancy and the rescue framing should keep it light-energy, but it needs care in drafting.
6. **Ch 3's taste beats** (the monkshood laugh, "Don't say gift") split readers.
