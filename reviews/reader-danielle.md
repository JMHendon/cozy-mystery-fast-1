# Danielle's Reader Report: *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather*

Reader: Danielle, 34, ER nurse in Charlotte. I read it in break-sized chunks and in bed. It took me most of a week of lunches plus one very bad night shift I can't blame on the book.

**Short version:** 4.5 stars. It starts with a bang, sags in the middle, and then the last third is the best thing I've read this year. I cried in three separate chapters. I want book 2 right now.

---

## 1. Page-turner map (score out of 10, plus where I quit or stayed up)

**Ch 1, Upside Down: 8.** Great opening line ("Hanging upside down, Archie Fairweather had discovered, improved almost everything"). A straitjacket escape, a creepy-glam medium, and then the trunk line. The hook that gets me is "He says you still haven't opened his trunk." *Mild overload:* Lenny, Hollis, Teddy, Ivy and Lorelei all show up in one scene. I had to make a mental cast list.

**Ch 2, The Trunk: 6.** Harold the lobster is adorable. "He didn't name him. He introduced himself" made me laugh out loud in the break room. But the kitchen scene is long. Vivienne is a hoot ("I'm still going"). June's "promised me a real year" is the emotional hook. The turret and trunk part ends on a little click of mystery. *Skimmed the description of the kitchen with its pantry trapdoors.*

**Ch 3, The Liars' Table: 8.** The spoon-on-the-neck demo is delightful. Then it turns on a dime. Shannon at the memorial, "He told me he wasn't cold anymore." That's where I went from amused to invested. *Grabbed me:* "I won't make fun of you." "It came out plainer than he meant it to."

**Ch 4, Sea View: 9.** TESS. The braid, the gray eyes, "Dad. It's Tess." Her checking behind the piano for cameras is such a good character intro. Walt laughing at the pen made me tear up on my break, in my scrubs, in the stairwell. "She's terrifying, Chief." "Ayuh." Perfect chapter ending.

**Ch 5, The Sign-Out Card: 8.** June doing a pass in the armchair. Her "It's mine" speech, and Archie having no next line, hit me right in the mom-guilt (I'm not a mom, but I have a mom). The library card detective bit is cozy. I loved "the right thing to do was nothing at all."

**Ch 6, Two of a Kind: 8.** Lorelei by the fire is the best villain-who-isn't-a-villain I've read in ages. "You're wasted on rabbits." I *liked* her, which means the next chapters are going to hurt. Archie cold-reading her wrists was a great moment. Overheard Ivy/Lorelei in the parking lot, "You're done when I say you're done, sweetheart," gave me chills.

**Ch 7, Controls: 6.** *Almost closed the app here.* There's a long stretch of stage description and "who stood where" (candles, tray, water glasses, slide the horn left). I could feel the author laying clues and it slowed the energy. The raspberry-through-the-trumpet bit is hilarious ("PPPBBBTHHHHT"), and the thermos/tea toast has tension. But I wanted to get to the séance.

**Ch 8, The Other Side: 10.** The best chapter in the book. The dark, the green bands floating, "Shan. It's me." Then *"And Sparrow. Sparrow's here."* I put my hand over my mouth at work. Then the poison starts and the voice slurs, "The answer'sh — in—", and the trumpet drops. "Honey. Did you catch me?" "No. You were perfect." **STAYED UP TOO LATE.**

**Ch 9, The Waiting Room: 8.** Ivy in the plastic chair, "She held my hand." "You can stop now." Ivy wiping the horn and Archie *telling* Tess about it is the first big moral bomb. The "how many people in Merrow would know the symptoms of aconite" line is a great scene button.

**Ch 10, Pancakes: 8.** Pancakes shaped like cards, "The queen of hearts looked like Ohio." June's three-finger takedown ("And you're at the top of it") is a standout, a 16-year-old out-lawyering her dad. Ends on "That's not what that was." Fun, cozy, moves the plot.

**Ch 11, Walkthrough: 8.** Tess *laughing* at "I was *in* on it. I was the *accomplice*" and then composing her face. That's the swoon. I also liked the bobby-pin lock pick and "Would you like to be arrested now, or after the tour?" The remote in the umbrella stand is a good find.

**Ch 12, The Captain's Suite: 6.** The Ivy interview is good (the "she told *you*" moment is a real heartbreak), but the chapter is talky and a bit repetitive. Archie walking into an interview and being scolded for it is a trope I've seen before in this book already. *Skimmed Teddy's network spiel.*

**Ch 13, Rosie and Liam: 8.** Rosie in the ladybug costume, the pumpkin with "no fear of triangles." Shannon's "You and your bet. You took him away again" gutted me. Hollis arriving with American chop suey is such a warm beat. (In hindsight that one's a flashing red light.)

**Ch 14, Five-Card Draw: 9.** **STAYED UP.** Poker night with the whole found family. Archie's "she's in Baja" theory is a delightful derail. And then TESS walks in and takes his chips because "It's your hands. When you've got something, they stop." Vivienne's last line: "I like her. She doesn't need you to be wonderful." Screenshot-worthy, whole chapter.

**Ch 15, Hauling: 7.** Lobster boat comedy. "Harold's a girl" and "You absolute professional" made me snort. The seasickness bit and the radio ("Looks like he's making something disappear") is funny. Then Dale's phone call quietly changes the tone. Solid but a bit of a detour.

**Ch 16, Person of Interest: 7.** Brannock is a good foil (reads Archie's left thumb!). The espresso machine / heater bit is funny and Tess's "Leave the heater" is great. But it's a lot of case-recap dialogue. I wanted less of Brannock laying out the evidence and more feeling.

**Ch 17, Malvolio: 7.** I love the cat. I love Hollis's kitchen. The costume-fitting montage drags a little, though. *In hindsight:* "Don't you sit in my kitchen and mourn Lorelei Crane like she was your sister" is the moment I went from "cozy mentor" to "wait a minute."

**Ch 18, Harborview Antiques: 6.** *Almost closed the app.* June as detective is great, and her "three conditions" is cute. But then it's a long run of auction listing details ($1,150, $1,400, $975, $1,200) and I started skimming. The only part I loved is "Somebody sold my mother to a man in Ohio for nine hundred and seventy-five dollars." "In two pieces."

**Ch 19, Town Business: 9.** **STAYED UP.** Archie's public Galley takedown of Warren is cringe in the best way, and I could feel it going wrong. Then: "My wife isn't dead, Mr. Fairweather. She's up the hill at Sea View." Oh no. "You are not going to buy my wife." I hated Archie for a minute, and that's a good sign.

**Ch 20, Outside. Now.: 9.** Tess *tears into him* and it's amazing: "You built it so you wouldn't have to." "I know how a trick works, Archie. I've watched yours." His pickpocket attempt on her notebook and her hand clamping on his wrist ("That's twice") is the closest the book gets to hot. Her line "Dad likes the brown Necco wafers" got me.

**Ch 21, Spectral Kev: 8.** Kev is a delight (the eras of Sister Lori, "Pre-Fenway and Post-Fenway"). The EMF meter scene is funny. Then the infrared video reveal, a woman's hand with rings lifting the trumpet, is a great hook. Good chapter ender: "You're getting a thank-you, Kev."

**Ch 22, Evidence: 7.** The aconitine result and Brannock's reasoning are solid, but a lot of procedure again. The moment where Archie repeats his statement about Ivy's scarf, even though he knows it will hurt her, is good moral weight. "Holy Houdini" is cute.

**Ch 23, The Wrong Woman: 7.** Ivy's arrest is sad, and Teddy's "The numbers are going to be insane" is peak sleaze. "Don't help me" repeating works. Margaret Okafor is a fun cameo. A bit short on payoff.

**Ch 24, Family Meeting: 7.** Lenny asking "Who touched it, after you?" is the smartest line in the book. "Hollis Tibbetts taught half the State Police how to place a comma. **Ch 25, Owls Head: 7.** The airport scene with Teddy and the napkin contract is clever and gives Archie a real sacrifice. I like that Archie trades the Glass Box. But the dialog is a lot of exposition and I don't know what the Glass Box *is*, so the sacrifice didn't land as much as it should.

**Ch 26, Forty Seconds: 6.** *Almost closed the app.* The video review with stopwatches, "twenty-three inches," and walking the table for timing was the most homework-feeling chapter. The deduction (forty seconds, five people) is clever, but it took a lot of pages. I just wanted someone to say "Hollis."

**Ch 27, Narrowed Down: 7.** Tess in the rain, furious: "That's what calling me is for, Archie." Great emotional beat. Brannock's summary of the case against Vivienne is a decent scare. It's mostly a recap, though.

**Ch 28, Paint Day: 8.** Community warmth. Fifty-one people painting. The sunlight when the drapes come down. The glove collection (with Hollis dropping hers in the box, which was an eyebrow-raise). And June's oath on the scaffold: "I know what a secret is. I kept one from you for two months." Lovely.

**Ch 29, The Method: 8.** Father-daughter magic lesson. "It's a coat hanger." "It's a very good coat hanger." June looking at him the whole eleven seconds, "It was perfect," and Archie walking out because he can't speak. Ugh. **Cried a little.**

**Ch 30, Visiting Hours: 8.** Ivy explains how the act worked: "Take my hand, baby." The Trumpet Hiatus from Kev pays off here. "The man in Maine." The hatbox is a great mystery engine.

**Ch 31, Dress Rehearsal: 5.** *Closed the app here; came back the next day.* A whole chapter of rehearsal quirks (Dale's tickets, the hinge scream, the fire-escape door, cue book, "Which one's the blue button?"). It's cute and I get that it's planting things, but nothing is pulling me forward for long stretches. The "Pru is back at nine" ticking clock isn't strong enough.

**Ch 32, The Hatbox: 9.** **STAYED UP TILL 2 A.M.** Decoding the notebooks with June at the kitchen table. "She moved everything over one. Every relation. One place." Then the Thursday entries, "It's warm here — check," and the plain line: *God forgive me.* I figured out where it was going and still gasped. When the "H. Langley" card connects, my stomach dropped.

**Ch 33, Blueberry or Apple: 9.** Bev at 5 a.m.: "Langley? That's Hollis." "Blueberry or apple." Stunning use of the pie as a coping device. Elwin saying "Morning, Archie" at the end got me. **Stayed up.**

**Ch 34, The Trumpet Woman: 8.** Tess POV! The filing cabinet by complainant name, her father's block capitals, Walt: "You made me mail that letter... Two stamps. **Ch 35, Pudding Lane: 7.** Emotionally strong: Hollis in the garden, "How did you know?" "Deb and her cards." But I got *really* annoyed that Archie takes the envelope and lets a murderer run the lights. And that cryptic "Intermission. Please." text. I yelled at my phone. (More on this in #9.)

**Ch 36, The Great Fairweather: 9.** The Silver Moon opening, the linking rings, Carol and the queen of hearts with the watch taped to it (CRYING), and **Pickle: "Seven of hearts. And my name's Lionel."** I screamed in the break room. Then the blue cue doesn't come. Lovely.

**Ch 37, Intermission: 10.** **STAYED UP.** "Ivy Crane didn't do it. You know that. — H." Archie's choice between the show and Hollis is the heart of the book. June: "Go." "I'm still mad at you." That is a daughter. That is a *good* daughter. Chills.

**Ch 38, The Bell Buoy: 10.** The skiff, "Do you know what she said to me? *Honey, where's the ladies' room?*" is devastating. **Ch 39, Harbor Blanket: 9.** The town on the wharf, Hollis handing over the key ring ("Somebody has to run the lights"), Bev with the cider pitcher, Archie in one sock. A tiny bit melodramatic ("Seven minutes. Don't tell June."), but I was fully along for the ride.

**Ch 43, The Memorial: 9.** Tess is *furious* ("That's not what you risked"), then she gives him the watch back. "That's a *lift*." "Don't tell anyone." I am screaming. It's the whole romance in four lines. A tiny bit overlong on the lecture.

**Ch 44, Sparrow: 10.** The trunk opens. "SPARROW — You remembered. That's the whole trick." Then the Key West postcard, March 1996, "Still working on the trick. Don't let your mother sell the house." GUS IS ALIVE. "There's a man there I need to see about a boat." I put my phone down and said "no way" out loud to an empty room.

**Average engagement:** about 7.8. First half is 7s with peaks. The last 12 chapters average about 9.

## 2. Archie and Tess

**Did I fall for Archie?** Yes, though it took a while. At first he's a bit of a show-off who talks too much (his rambling theories in Chs 14, 18 and 20 can be exhausting). What won me over:
- his relationship with June ("There wasn't a rest of it. He stood up... 'Okay,' he said.")
- "I won't make fun of you" to Shannon
- Sea View. He does magic for people who won't remember it. That's the whole man.
- the cloak folded by instinct on the boat
- "I don't have a trick. I've just got me."

He also does genuinely dumb things (Galley accusation, airport deal, the envelope), and the book knows it and calls him on it, which makes me forgive him.

**Did I ship them?** Absolutely. Tess is the best love interest I've read in a cozy: competent, sharp, funny when she doesn't want to be. The trick-for-trick banter is what I'd text my friends about ("That's twice." "You're keeping count." "I'm a cop. I keep everything.").

**Is the slow burn hot enough for book 1?** Almost. The sparks are really good (Ch 4, 11, 14, 20, 43) but there's a seven-chapter gap (Ch 27 to Ch 34) where they basically aren't in a scene together, aside from a silent pass in a jail corridor. And Tess is angry at him in most of their scenes. For me, a swoony slow burn needs one more *warm* scene before Ch 43, something cooperative where they like each other and know it. The watch scene almost makes up for it by itself, because the payoff is big, but it's a little earned-on-credit.

**Single moment to screenshot:** Ch 43. Tess buckling Gus's watch on his wrist: *"You lifted it." "I took it." ... "Sergeant Calloway. That's a lift."* ... *"Don't tell anyone."* Her fingers lingering on his pulse. I would send this to six people with ten crying emojis. (Runner-up: Tess pressing her hand to her mouth, laughing, in Ch 11.)

## 4. Mystery

**When did I suspect the killer?** Honest answer: **Chapter 2**, at "Hollis says hers are a foot taller than these, by the way. The deer won't touch it." I told myself it was just garden talk. By **Chapter 7** (black stage gloves, lights the candles, slides the horn) I was 60% sure. **Chapter 13** ("Aunt Hollis," Rosie's godmother) pushed me to 75%. **Chapter 17** ("that woman was a vulture") made me 90% sure. **Chapter 24**, when Vivienne laughs "She isn't poisoning anybody," I wrote *HOLLIS* in my notes app. **Chapter 28**, when Hollis hands over her gloves, I knew.

**Surprised?** Not by *who*. I was surprised by the **why**: Peter, Danny, the quarry, the 41 Thursdays, the "man in Maine." That really got me and it's the emotional height of the book. The hatbox/notebook reveal was the strongest piece of mystery writing here.

**Did anything feel fake or confusing?**
- The book works very hard on red herrings that I didn't buy for a second: Warren (too sympathetic, and his alibi is dull), Kev (a spy? no), Teddy (explained away in a single scene), and Ivy (she wiped the horn, but Archie literally says it's a "reset"). The only real misdirect is Ivy, and that's more of a "will they convict her" than a "who did it."
- Brannock saying Hollis has "no connection to the victim we can find" in Ch 27 is hard to believe when she's Rosie's godmother and Shannon's mentor. Also, she wrote the original letter to the production company and got Lorelei to town. A cop would find that in a day.
- The aconite timing: it was applied during the forty seconds. But in Ch 9/16/22 the "after 7:35" window kept getting re-argued, which made me feel I was reading the same logic loop three times.
- **The Sparrow explanation** is a little thin. The idea that Gus said it to a reporter in 1988 and Ivy found it in the *Packet* is dropped by June in one line in Ch 44. For a detail that blew Archie's mind in Ch 8, I wanted a better reveal of how Lorelei got it.
- **The Glass Box** is referenced about six times (Chs 25, 26, 28) and I still have no idea what it is. That's fine as a tease for later, but the "four people know" stakes felt vague.
- **Ch 35/37 logic:** Tess had a warrant, plain-clothes cops in the hall, and the booth watched. Yet the balcony fire door, which the *fire marshal had Hollis unlock*, wasn't covered? And Archie sending "Intermission. Please." when Tess was already planning to arrest her at intermission? It's tangled enough that I felt the plot was being pushed.

## 7. Prose

I loved the voice overall: dry, warm, jokey, specific. Great lines:
- "He had built every illusion Archie had ever performed... and he had never once said a word of praise about any of them, because, as he'd once explained, they were hinges. You didn't praise a hinge. You oiled it."
- "Nobody watched his hands. That was the whole point of the face."
- "She didn't look sorry."

**What felt slow, overwritten, repetitive or generic:**

1. **Repeated tics.** Counting these made me twitch:
   - "He felt the grin arrive before..." (Chs 1, 10, 36, 44 and more)
   - "her mother's level eyes" (June, about six times)
   - "pushed straight through it" (Shannon, then June; "pushed straight through the crack")
   - "Merrow Light swept its green flash... every six seconds" (probably 15 times)
   - "the bell buoy, ringing slow and irregular" (nearly every chapter)
   - "in all four sweaters"
   - "looked at him over her glasses"
   - "the whole room in a second and a half" (Tess's cop-glance, used at least four times)
   
   Each of them works the first time and wears out by the fifth.

2. **Over-described staging.** Examples: Ch 7 ("a brass hand bell, a tambourine, a child's chalk slate in a wooden frame, and a long cone of dull silver aluminum with three bands of pale green paint around it"), Ch 25's airport, Ch 17's front room of dress forms, and Ch 31's run of rehearsal cues.

3. **Recap-heavy dialogue.** Brannock's speeches in Chs 22 and 27 are essentially "here's the evidence." Tess's rain-soaked line "I watched twenty minutes of a tablecloth" is the best of them, and the rest could be half the length.

4. **Speechy emotional beats.** Hollis's Ch 42 confession, and Tess's Ch 43 lecture, each go on a beat too long. People in the book sometimes speak in perfectly shaped paragraphs.

5. **Generic Maine texture.** "Ayuh" is used a *lot*, and the diner-regulars-in-a-corner trope is familiar. It's charming, but I could feel the set dressing.

6. **Overworked motif.** "Lining out, shoulders in, thirds" is a beautiful detail in Ch 2, 38, 40 and 44. It does not need to be quoted in full each time.

## 8. Star rating and TikTok review

**4.5 stars** (I'd post it as 5 on TikTok because the last third earns it).

> **"Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather" is a cozy mystery with a magician, a medium who dies on camera, a lobster named Harold, and the best father-daughter relationship I've read in years. It starts a little slow in the middle, but then the last 12 chapters destroyed me. I cried in my car at work. There's a cat. There's a slow-burn cop who steals his WATCH to save it. There's a pie. There's an ENDING with a Key West postcard that made me say "NO WAY" out loud. If you like Richard Osman, Knives Out, and Gilmore Girls, pick this up. Book 2 can't come fast enough.**

## 9. The 10 changes that would most improve it for readers like me

1. **Trim the middle by 10 to 15 percent (Chs 16, 18, 22, 26, 31).** These are the app-closing spots. Cut half the auction listing detail in Ch 18, cut Brannock's recap speeches in Chs 22 and 27, and drastically shorten Ch 26's stopwatch walkthrough. Ch 31 (dress rehearsal) should either gain a real engine (e.g., Tess shows up, or Rosie's "warm" line lands with more menace) or be merged into the start of Ch 32.

2. **Add one warm, charged Archie/Tess scene between Ch 27 and Ch 34.** Brannock banned contact, so make it forbidden: a late-night call, a secret text exchange, a "wrong number" that isn't. Right now the gap makes the Ch 43 payoff earned-on-credit. They need to *like* each other in the middle, not just clash.

3. **Seed the "Danny's letter / Don't tell anyone" callback more clearly.** In Ch 34, when Walt mentions the letter, add a line or two about what Archie wrote back. Then Ch 43's "Don't tell anyone" lands as a fireworks moment for readers who spot it, instead of a vague "something he'd known once." Right now I only got the chills because I guessed.

4. **Reduce the neon signs pointing to Hollis (Chs 2, 7, 11, 13, 17, 24, 28).** I counted at least seven. You don't need all of them. Pick three: the monkshood line, the gloves, and the "vulture" speech. Drop the gloves-in-the-box wink in Ch 28 or turn it into a real fake-out. Or lean into it: let readers know and make the dread the point.

5. **Make Brannock and the cops less oblivious about Hollis (Ch 27).** "Retired schoolteacher with no connection to the victim we can find" is unbelievable when Hollis wrote the invitation to the production company and is Shannon's godmother and mentor. Have Brannock raise her as a suspect early, so the reader doesn't lose trust in the cops.

6. **Fix the Ch 35 logic.** Archie handing the poisoner a free pass to run the lights is the one choice I couldn't forgive in the moment. Give him a reason that isn't "she asked nicely": maybe Tess is unreachable until the show, maybe Hollis has Danny's photograph, maybe he *tries* to call Tess and gets Brannock. Also make the "Intermission. Please." text less coy ("Hollis. Booth.") or drop Kayla's booth watch so Hollis' escape isn't a plot hole. And someone on Tess's team should be on the fire-escape door.

7. **Trim Ch 34's recitation of the file.** We already read the same facts in Ch 32 via the notebooks. Keep the Tess backstory (her father writing everything down, Danny as Puck, Walt's two stamps) and cut the long extract. Better: put Ch 34 *before* Ch 33 so the Langley reveal doesn't happen twice.

8. **Break up the confession in Ch 42 and shorten Tess's lecture in Ch 43.** Put some action or interruption in Hollis's story ("Why didn't you just tell Tess?" "Because you can't debunk a widow" is the best bit; make the rest shorter). In Ch 43 cut the repeat of "you risked Hollis" and go to the watch faster.

9. **Pay off or cut the Glass Box and Marco.** Either let the reader see something about the Glass Box (even a tiny hint) so the Ch 25 sacrifice has weight, or reduce the mentions. And let us meet Marco, even in a line, before his card becomes a major clue.

10. **Run a repeated-phrase pass through the whole manuscript.** Cap each of these at about three uses: "the grin arrived," "her mother's level eyes," "pushed straight through it," "Merrow Light every six seconds," "bell buoy slow and irregular," "four sweaters," "over her glasses," "the whole room in a second and a half." The motifs that matter ("lining out, shoulders in, thirds"; "nobody watched his hands") should be the ones we remember, and they get drowned by the filler ones.

*Bonus change:* Give Ivy a mid-book moment (a letter, a call, a note passed through Okafor) so she doesn't disappear from Ch 23 until Ch 30. She's the book's real wrongfully-accused heart.
