# Editorial Letter — Revision 1

From the showrunner, after: three blind readers (`reader-marjorie.md`, `reader-danielle.md`, `reader-raymond.md`), the Story Grid audit (`storygrid-audit-draft1.md`), the craft audit (`craft-audit-draft1.md`), and the continuity pass (`continuity-pass-1.md`). Where reports conflict, **this letter governs.**

## Where we are
Readers: 4 / 4 / 4.5 stars; catharsis 9–9.5; would buy Book 2: 8–9/10. Story Grid: A−. The engine works: the séance, the click, the climax run, June, Tess, the town. **Three problems stand between this and a book people hand to their friends:**

1. **All three readers solved the killer early** (Ch 2, Ch 7, Ch 17; certainty by Ch 24–32). The clues are fair but *stacked* and *spotlit*, and the alternatives aren't live.
2. **The middle sags** (Ch 18, 22–27, 31): the method is explained ~5 times and the 7:35–8:00 window reconstructed ~8 times; recap openings; briefing chapters.
3. **Prose habits** shared by every hand (manner clauses, "X stopped." one-liners, "for a long moment", epigram chapter-endings, narrator diagnosing feelings, repeated images), plus a few Story Grid gaps in Act III.

**Length target after revision: 80,000–82,000 words** (from 89,400). Cut repetition, recap, and explanation; never cut a set-piece's human interaction or the companionship that readers loved (see each report's "do not cut" notes: poker, Lionel, Carol's card, "You came back," "You're late," Bev's pie, the method in Ch 29, the click).

## A. Guessability — lower it without cheating (TOP PRIORITY)

Goal: a savvy reader may *wonder* about Hollis; she should not be *sure* before Ch 32. Every clue remains on the page, once, in ordinary use; we remove stacking, spotlighting, and adjacency, and we give the reader live alternatives in the late middle.

The Langley line gets *no* more weight than any other; June's name interrupts. No arrow on it.

Hollis lights candles (one clause), sets glasses, slides the horn at Teddy's call (one clause), among four other people's business. ## C. Prose — rules for every reviser

31. **Manner clauses** ("the way X…", "as if/as though", "like someone who…"): ≤1 per 500 words; ≤1 per scene; never two in a paragraph. Ch 34–44 need the hardest sweep.
32. **"X stopped." / "He stopped breathing." one-line punches**: max one per chapter.
33. **"for a long moment" / "looked at him for a long moment"**: max 5 in the book.
34. **"very still / very quiet / very steady / very X and very Y"**: cut by two-thirds.
35. **"something went across her face" / "his face did something"**: max 4 in the book.
36. **Body-before-mind reflex** ("He hadn't decided to," "his hands were already," "before he could stop it," "He found that he…"): max 1 per act.
37. **Chapter endings:** no more than a third of chapters may end on an epigram or a harbor image (bell buoy / Merrow Light). End on action, decision, a line of dialogue, or a plain fact. The bell buoy may close at most five chapters in the whole book.
38. **Repeated images / jokes to cap:** "whole room in a second and a half" (keep once, Ch 4); presidential-administration dating (keep two); "struck match" (keep once, Ch 44); "pushed straight through it" (once); "meant it so much that she looked faintly alarmed" (once, Ch 5); "1994" as punchline (once); "folded in half and in half again" (once); "Hear me out" (twice); "four sweaters" (twice); Merrow Light "every six seconds" (twice).
39. **Narrator doesn't diagnose Archie's wound** or state the theme. Cut any sentence that explains what an action meant after the action showed it.
40. **Wit is owned:** Tess is deadpan, not quippy (restore in Ch 11, 20). Exit lines: not everybody gets the last crafted word — let some scenes end on a plain answer or someone continuing their task.
41. **Lifts:** only Ch 4, Ch 20, and Tess's in Ch 43. Remove the lift in Ch 21 (Kev's selfie handle) — make it a request.
42. **American English** (fix "rather wonderful" and any Britishisms).
