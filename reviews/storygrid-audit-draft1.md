## 0. Foolscap (what the prose actually delivers)

| Field | As executed |
|---|---|
| External genre | Crime › Mystery › cozy / amateur sleuth (master detective subtype: an expert in deception reads a "locked-room" séance) |
| Internal genre | Worldview › Education. Archie moves from *performance* (giving people he loves the trick) to *presence* (letting them see the method; coming back) |
| Global external value | Justice. Ladder as played: hidden injustice → murder disguised as natural death → suspicion on the protagonist → **injustice wearing justice's authority** (Ivy jailed on true testimony) → truth → justice with one human cost |
| Global internal value | Performance ↔ presence. Negation of the negation: absence disguised as presence (Lorelei's dead who "speak"; Archie onstage for June but never backstage) |
| POV | Close third on Archie, 43 chapters; Tess, Ch 34 only |

| Controlling idea (as executed) | Justice is restored, at a human price, when the truth kept on paper outweighs the performance. Love becomes real when we stop performing for the people we love and let them see how it's done. |

### Acts: the Five Commandments in the prose

**Beginning Hook (Ch 1–9; 23%)**
- **Inciting incident:** Ch 1. Lorelei: "He says you still haven't opened his trunk." Archie raises the wager and accepts the dark. This is a causal inciting incident: the medium invades his private wound, and he says yes.
- **Progressive complications:** Shannon's grief turns the game into a promise (Ch 3). Tess's warning (Ch 4). June's "It's mine" (Ch 5). "We're the same" and "you don't know how to stay" (Ch 6). He loses control of the controls (Ch 7).
- **Turning point:** Ch 8, revelatory. The voice chokes, the trumpet falls, "I can't feel my mouth." That is also the global inciting crime, at 20.9%.
- **Crisis:** Ch 9. Tell Tess he saw Ivy wipe the horn (costing a grieving daughter), or stay silent (a lie). Best bad choice, cleanly staged ("It would cost her … The other thing was a lie").
- **Climax:** he tells.
- **Resolution:** "The most interesting man in the building to the Maine State Police."
- **Verdict:** a textbook Beginning Hook. The crime lands late for a mystery (about 21%), but seven chapters of rising, value-bearing complications earn it. The wager is the real hook, and Coyne would accept it as such.

**Middle Build (Ch 10–33; 54%)**
- **Inciting incident:** Ch 10, "I'm going to find out," then Tess's call. Ch 15 adds the aconite confirmation as the formal homicide.
- **Progressive complications:** the washed thermos (14); person of interest (16); the Warren disaster (19–20); Kev's footage (21).
- **Midpoint shift (Ch 22, 51%):** vindication becomes tyranny, and his truth jails Ivy. An exact Coyne-style "point of no return," landing on a real best bad choice.
- **MB2:** the Glass Box trade (25); the B-roll clears Ivy but narrows onto his mother (26–27); Tess cut off (27); Ivy charged with murder (29); the ledgers (30).
- **Turning point:** Ch 32–33, revelatory. "God forgive me" and "Langley? That's Hollis." Archie and the reader arrive together, at 75–77%.
- **Crisis:** Ch 33. Take it to Tess now, or face Hollis first because he owes her.
- **Climax:** Ch 33/35. Hollis first. In the garden he takes the envelope and grants her the show.
- **Resolution:** "Intermission. Please."
- **Verdict:** strong spine, correctly placed midpoint, an earned click. The sags are the connective chapters (11, 13, 15, 17, 27, 31), plus a logic hole in the Warren theory (Fix #8) and one continuity error (Ch 20).

**Ending Payoff (Ch 34–44; 23%)**
- **Inciting incident:** Ch 34. Tess independently finds Walt's file and gets the warrant. Then Ch 36: the blue cue never comes and the booth is dark.
- **Progressive complications:** the note; the jar and gloves found; "You gave me a riddle"; June discovers he hid it.
- **Turning point:** Ch 37. Ozzie: the skiff is heading "toward the bell."
- **Crisis:** Ch 37. Stay and keep his word to June, or go after Hollis. Irreconcilable goods, explicitly staged ("He couldn't do both. There wasn't a trick for it").
- **Climax:** Ch 37–38. He accepts June's "Go," lets her see him choose, and at the ledge talks to Hollis with no trick and goes into the water. Ch 40 completes the internal climax: he comes back into the chair.
- **Resolution:** Ch 39 and 41–44. Confession, restoration, the speech in praise, Tess, the trunk.
- **Verdict:** emotionally the best part of the book. Three structural softnesses:
  - June removes Archie's dilemma before he has fully chosen (Fix #3).
  - The plunge is written as unchosen (Fix #4).
  - The reveal never shows the murder method or explains the flight (Fixes #1 and #2).

## 6. Controlling idea

**What the north star intends:** "The trick is for strangers. The method is for the people you love." Justice depends on someone finally looking backstage.

**What the draft actually says:**
- *Internal:* Love becomes real when we stop giving the people we love the show and let them see how it's done, and come back. This is fully dramatized and fully proven. Archie shows June the hoop (Ch 29), fails and hides Hollis from her (Ch 37), then lets her see him choose (Ch 37), and returns through the trap into the chair (Ch 40). The internal climax *is* the external climax's coda, which is exactly the Story Grid ideal.
- *External:* Justice prevails, at a human price, when the truth kept on paper outweighs the performance. That's not quite "someone finally looks backstage." In the prose, nobody looks at the stagehand. Archie admits "I never saw it." The case breaks on records:
  - Lorelei's ledger ("God forgive me")
  - Walt's yellow legal pads
  - Deb's library card
  - Marco's B-roll
  - June's calendar arithmetic
  - Hollis's sealed letter

  That's a coherent and better idea than the plan, and it rhymes with the theme: a written method outlives the trick.
- *Counter-idea, fully voiced:* Hollis's "You can't debunk a widow." Exposure doesn't stop the trick; only killing the trickster does. The book refutes it without a speech. Lorelei *did* remember Peter and *did* stop; mercy, not murder, would have been true.

**Does the climax prove it?** Mostly yes. At the ledge Archie wins with no trick and a written truth (Lorelei's own ledger line), which proves both halves at once. What it doesn't prove is the north star's "looking backstage" clause, because the stagehand's method is never shown. Fix #1 (Hollis gives Archie the method) closes that gap and lands the thesis exactly: a trick for strangers (the murder), a method given to the one person she loves in the room (Archie).

## 7. Verdict

**Would Shawn Coyne say this book works?** Yes. This is a working book in draft, not a draft hoping to become one.

It has:
- a global inciting crime that is impossible, fair and thematic
- a ladder of progressive complications that genuinely costs the protagonist more at each rung
- a midpoint at 51% that inverts the value onto a real best bad choice
- a shared click at 75%
- a global crisis staged as irreconcilable goods
- a climax that pays off the external and internal genres in the same movement, plus a rhyme (the séance chair and the Empty Chair) that most published mysteries never find

Thirty-two of 44 chapters are complete dramatic units. The fixes below are surgical, not structural. None requires moving the spine.

What keeps it from an unqualified A is concentrated in the last 10%: the method is never revealed, the flight is never motivated, and the climactic act is written as reflex. In the Middle Build, a handful of connective chapters (11, 13, 17, 27, 31) don't turn on a choice, and there is one logic hole (the Warren theory) and one continuity error (Ch 20).

### The 15 highest-leverage fixes (ranked)

1. **Ch 42: Hollis gives Archie the method.** Add 300–400 words in which she shows him how it was done. She placed the tall pillar candle a hand's width from the polished horn, knowing from thirty years of lighting that the handheld lens would flare. The flare makes the cameraman swing to the window, and the producer then asks the stagehand to "slide the horn." Inside those forty seconds, with a dab of paste on a gloved fingertip, she pressed it into the rolled seam of the small end; the black glove went back into the booth's shoebox among a dozen identical pairs. Archie, a magician, recognizes it as a perfect routine: forced misdirection plus an invisible assistant. This completes the "exposure of the criminal" with method, makes "best trick I never saw" literal, and enacts the north-star theme (the method given to the one person she loves).

2. **Ch 36 + Ch 42: Motivate the flight and the bell buoy.**
   - *Ch 36:* Archie sees Tess arrive and Kayla take a phone call and look up at the booth. The booth light dips for a beat. Make it Archie's crisis too: signal Tess, or keep his word and play the Sparrows. He plays.
   - *Ch 42:* Hollis says she knew from the booth that they'd been in her shed. She would not be taken in front of Rosie and Liam, and she "only meant to go and sit where Peter was." Still never say she jumped.

   This repairs her broken promise, which currently has no cause on the page.

3. **Ch 37: Make the crisis Archie's before June answers it.** Have him begin to choose wrong-for-the-right-reasons: "I'm staying. Tess is right. I promised you." June refuses the sacrifice: "Don't you dare stay *for* me." His dilemma becomes keeping a promise (performing fatherhood) or honoring what his daughter actually asks. His "Okay" becomes the global climax of the internal genre rather than a gift received.

4. **Ch 38: Make the plunge a decision.** Keep the cloak-fold involuntary; it's the book's best plant for Ch 44. Replace "He didn't decide anything" with a beat of will. Tess: "Archie — don't." He meets her eyes and chooses: "Line. Around my waist." Story Grid requires the global climax to be an enacted choice.

5. **Swap Ch 41 and Ch 42 (hospital first, then the Galley).** The Galley becomes one pursuit: Archie carrying Hollis's bequests.
   - Her apology to Ivy, promised in Ch 42 and currently never delivered.
   - The godmother request to Shannon, with Vivienne.
   - The posters for Carol.

   Adjust the exits so Ch 42 ends with Tess driving him back and Ch 41 ends on "Correct answer." This fixes the itinerary chapter and two loose ends.

6. **Ch 35: Stage the hero at the mercy of the villain.** Hollis has already offered tea ("Did you want tea?"). Have her offer it again, now poured, with the jar on the shed shelf thirty feet away. Archie knows exactly what she is and drinks it from her hand, the tea truce of Ch 7 inverted, as a choice of trust. That gives the genre its missing obligatory scene. It also gives her request for "one more night" its moral weight: he has just put his life in her hands.

7. **Pay off "Sparrow."** Vivienne demands in Ch 10, "Then show it to me," and Archie's personal motive rests on it. On the page it's never shown, yet June asserts the *Packet* source in Ch 44. Plant it in Ch 12: Archie glimpses a photocopied 1988 *Packet* clipping behind the FAIRWEATHER, A. tab. Pay it off in Ch 41/42: Ivy hands Vivienne the clipping (Gus quoted: "That sparrow's been at the jar again"). Vivienne's thread closes, and June's Ch 44 argument becomes earned rather than asserted.

8. **Ch 18–20: Repair the Warren theory's logic.** As written, Archie's own Ch 16 argument (the horn was clean at 7:35) rules out Warren's Friday key, yet he builds the Galley accusation on it. Make him wrong but smart. His theory is a *duplicate trumpet*: Kev's Ch 21 lore of "photos from a Grange in Vermont with the same dent" can be moved earlier or echoed. Warren swaps it during his 7:47 "stomach" exit. Then Tess's rebuttal (Dale watched the stair door; Warren never neared the table; the frames) dismantles a clever theory rather than a careless one.

9. **Ch 27: Give Archie the decisive choice.** When Brannock says "And I've got your mother," Archie can save her by pointing at the others in the forty seconds. He refuses: "Not Hollis. Not anybody. I did that once this week." The chapter becomes active, the Warren lesson is shown as learned, and the irony detonates in Ch 33.

10. **Ch 17: Make supper a scene.** Hollis asks, "What do they think happened?" Archie breaks Tess's confidence and tells her: the numb mouth, the trumpet, aconite. That's a choice (comfort over discretion), it's re-read irony, and it sets up Ch 42's "I heard Tess say *wolfsbane* … and was sick in the sink," which can be rewritten to "I heard it from you, at my own table."

11. **Ch 13: Give Archie the crisis.** Liam asks, in front of his mother, "Is my dad really talking to Mom?" Archie must choose between the comforting performance and the truth. He chooses neither: "That's your mom's to tell you." That's presence over performance, and the reason Shannon volunteers the bruises and the $1,050 at the door.

12. **Continuity and fair-play corrections:**
    - *Ch 20:* "Warren walked into my command post at four o'clock on Sunday" is impossible. Tess was at the autopsy in Augusta at four (Ch 11, Ch 14), and the command post opened Monday (Ch 15–16). Change it to "Monday, an hour after you left," and "I've known since yesterday."
    - *Ch 10:* "everybody in Merrow under about sixty" becomes "under forty." The unit dates from after 2003 (Ch 42), and the line currently blurs a clue to Hollis's motive.
    - *Ch 41 vs. Ch 43:* Ivy's release is at 8 a.m. in one and 10 a.m. in the other. Pick one.

13. **Ch 23: Cut the reused statement recap.** Delete the opening two paragraphs that re-narrate the formal statement the reader watched in Ch 22, and open on "At five thirty-one he was running." Keep the call to Okafor as the chapter's climax.

14. **Ch 31: Convert the rehearsal into a scene.** On the booth stairs, let Rosie's line register as strange: how does Hollis know what Tommy "said"? Shannon told her. Archie chooses not to intrude on a grieving woman's tenderness and asks about the cue instead. One paragraph gives the chapter a crisis and makes the clue more painful on re-read.

15. **Ch 11 and Ch 26: Put the choices after the turns.**
    - *Ch 11:* At the umbrella stand, a sentence of deliberation. If he calls Tess, she bags it and he never learns whose it is, so he picks it up knowing the cost.
    - *Ch 26:* Move the dilemma after the forty seconds: show Tess footage that puts his own mother two feet from the horn. He does, which makes Ch 27's "your mother" land on a choice he made knowingly.

Fund them by trimming Ch 34 (about 500 words of Tess's interior recap), Ch 42 (the backstory the reader already holds from Ch 32 and Ch 34), and Ch 24 and Ch 27 (re-narration of frames already seen).
