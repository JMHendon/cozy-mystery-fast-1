# Red-Team Review: *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather*

## Preface: what holds

These parts already work and the fixes below are built to keep them. The four clocks are kept honestly separate. The toot is a superb double-edged device. Ivy's wipe works as a performer's "reset" that looks exactly like guilt. The Hollis/Langley name split protects the killer without any cheating. "The water's never warm here" (Ch 31) is a model late clue. The rhyme between the séance chair and the Empty Chair is the book.

The faults sit in the evidence logic around the window, in how the police are shown, and in what Hollis expected to happen. Those three areas are where an ME, a Maine prosecutor and a seasoned cozy reader will each push.

---

## CRITICAL (would break the book)

### C1. The window that "clears" Ivy never closes. Ivy had a free hand beside the trumpet in the dark, and she killed the cameras.

The spine's break-point (§2) says: *"the B-roll (Ivy never touched the table after 7:45; so the toot window 7:36–8:00 excludes her if the toot was real)."* That is false three times over, using the plan's own facts.

- **7:44 is inside the window.** Spine §1: *"7:44 (B-roll) Ivy slips the telescoping touch-rod under the tablecloth's overhang at her place."* The toot was at 7:35, so Ivy was at the table nine minutes after it. Ch 26's *"with the toot at 7:35, Ivy never touched the trumpet in the window"* is a non-sequitur unless the B-roll is explicitly shown to keep her hands away from the centre.
- **Ivy returns to the table at 7:58 and sits beside the trumpet in total darkness.** Spine: *"Ivy released Lorelei's left hand at ~8:07 and freed her own right."* From 8:07 there are two free hands in the circle, and one of them belongs to the plant, who is sitting next to the weapon.
- **At 8:14 Ivy kills the infrared cameras** (*"the IR cameras' illuminators die [Ivy's pocket remote]"*). That is three minutes before the trumpet rises at 8:17.

A competent Brannock builds the whole AG case on exactly this: a free hand in the dark, inches from the trumpet, plus deliberately blinded cameras in the three minutes before the victim put it to her mouth. That is far stronger than "she set out the props at 7:20", which is the argument the plan actually gives him (Ch 27). Nothing in the plan answers it. The B-roll can't, because it was shot in the light before 8:00.

So Archie's joy in Ch 26 ("Ivy's cleared — that's what matters") is a logic error that attentive readers will catch. The premise lines are also untrue as written: spine §1, *"the only hand that was free in that circle belonged to the victim"*, and north-star, *"The only person in that circle who let go was the victim."*

**Fix:** see F1. Kev's pocket camera has to show the trumpet sitting untouched on the table from lights-out until Lorelei's ringed hand lifts it. The B-roll has to hold the table continuously, except for the 40 seconds.

### C2. The footage record is underspecified. As written, the police are negligent, and the killer is saved by a coincidence.

- **Teddy at 7:51:** *"Hollis — sorry — can you slide the horn left, it's fighting the candle."* Teddy can only see the horn "fighting the candle" on a live feed from the official IR cameras, so those cameras were live at 7:51. If they were also recording, the police have had Hollis's 40 seconds from two locked-off angles since Sunday, and the B-roll is pointless. The plan never says.
- **Ch 24:** *"the police only got the official cameras."* The death happened at a TV taping, and the whole room watched Marco carry a handheld camera. Any homicide team demands every card on day one. Brannock not asking is negligence; Brannock asking and accepting a no is credulity. The plan has to show that he asked and that Teddy lied (the card says Teddy "withholds"; it never says he lied to the police).
- **The cutaway (7:51:10–7:51:50) lines up to the second with Hollis touching the mouthpiece.** The killer is shielded by a cameraman's pan she didn't control. That kind of luck makes an editor wince.

**Fix:** F2. Make the candle glare cause both the pan and Teddy's request, and make Hollis the person who placed the candle.

### C3. The plan never says what Hollis expected to happen next, and every possible answer makes her frame her friends.

She talked Archie out of cancelling (card: *"Don't cancel her. Beat her."*). She used a poison that grows in Gus's own garden, at a séance where Archie had a $2M public feud with the victim and served her tea on camera. She put it on a prop that Ivy sets out and handles.

If Hollis expected a murder investigation, she knowingly built a frame around Archie (and, by the trumpet, around Ivy). Then the card lines *"She never planned for Archie… She never meant anyone else to be blamed"* are incoherent, and the "killer is human, never a monster" non-negotiable fails at the confession. Ch 39 can't survive the obvious question, "You knew they'd look at him?"

The mechanics also put other people at risk:
- Spine: *"with a gloved fingertip smears the salve… She carries on setting water glasses."* The same contaminated cotton fingertip then handles the rims of the sitters' water glasses. Either a sitter is poisoned, or the police find aconitine on a glass Hollis set. Spine §4 says *"water bottle negative"*, which is also inconsistent with "water glasses".
- Cotton gloves wick an ointment straight through to the skin. Hollis would have a numb fingertip, and gardeners know that.
- From 7:51 to 8:17 anyone might pick up the horn: Kev (who photographed the table at 7:46), or Archie the ham. Hollis "grew to love him" and left a loaded mouthpiece on the table he had just put his lips to, with no failsafe.

**Fix:** F3. She expected a death that would be ruled natural. That one decision explains the trumpet, the jar she kept, why she didn't clean up afterwards, and her horror when Archie said "aconite".

### C4. Ivy sits in a cell while Hollis says nothing, and then Archie, June and Bev all say nothing too.

Ivy is arrested Tuesday at 6 p.m. and charged with murder on Thursday. Hollis is silent until Friday night. Archie is certain by 5 a.m. Friday (Ch 33) and leaves Ivy charged with murder for about 16 more hours so that Hollis can *"run Gus's show"* (Ch 35). June did the click with him at 2 a.m. (Ch 32), and her card says she *"wants the truth, not a show"* and is bound for law school, yet she goes to school and then performs in a costume Hollis made while the killer runs her lights. Bev (Ch 33) is told enough to grieve, then opens her diner.

All of this cost lands on Ivy, the person Archie is supposedly fighting for. That is the one thing that turns "mercy as a mistake" into "a man who preferred his show to an innocent woman's freedom", and it drags Hollis's tragedy down with him. The outline frames the choice as *"he chooses mercy over procedure — a mistake with a cost"* (Ch 33), but the cost is borne by a third party who has no say.

**Fix:** F4. Make the delay cost risk to Hollis and to Archie, not Ivy's liberty. Archie holds the written confession. June is kept out, which is his thematic backslide. Bev sets a deadline.

### C5. In a murder of a fraudulent medium, nobody looks at her marks. The police skip victimology.

A poisoned fake medium has one obvious class of enemy: the people she cheated. The plan's police never examine that class.
- **The ledgers:** Lorelei's thirty appointment books, which say *"P. Langley, Merrow"* in plain words (the town she died in), sit *"in a green hatbox in the closet at the inn"* (Ch 30). That is the victim's own suite. They are there through a homicide inquiry and through the search that comes with Ivy's arrest at the inn (Ch 23), and no officer opens them. Checking a dead fraud's client records for the name of the town where she was killed is first-week work.
- **The 2003 complaint:** It names *"Sister Lori Cranmer"*, the victim's legal surname, and it sits in the basement of the department whose chief is assisting the inquiry. Pinkham is 63 and has been in a three-person department for years; on the plan as written he was probably a Merrow officer in 2003. Nobody runs "Cranmer" through local files for six days.
- **How the case breaks for the police:** Tess's route then breaks on Friday because of **Walt's convenient memory** (Ch 34: *"Walt is agitated by the TV news coverage"*). Lorelei's face has been on the news since Sunday. Why does it register on Friday, at the first moment the plot needs it?

The previous red-team named "investigators who had to be negligent" as a critical class. This is that fault.

**Fix:** F5. Ivy hides the hatbox, Walt's file is indexed by complainant, Pinkham arrived in 2010, and Tess's route is driven by her own work, with Walt confirming rather than causing.

## MAJOR (would bother attentive readers and editors)

### M1. Hollis is too guessable: about 55–65% of seasoned cozy readers will name her before Ch 32.

Where she gets fingered, roughly cumulative:

| Ch | Trigger | Cumulative % of savvy readers who suspect her |
|---|---|---|
| 3 → 5 | The killer herself says the murder method aloud (*"lips to the small end"*, spine clue 3). Two chapters later the book's card shows **H.** Langley, and **Hollis is the only H in the cast**. Ch 5 also has Archie read page 112, the page Hollis quoted. | 10–15% |
| 10 | June: *"someone knew she was faking and knew she'd put her mouth on it — that's a short list."* Readers remember who said "lips to the small end" in a crowded diner. | 20% |
| 16 → 17 | **Adjacent chapters.** Ch 16 makes monkshood in Archie's garden an accusation. Ch 17 then gives Hollis's monkshood "cut to the ground and the bed turned over", plus a dead son (*"He'd be thirty-nine"*), *"the sea's greedy here"*, and Lorelei as *"a vulture"*. That is five pointers in one warm supper. The craft rule (*"Judge the cumulative emphasis across neighbouring chapters"*) is broken here. | 45% |
| 24 → 26 | Lenny: *"You never look at the crew."* Two chapters later the B-roll list reduces to Vivienne (series regular, protected), Kev (comic), Shannon (no motive on the page) and Hollis. The list is a one-horse race, and Lenny's *"stage gloves, crew wears black"* spotlights the gloves. | 60% |
| 31 | "The water's never warm here." | 65% |

The worst moment is Ch 17, coming right after Ch 16. Ch 3–5 comes next: an English teacher reciting the murder method, followed by a lone "H." Fixes are in F6. Expected rate with them: about 25–30%, with no cheating.

### M2. Viewpoint honesty: Archie already knows Hollis moved the horn, and the plan pretends the B-roll tells him.

He hears Teddy's call in Ch 7, and Hollis says it in front of him in Ch 11 (*"she moved the horn when Teddy asked"*). So when Lenny asks in Ch 24, *"who touched the horn after you tooted?"*, the honest answer in Archie's head is: Hollis, at Teddy's request, plus Ivy at 7:44. Ch 26 then presents Hollis's slide as something the footage reveals. That breaks non-negotiable 4: *"Preserve what the viewpoint character plainly sees or knows; never make an observable fact vague merely to hide its significance."*

**Fix:** Archie answers in Ch 24, and the family laughs it off (Vivienne: "Hollis? Hollis taught half the State Police to diagram a sentence."). An innocent use and social proof do the misdirection honestly.

### M3. Brannock's "narrowing onto Archie" contradicts the chief of police's own alibi for him.

Ch 26 has June say *"Dad's not on camera during the forty seconds either"*, and Ch 27 has Brannock say *"the one man with a motive isn't on camera."* But spine §1 puts Archie at the back of the hall from 7:45 to 7:58 *with Chief Pinkham*, who also logged Warren in and out. A competent lieutenant can't use Archie's absence from the 40 seconds when a police chief alibis him for it.

There is a second gap. Lorelei's mouthpiece was swabbed, and a saliva DNA mixture would test whether Archie's lips really touched it. That would settle the toot argument, and the plan never mentions DNA.

**Fix (best):** Brannock's theory of Archie should be that the faked toot *is* the poisoning: a sleight-of-hand man palms salve onto the rim while pretending to blow. The 40 seconds then don't matter to him, and the line works: *"Your footage clears Ivy only if your toot was real. If it was, I have your mother, a fan, a widow and a schoolteacher, none with motive or a source. If it wasn't, I have the one man on tape with motive, poison in his garden and the hands to do it."*

Add: the mouthpiece DNA is a low-template mixture after Ivy's wipe and Lorelei's lips, inconclusive for a third contributor, with a lab backlog of weeks (label it as convention). He can also question the custody of the B-roll: a professional camera-fooler hand-delivered the only footage that clears Ivy, so the metadata goes to forensic video for verification.

### M4. Procedural holes in the Ivy phase.

- **Ch 9:** Archie tells Tess that Ivy wiped the mouthpiece with her scarf and pocketed it, and Ivy is *sitting in the same waiting room with it in her coat*. A competent detective at least asks for the scarf there. As planned it waits three days (seized on Tuesday). Fix: Tess asks; Ivy refuses (it's her mother's, and grief); with no crime yet established, Tess can't compel. Brannock gets a warrant on Monday night after the tox result. That shows competence and adds a grain of suspicion against Ivy.
- **Ch 22 to 23 clock:** Ch 22 exits *"the cars go to the inn"* (~2:30 p.m.), but the arrest is at ~6 p.m. (Ch 23, spine), and Archie *"wants to reach Ivy before they do"* and fails over four hours. Fix: Ch 22 exits on Brannock sending for an arrest warrant and holding Archie for a formal recorded statement until after 5. That gives the reason he's late.
- **No re-interview of Hollis.** The trumpet is proven to be the weapon on Tuesday, and Hollis *admitted handling it on Sunday*. The B-roll arrives on Wednesday and shows her as the only person who touched it in the gap. Nobody re-interviews her or collects the gloves. Brannock's line *"a retired schoolteacher"* (Ch 27) skates past the fact that she handled the weapon. Fix: see F5/F7 (Kayla collects every crew glove on Paint Day, Ch 28; Brannock's line names her handling and "no connection to the victim we can find").
- **Charge timing:** Ivy's statement is placed on Thursday (spine), but Dale's call on Wednesday night (Ch 29 exit) already says *"the AG's office is charging Ivy with murder tomorrow."* Move the statement to Wednesday afternoon. Her initial appearance on the tampering and falsifying charges would also be Wednesday (next business day after a Tuesday-night arrest), not folded into Thursday.
- **The AG's case:** It needs a theory of where Ivy got the aconite. She is from out of state, her room and car were searched, and her phone and laptop showed no searches or purchases. That has to be acknowledged in Ch 27 ("several Merrow gardens grow it; she had three days in town"), or the murder charge reads as rushed. Re-centring the case on the dark (C1) makes the charge credible. Kev's footage then knocking out the dark is what forces the case back onto the faked-toot argument.
- **Ch 11:** Tess lets a man who named the poison, served the victim tea, and holds a $2M grudge walk the scene as a consultant on Sunday. It's defensible (an unexplained death, not yet a homicide, and she needs séance expertise to know what to bag), but Brannock should rebuke her for it on Monday. That gives her a real cost and keeps her "never wrong about procedure" ceiling honest.

### M5. Medical and forensic objections, in the order a pharmacologist or ME would raise them.

1. **"makes a vodka tincture, reduces it."** Heating hydrolyses aconitine to benzoylaconine, which is roughly 100–1,000× less toxic. That is exactly how traditional medicine detoxifies aconite root. A pharmacologist will catch "reduced on the stove." Change to: cold maceration for weeks, then evaporation of the alcohol at room temperature (on the radiator, under a cloth).
2. **The vehicle.** Petroleum jelly holds lipophilic alkaloids and releases them slowly. A lanolin or beeswax lip-balm base gives a more plausible fast transfer to the mucosa, and Hollis has both in the costume shop.
3. **Dose.** The lethal dose of aconitine is about 2–5 mg. A fingertip smear of 0.1–0.2 g of salve needs an extract of several per cent, which means tens of grams of dried root, i.e. a whole bed. That is a gift: it explains why the entire border was dug, not a plant or two.
4. **Onset.** Aconite tingles and burns on the lips within a minute or two, not five. Lorelei would also feel a greasy rim. Fix both with existing facts: the **menthol lozenge Ivy gave her at 7:50** (a cooling, numbing mouth she'd expect) and **Archie's raspberry-toot at 7:35** (anything slick on the mouthpiece is "the magician's spit"). The spine's *"she ignores it — cold metal"* is the weakest of the three explanations; use the other two.
5. **Collapse at 8:28, death at 9:40.** This is fine. Aconite causes bradycardia, hypotension and ventricular ectopy progressing to VT/VF, and those arrhythmias are notoriously refractory to cardioversion. Dr. Rao can say so. She is conscious and speaking at 8:29 (correctly not in arrest yet).
6. **Residue after the wipe.** Yes. A silk scarf drawn across the rim doesn't clean the throat of a cone, LC-MS/MS detects aconitine at ng levels, and the scarf picks up both aconitine and lipstick. Plausible.
7. **Lip swab and lipstick.** Once Tess hears "trumpet", she would ask the OCME for perioral and lip swabs and would test the lipstick tube (Ivy had access to it: a poisoned lipstick is a natural theory). A positive lip swab doesn't kill the tea theory, because she drank tea; the negative lipstick tube narrows it. Add both: they cost a sentence and buy competence.
8. **Tox timing contradiction.** Spine: *"Mon afternoon: preliminary targeted tox positive."* Outline Ch 15: Dale gets the call during *"Mon Oct 27, 5:30–9 a.m."* and the exit is *"the lieutenant wants him at two."* With the autopsy on Sunday afternoon (Ch 11), that is under 18 hours for a non-routine LC-MS/MS screen at a reference lab. Fix: at Tess's request on Saturday night, Pen Bay sends the **antemortem ER blood** for a STAT targeted screen. Saturday night to Monday morning is about 36 hours: still compressed, but labelled convention, not impossible. Update the spine to "Mon early morning".
9. **The tea cups.** If the tea went into cups left on the stage, the police test those cups on Sunday and Phase 1 dies. Specify that Archie poured into the thermos's own nested cap-cups and both went back with the thermos to Bev and were washed. Also note that **Archie drank from the same thermos**, so Brannock has to say "you poured hers; you're a sleight-of-hand man", which is good and should be said.

### M6. The click is handed to Archie rather than caused by him, and he leaves the box unopened all day.

Ivy volunteers both the memory and the hatbox (Ch 30), and the ledger spells the answer out (*"P. Langley, Merrow… Son Daniel… Wife H."*). Archie's contribution is to connect three names. That is close to *"a resolution the sleuth receives rather than causes"* (anti-pattern list).

Worse, Ch 31's orientation: *"the hatbox rides in the hearse's passenger seat, belted in; he hasn't opened it — there's a show to rehearse."* That violates his card's appetite law (*"Archie cannot leave a locked thing locked"*), and he chooses a rehearsal over a lead that could free a woman charged with murder.

**Fix (best):** plant **"the Trumpet Hiatus"** as Kev's fan lore in Ch 21, played as comedy: "Sister Lori dropped the trumpet from private sittings from 2003 to 2006; the fandom thinks Brother Amos told her to." In Ch 30 Archie makes a performer's read: *you only drop a routine when it hurts someone.* He asks Ivy the specific question, "What happened in 2003?", and that is what unlocks "a man in Maine." That is agency through expertise. It is also fair play: the reader holds "trumpet stopped 2003" (Ch 21), "Peter Langley 2003" (Ch 3) and "H. Langley 12/03" (Ch 5).

Then make the hatbox unavailable until evening (F5: it's at Pru's, who is in Bangor until 9 p.m.), so the delay is a locked door and not a choice. Optionally, Lorelei's books are in her private sitter shorthand, which Archie can read because it is a variant of the billet code in Gus's book.

### M7. Ch 34–35: an impossible sighting, five hours with no surveillance on a poisoner, and how Hollis leaves the building.

- **The sighting:** Ch 34 runs *"~11 a.m. → 1 p.m."*, and Tess *"from the Sea View porch, earlier or on her way out… sees the hearse turn onto Pudding Lane."* But Archie goes to Pudding Lane at *"~3 p.m."* (Ch 35). She can't see a 3 p.m. hearse during a visit that ended at 1.
- **The five hours:** When Tess does know he's at the suspect's house, she has a poisoner with (presumably) poison at home, and the subject has just been tipped off by Archie's visit. Waiting for a 7:30 p.m. warrant and planning to "pick her up at the show, in public, quietly" with **no eyes on Hollis for five hours** is negligent: risk of evidence destruction, flight and self-harm.
- **The exit:** Ch 36 has police pulling up during Act One, and Hollis then leaves a balcony booth that opens onto the hall's main stairs. She'd walk into them.

**Fix:**
- Ch 34 runs ~12:30–4 p.m. (Sea View, then the basement files, then on her way to the courthouse). She passes the hearse parked on Pudding Lane at about 3:15.
- From 5 p.m., Kayla is in the hall in plain clothes and a cruiser is on Main Street. A warrant for the house and shed doesn't let Tess arrest Hollis without probable cause; surveillance is the competent move, and Brannock's no-contact order is why Tess doesn't phone Archie.
- Hollis leaves by the **balcony fire escape** (fire code requires a second egress from a balcony; establish it in Ch 31's rehearsal). Kayla's failure is a small human one, not stupidity.
- Ch 36's *"from the stage, he sees through the arched window the gray Interceptor"* is also physically doubtful: the stage faces down the hall, and the windows look down on the street from the second floor. Use Kayla visible at the back of the house instead.

### M8. Confession, custody and visitation procedure (Tess is "never wrong about procedure").

- **Ch 39:** *"Tess cautions her; Hollis tells it."* "Caution" is British; this is Miranda. Taking a custodial confession on a wharf from a 66-year-old who is hypothermic after 52°F immersion and has just tried to drown herself, while EMTs want her in the ambulance, invites a voluntariness challenge. That is the exact error Tess's card says she never makes. Fix: Hollis's statement is **spontaneous**. Tess tells her to stop, reads her rights, and Hollis insists. Tess asks no questions, and spontaneous statements are admissible. Tess puts her in the ambulance, and the formal Mirandized statement comes on Saturday at Pen Bay once she's medically cleared.
- **Ch 42 (Sat 1 p.m., Knox County Jail):** Hollis attempted suicide 16 hours earlier, so she'd be under medical observation and then on suicide watch. She is a new detainee with no approved visitor list, and Archie is the State's chief witness against her. On top of that, *"she gives him her ring of keys"*, which jail property rules make impossible across a visiting-room table. Fix (best): the keys come out of her **soaking cardigan pocket on the wharf in Ch 39**, *"Somebody has to run the lights"*, which is the stronger image anyway. Move Ch 42 to a supervised glass-partition visit she requests through her lawyer (Saturday afternoon at Pen Bay with a trooper at the door is the most plausible place).
- **Ch 30 (Ivy's visit):** Her public defender is *"present, a cameo"* while Ivy talks to the State's star witness about the facts. A PD would shut that down unless it served the defence. Fix: make it serve the defence. The PD wants Archie, the world's best debunker, to find a third party, and third-party culpability is defence gold. That is why the PD allows it and approves the hatbox release. It also explains how the release note is legally worth anything.
- **Ch 41 (Sat 9 a.m.):** Ivy is free and at The Galley ("AG drops charges", spine §4). That is too fast for the AG to dismiss a murder charge on the morning after an uncorroborated confession from a suicidal woman. Fix: on Saturday morning a bail commissioner releases Ivy on personal recognizance on the remaining tampering count. The AG moves to dismiss the murder charge on Monday. Move Ch 41 to late morning.

### M9. Climax mechanics.

- **Ozzie:** He is *in the audience* in Ch 36 (*"the town in the seats (… Ozzie …)"*), yet he texts *"a skiff's gone from the float"* in Ch 37. Fix (best, and it uses the bible): the harbormaster is **on wharf duty on Halloween night** because of the kids' midnight "Fairweather three times" dare. He watched Act One and went down at 8.
- **The rescue:** A 70-year-old harbormaster's first act is to throw a ring buoy, not watch a magician dive. Fix (best): Ozzie throws the ring and **Hollis won't take it**. That makes Archie's dive necessary, characterful and kinder to Ozzie's competence. Ozzie bends a line to Archie's waist before he goes ("escape artist's habit": he's never gone into water untethered). Tess radios Marine Patrol and the Coast Guard (Rockland station, twenty minutes out in fog), and Ozzie is simply here now. Note that Hollis "goes in" moments after Archie gives her Lorelei's "God forgive me". Keep the swell clearly the cause, or the book says his truth pushed her in.
- **The light board:** Ch 35 says *"two hundred and eighty cues no one else knows"*, yet Act Two runs, with a finale that needs a spotlight on the chair. Who is on the board? Fix: Hollis taught one other person the board, Mattie, at Thursday's rehearsal ("cue book in the drawer, girl"). Or Act Two drops to presets and Pinkham works the follow spot. Either way, say it in Ch 37.
- **Ch 37's "empty hook where the town-float skiff key hangs":** Small outboards are pull-start with no keys, and why would the opera house hold a skiff key? Use Ozzie seeing her take a skiff off the float.

### M10. Motive shape: six months of premeditated murder, procuring her neighbours, and refusing the obvious exposure.

- **The pitch letter.** Spine, May: *"local sitters with genuine losses — I can introduce you."* Hollis is offering grieving neighbours, possibly Shannon, as bait for the medium she believes drove her husband to his death. That is a sympathy-killer that nobody in the book addresses. **Cut that clause.**
- **Why murder rather than exposure?** She persuaded the world's most famous debunker to *keep* the séance so he could beat Lorelei on camera. A reader will ask why she didn't let Archie expose her. The plan needs Hollis's answer, and the book already has the evidence for it: grief is debunk-proof. Shannon, to Archie in Ch 3: *"Don't you dare make fun of it."* Peter would not have stopped.
- **When the decision turns into murder.** Make it a two-step: **May** is an exposure plan (bring her to the town where Peter's name is cut in granite; confront her on camera with Peter's notebook). **September** is the turn: in her paid phone reading, Shannon is told *"Tommy misses you on the water, Shan"*, the first step of Peter's pattern (*"Come see me on the water"*). Hollis sees it starting again and digs the bed. That shortens the cold premeditation to about four weeks, gives a trigger, and makes "why now" Shannon in a sharper sense. Ch 13 can carry an honest seed: Shannon, bitter, says *"Hollis already gave me the speech."*
- **Clue ledger.** Add to it the card's *"Don't cancel her. Beat her."* (Ch 1 or 2, said by Vivienne in admiration). It's a superb in-plain-sight clue (she needed the séance) and is currently unplanted.

### M11. Warren is dismissed with an argument that doesn't exist yet, and the reason given is false.

Ch 20 (Tue 7:30 a.m.): Tess clears Warren as *"never near the table after the toot."* But the lab doesn't make the trumpet the weapon until Tuesday afternoon (Ch 22), so on Tuesday morning the police theory is still the tea and the toot window means nothing to them. It's also false: Lorelei whispered to him at 7:38, presumably at the table, and he sat *at the table* at 7:58.

Separately, Warren selling Gus posters that belong to the building is theft of town (or estate) property. Tess, *"honest to the point of discomfort"*, can't just pocket that.

**Fix:** Tess clears Warren on what the police actually hold on Tuesday morning. He never went near anything she ate or drank. Under Archie's own trumpet theory, the toot cleared the Friday key, and Warren was downstairs from 7:47 to 7:57 with Pinkham watching the stairs. The key was for the poster, and they already know (he told them on Sunday). On the posters, the owner decides: they were Gus's, on loan to the town, so Vivienne is the owner. Tess refers it, Vivienne declines to press charges, and that sets up Ch 41's gift as Vivienne's too.

## MINOR

1. **Ch 26 title** *"Seven Minutes to Eight"* = 7:53, but the cutaway is at 7:51. Retitle "Nine Minutes to Eight" or "Forty Seconds".
2. **North-star:** *"Tess's father, then a young officer, closed the case"* contradicts Walt as *"Merrow police chief 1978–2010"* (he was chief in 1988 at 37). Also, a chief at 27 in 1978 is unusually young; consider 1981–2010.
3. **North-star:** *"Tess tells him he once wrote her a letter"* versus outline Ch 43, where she only quotes it and he doesn't recognise it. Align the north-star to the outline.
4. **North-star premise:** *"a million dollars"*, but the outline has Lorelei raise it to two million. Fine as a jacket, but say "a million — she raises it to two."
