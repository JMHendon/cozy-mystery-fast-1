## 1. When did I suspect the killer? Was I surprised?

**Who:** Hollis Tibbetts.

**First flicker, Ch 7.** It's a throwaway line, but the placement bugged me: *"Hollis, in a pair of the crew's black stage gloves, lit the candles; the town's open-flame permit was in her name."* Then, a paragraph later: *"when Teddy called from the wing for her to slide the horn away from the candle, she slid it over on her way off."* In a cozy, when the narrator lists who touched the murder weapon in a crowd and gives one person gloves, she's mine.

**Firmer, Ch 13:** Rosie yells "Aunt Hollis!" and Shannon says *"Hollis already sat at this table this morning, at seven o'clock, and gave me the whole thing again... Page one-twelve."* She's the widow's protector, she's with the victim, and she's so kind it's suspicious.

**Locked in, Ch 17:** *"Danny. My son."* plus the piano of photographs, plus "I was in the booth that night." A dead kid in a framed photo is the oldest motive tell there is. I hit about 65% there.

**85% by Ch 26,** when June reads the forty-second list and the first name is *"Mrs. Tibbetts, with the tray."* The book then spends two more chapters on Kev, Vivienne, and Ivy.

**Was I surprised?** Not at the WHO. I was surprised by:
- HOW: the nitrile finger cot under the black cotton glove and the tin the size of a quarter. *"Nobody looks at the stagehand, dear."* That's a satisfying method reveal, and the paper-cone demo with the lunch menu in Ch 41 is the best "explain the trick" scene in the book.
- The emotional register: I did not expect to be sobbing for the killer on a boat.
- **Gus.** The folded cloak was planted twice (Ch 2, Ch 15). I half-suspected he staged his vanishing, but the Key West 1996 postcard in the trunk, the wink to Walt, and "see about a boat" were a real kick. That's the twist I didn't see coming, and it was better than the whodunit.

**Honest critique:** the sleuth is about 15 chapters behind the reader. Archie lists "Mrs. Tibbetts" first in Ch 26 and doesn't blink. It makes him slightly dim at the exact thing he's supposed to be best at (reading people). The cozy convention is that the beloved mentor is the killer. A seasoned BookTok reader sees that coming, so you needed a stronger second suspect.

## 3. Archie and Tess

**Archie: gremlin?** He's a lovable show-off with a golden retriever's heart, but he's not quite a chaos gremlin. His actual gremlin moments are the best things in the book:
- The lobster he can't boil, who turns out to be a girl and whom he can't stop calling Harold.
- Pancakes shaped like cards ("The queen of hearts looked like Ohio").
- Delivering an espresso machine to a homicide lieutenant.
- Getting sick over the rail while the harbor radio comments on his chumming.
- The bobby pin and the "four seconds" lock pick.

Those are 9/10 gremlin. But by the middle he turns into a competent noir hero who explains tricks and has grim insight, and the gremlin energy fades. The Galley accusation (Ch 19) is the book's best character move: his showmanship hurts Warren and he has to live with it. I'd die for him in Ch 2, 15, 16, and 29, and be annoyed in Ch 22-27. Overall: 8/10, would die for.

**Tess/Archie slow burn:** scream-worthy in bursts, but too sparse. Moments I screamed at: the pen lift (Ch 4), "It's your hands. When you've got something, they stop" (Ch 14), "I know how a trick works, Archie. I've watched yours" (Ch 20), her hand on his wrist over the notebook (Ch 20), and the shoebox letter "Don't tell anyone. — A.F." (Ch 34). **Best moment:** the watch in Ch 43. She lifts it off him mid-rescue, "That's a *lift*," then ends on "Don't tell anyone," which echoes the letter she kept for 25 years. That's a ten. Problem: from Ch 23 to Ch 33 they share maybe two scenes and one phone call. Give me one more banter-and-heat scene in the middle.

## 7. Slow, repetitive, overwritten, AI-ish

- **Re-explaining the trick.** The hand release and the "cold touch" get demoed in Ch 3 (spoon on neck), Ch 11 (spoon on neck again, with Dale as the plant), and Ch 21 (wet glove on Kev). Three times is one too many for a reader who already knows. Cut Ch 11's.
- **Brannock recaps.** Ch 16, Ch 22, and Ch 27 each tell Archie why he looks guilty. The Ch 27 one: *"Your mother, who cut monkshood out of your garden for the kitchen table..."* I already know.
- **The Ch 33 montage paragraph:** *"A letter to a television company in May... A fire permit in a cardigan pocket, and candles lit by the one pair of hands allowed to strike a match on that stage..."* Pretty, but it tells me what I already worked out ten chapters ago.
- **Verbal tics:**
  - Eyebrows moving a fraction of an inch (*"went up a quarter of an inch," "a sixteenth of an inch"*).
  - *"which was how he knew what it cost."*
  - *"She didn't smile. She didn't put the card away either."* The "didn't X. Didn't Y." beat is everywhere.
  - Archie "grinned" a lot.
  - *"stood in front of thirty thousand people"* appears in Ch 5, 14, 36.
  - Chapter-end buttons: *"Folded."* *"He's lying to me about something."* Nearly every chapter closes on a mic-drop line, which feels formula by Ch 25.
- **Everyone speaks in aphorisms.** June, Tess, Hollis, Bev, Brannock, Margaret all land crisp epigrams: *"Nobody looks at the stagehand, dear."* *"That's not a feeling. That's arithmetic."* *"You can't debunk a widow."* They're good in isolation, but the voices blur in the middle.
- **Overwritten similes in stacks.** *"low and warm and rich as a cello"*; *"like a bird thrown against a window"*; *"went into him like a splinter."* Emotion is sometimes named a beat after it's already shown.
- **Saint-Pierre rum-runner riff (Ch 11):** funny once, but it doesn't pay off.
- **Cloak-fold hammer:** Ch 2 plants it, Ch 15 hints, Ch 38 does it, Ch 40 has Vivienne spell it out (*"exactly the way my father used to fold it, Archibald. Exactly."*), then Ch 44 reveals it. By the time Ch 44 says "Folded." I've been told three times. Trust me more.
- **Plot nit:** "Intermission. Please." as Archie's text to Tess (Ch 35) is a cute riddle but sells Tess short. She already had the warrant and could have walked in at 3 p.m.

## 8. Rating, caption, top 8 changes

**Rating: 4.5 stars.** Rounds to 5 on Kindle.

**BookTok caption:** "POV: you picked up a cozy about a retired magician and a lobster named Harold (she's a girl, he finds out Chapter 15) and ended up crying over a retired English teacher with a cat named Malvolio. Found family that feeds you pie, a state cop who lifts his watch back off him mid-rescue, a sixteen-year-old who does the whole trick, and a postcard that made me scream at 2 a.m. 'Don't tell anyone.' Ma'am, I am telling EVERYONE."

**Top 8 changes (chapter-specific):**

1. **Ch 7 and Ch 17: hide Hollis better.** Remove "black stage gloves" and "slid it over on her way off" from Ch 7, or surround them with other gloved hands and other horn-sliders. Make "Danny. My son." in Ch 17 less on-the-nose (a card, not a framed photo). Right now a savvy reader nails her by Ch 17 and watches Archie lag for 15 chapters.
2. **Ch 26-27: kill the carousel.** June reads "Mrs. Tibbetts, with the tray" first and nobody pauses. Either give Hollis a clean, plausible alibi beat that lets the reader doubt, or let Archie notice her and fear it. Archie ignoring her reads as plot convenience.
3. **Ch 16, 22, 27: merge Brannock's three "here's why it's you" speeches into one** and give the saved chapter space to Tess/Archie.
4. **Ch 11: cut the Saint-Pierre rum-runner riff and the second spoon-on-the-neck demo** (Ch 3 already did it).
5. **Ch 23-24: tighten.** Two phone calls, Margaret's backstory, and a legal-pad table redraw. Keep Lenny's "Who touched it after you?" and June's laptop exchange; cut the rest.
6. **Add one charged Tess/Archie scene between Ch 23 and Ch 33.** A stakeout, a late-night bobby-pin callback, anything. Ch 29's single phone call ("I believe you") is the sole warm beat in a ten-chapter stretch.
7. **Ch 33: replace the clue-montage paragraph with one sharp image**, and trim the Ch 40 Vivienne line so Ch 44's "Folded." lands harder. Let readers feel clever instead of being told.
8. **Give Archie a mid-book disaster** (a botched trick, a broken prop, a dove on the select board) around Ch 28-31 to bring the gremlin back. He's at his best when he's making things worse.
