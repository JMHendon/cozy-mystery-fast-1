# Reader report — Grace (45, Minneapolis; school counselor; runs a monthly book club; ~50 books/yr)

**Title read:** *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather* (An Archie Fairweather Mystery), 44 chapters.
**Read:** straight through, in order, over what would have been three nights.

---

## 1. The killer: when I suspected, when I was sure, what tipped me

**Killer: Hollis Tibbetts.** I called it.

- **First flicker — Chapter 7 ("Controls").** The crowd-scene paragraph while Archie talks riverboats with Dale is the giveaway, and it's one sentence doing too much work:
  > "Hollis, in a pair of the crew's black stage gloves, lit the candles; the town's open-flame permit was in her name... when Teddy called from the wing for her to slide the horn away from the candle, she slid it over on her way off."
  Gloves + the one hand on the murder object + she runs the lights + she booked Lorelei in May. By the end of Ch10 I had her at ~30%, co-leading with Warren. That's early for a 44-chapter book, and I'm allergic to that.
- **Growing certainty — Ch13 and Ch17.** "Aunt Hollis" arriving with American chop suey, having *already* been at Shannon's table at 7 a.m. the morning after; then the piano in Ch17: "a boy of about twelve on a dock, sunburned to the ears, holding up a mackerel... 'Danny. My son.'" I went back in my head to the library card in Ch5 — *H. Langley — 12/03* — the one borrower Deb pointedly doesn't gloss.
- **Sure — Ch21/22 → Ch26.** Kev's "Trumpet Hiatus... oh-three or oh-four to oh-six," plus Lorelei's own line in Ch6 — "two hundred little towns with a white church and a war memorial and somebody's mother who wanted to hear from her boy" — plus *Peter Langley* on the memorial in Ch3. When the handheld swings to the window for exactly forty seconds in Ch26 and the person moving the horn is Hollis, I was at 80%.
- **Nailed shut — Ch31,** overheard from the booth stairs: "Oh, sweetheart. The water's never warm here." That's the author telling me, and I think the author knows it.

**Important nuance:** I had the *who* early; I did not have the *why* until Ch32, and the why (Peter's forty-one Thursdays, "Come see me on the water," "God forgive me") is what made the book. The red herrings (Ivy's scarf, Warren's posters, Teddy's policy, Kev's prop room) never fooled me as *killers*, but they worked beautifully as *character* — I never minded following them because each one cost Archie something.

## 3. The lead and the ensemble

**Archie.** Yes, I fell for him — but the book is smart about *how*. I didn't want to be him (he's exhausting; June is right about everything) and I didn't exactly want to be with him; I wanted to be *in the room* with him. What won me: he refuses the easy lie to Liam; he keeps his statement about Ivy when lying would be kinder to himself; he drinks Hollis's chamomile. His flaw (he can't not perform) is the engine of the plot, not a decoration — the Galley humiliation of Warren is the book's bravest chapter because the author lets him be genuinely awful. The "he counts everything" tic (fifty-nine days, two-eleven, three minutes forty) is the best kind of character shorthand.

**Loved:**
- **June** — the real detective. The three-finger list in Ch10, "It's arithmetic," the stopwatch, "You're late." She is the reason I'd buy Book 2.
- **Tess** — reads his hands at poker; "That's what calling me is for, Archie." The slow burn is calibrated exactly right for a series.
- **Hollis** — a killer I grieved. The chowder/cat/waistcoat chapter is why the ending lands.
- **Vivienne** — "out is a complete sentence." Every line is a keeper.
- **Lenny** — "You came back." Four words, thirty years.
- **Bev** — sits down for the first time in Ch33 and it's devastating.
- **The liars' table** — Pickle/Lionel's "And my name's Lionel" got an out-loud laugh.
- **Lorelei** — the book's quiet triumph is making me like the fraud. "People come to me, and I lie to them for two hours, and they go home with their dead."

**Flat-ish:** Teddy is a type (mango vapor, two phones) until the airport, where he gets one real chapter; Kev is delightful but a sketch; Brannock is "fair lieutenant" issue; Kayla and Marisol are furniture (fine). Ozzie I'd like more of — the man throws a ring buoy and says four words and I'd follow him anywhere.

## 4. Ending

**Catharsis: 9/10.**

**What landed:** the choice at intermission with no patter over his face; June's "So go"; his hands folding the cloak without him; "Hold on. I'm not letting go. So you have to"; Lenny's "You came back"; coming up through the trap in a foil blanket; "I came the long way"; Hollis handing the keys to *him*; the Galley giving him a chair; Elwin's "Correct answer"; "You remembered. That's the whole trick." Hollis's "Ask me in a year" is exactly the right refusal of a tidy moral.

**Unearned / saccharine / overlong:**
- **Ch34 — Tess's childhood fan letter** ("You're right. Don't tell anyone. — A.F."). One coincidence past earned; it makes the romance feel fated rather than chosen. Cut or reduce to Walt's half-sentence.
- **Ch36 — Liam dressed as Archie, Rosie as a lobster, Kev's "BEHAVE" lanyard, Carol with the WARREN card.** Each is sweet; together they tip into cute. Pick two.
- **Ch42–43** run a beat long; the Shannon/godmother exchange and the Warren posters could each lose a paragraph. The watch lift, though, is perfect — keep it.
- **Ch44 — the Key West postcard.** I *loved* it and I'm *suspicious* of it. "You remembered. That's the whole trick" is a complete, earned ending; the postcard turns it into a series hook and some of my club will feel the trunk's emotional payoff got traded for a cliffhanger. (I'm buying Book 2 because of it, so the author wins either way.)
- Minor: Hollis blurting a confession on the dock before Miranda, Brannock letting a person of interest wander every scene — genre-normal, but the lawyers in my club will grumble.

Nothing in the ending felt *unearned*. Sentiment here is paid for in advance: Danny's mackerel photo in Ch17 is what makes "Oh, Peter" in Ch38 land.

## 5. Book 2

**9/10 — buy immediately.** Reasons: a living Gus in Key West (1996) and a man at Sea View to see "about a boat"; Tess and the watch; June with Gus's jacket and the oath; Archie consulting on "the weird ones"; Malvolio. My one fear is that a sequel over-explains Gus — the postcard works because it's a single smudged postmark. Keep him a rumor as long as possible.

## 6. Prose

**Verdict:** this reads like an experienced, successful author with a strong editor — clean sentences, a consistent comic voice, set pieces that know exactly how long to run, chapter buttons that snap. It does not read as generic or machine-like in texture. Where I noticed the machinery was in *patterning*:

**Repetitive tics (an editor's find-and-replace pass):**
- "a second/moment longer than she/he needed to" — Ch5 (June), Ch15 (rail), Ch32 (June), Ch35 (Hollis: "She looked at him a second longer than she needed to"), Ch43 variants. Five-plus uses.
- Eyebrows measured in fractions: "a quarter of an inch" (Ch9), "a sixteenth of an inch" (Ch14). Once is a joke; twice is a tic.
- The bell buoy as scene-ender: Ch2, Ch15, Ch20, Ch33, Ch37, Ch38, Ch43 ("the bell buoy off Shag Ledge rang once, and then again, slow, on a calm sea"). It's the right image; it's overused.
- "said it to the counter / to the table / to nobody" — Ch19 (Warren, twice), Ch32, Ch36.
- "went through him" / "like a splinter" — Ch36 "this one went straight through him"; Ch35 "it went into him like a splinter"; Ch43 "They went into him now and stopped somewhere."
- Applause as weather, twice: Ch36 "began at the back and rolled forward and broke over the stage"; Ch40 "began somewhere in the back and came forward like weather."
- Every chapter ends on a wry one-liner. By Ch16 ("Somewhere I can find you") I could feel the button coming, and by Ch28 ("Every bucket of it") I was bracing for it. Let two or three chapters end mid-breath.

**Slightly over-written:**
- Ch8: "her heart going in his arm like a bird thrown against a window — too fast, then a stumble, then too fast again." Vivid, but the dash-clause after the simile over-explains it.
- Ch33: "It felt like a room he'd been living in for a week, filling up slowly with water, and everything in it was something he had already touched." Lovely idea, one clause too many.
- Ch40: "a cloak made of mirrors" for the foil blanket — the paragraph has already done that work.

**Expository seams / plants that show:**
- Ch6: Teddy's "*—no, the key-person policy runs through the end of the season, that's the point—*" through a half-open door is a stage whisper for the reader.
- Ch31: the fire-marshal/fire-door exchange exists only so Hollis can leave by it; Dale even underlines it ("the one the fire marshal made her unlock" is then repeated in Ch37).
- Ch2 and Ch16: monkshood introduced, then Archie volunteers it to the police — fine, but the symptoms lecture in Ch9 ("the queen of poisons") is a wiki paragraph in a mouth.

**Generic lines (few):** "the thousand-yard stare" (Ch4); "the whole thing had been undone" (Ch19); "It was only the shape of things, laid end to end by a man who was good at his job" (Ch16 — fine, but it's the book's second "laid end to end").

None of this is damning; it's the kind of list a good copyeditor fixes in an afternoon. The voice is the real thing.

## 7. Book club

**Three questions we'd argue about (for an hour, happily):**
1. **Is Hollis a murderer or something else?** She says "Ask me in a year." Archie drinks her tea. Shannon cries on the railing for her. Does the book let her off too easily — or is "I'd rather it had been anybody else" the most honest moral line in the genre?
2. **Lorelei's defense:** "People come to me and I lie to them for two hours, and they go home with their dead. Tell me which one of us they come back to." Archie's answer is weak — on purpose? Where is the line between Archie's lies and hers? (Shannon: "You took him away again.")
3. **Archie's two choices on Halloween:** giving Hollis her show instead of calling Tess at 5 a.m., and leaving June mid-performance to go after Hollis. Right, wrong, or the only version of him we'd read? (And the bonus fight: did Gus do the same thing to Vivienne in 1988 — and is the postcard a gift or a cruelty?)

**Star rating:** ★★★★½ (4.5/5). Half a star off because a seasoned cozy reader will have Hollis on the board by Chapter 7 — but the *why* is hidden fairly, and the book is about the cost of being right, not the puzzle.

**Two-sentence review:** A debunker who can't stop performing moves to a Maine harbor town and bets two million dollars against a TV medium — who dies in the dark mid-séance, speaking in his grandfather's voice. The mystery is good; the town, the daughter, the state cop who reads his hands, and the killer you'll grieve are the reasons you'll hand it to a friend the next morning.

## 8. Top 8 changes (chapter-specific)

1. **Ch7 — un-isolate Hollis.** The paragraph at the back of the hall currently gives her gloves, the match, the tray *and* the horn in one sentence. Bury the slide in a longer, noisier list (crew boy taping cable, Vivienne straightening the shawl, Marco adjusting) and move "the open-flame permit was in her name" to Ch11, where Hollis is already answering Tess's questions. Give one other person black gloves (Marco already has them — let him brush the table).
2. **Ch5 — fix the tell on the library card.** Deb glosses *every* borrower except *H. Langley*. Either gloss Langley with a plausible lie ("Summer people, the Langleys; she wanted it for a Halloween party") or have Deb skip two names so one doesn't glow. Keep the card — it's the fairest clue in the book.
3. **Ch8–9 — make the Ivy herring deniable.** Every cozy reader knows the person who wipes the horn in full view is innocent. Either let Archie *not* see the wipe clearly (he's on the floor; he sees the scarf go into a pocket and infers), so his statement is an honest guess that costs more, or give Ivy a second, genuinely ambiguous action (pocketing the lozenge tin) so the reader can't dismiss her by Ch10.
4. **Ch18 — cut by a third and give Archie a flicker of doubt.** Carol was planted in Ch4 and the reader is a mile ahead of him on Warren. If he half-knows the Carol connection and does the Galley trick anyway, Ch19 is a moral failure rather than a blind spot, and lands harder.
5. **Ch24–27 — compress the forty-seconds logistics.** Three chapters of "who had the horn" could be two. Fold Brannock's rain-soaked "and I've got your mother" into the end of Ch26 and lose the Ch27 parking lot; let Tess's "That's what calling me is for" close Ch26 instead.
6. **Ch31 — lighten the setup.** The fire-door scene telegraphs Hollis's exit; the cough-drop drawer telegraphs the lozenges; "the water's never warm here" is the author confirming the killer for anyone still unsure. Keep the Rosie line (it's the best thing in the chapter) but cut the fire-marshal exchange to one offhand sentence and drop the cough-drop drawer.
7. **Ch34 — remove the childhood fan letter.** Tess's "You're right. Don't tell anyone. — A.F." is one coincidence past what the romance has earned, and it pre-chews Ch43's "Don't tell anyone." Walt's "trumpet woman" carries the chapter alone.
8. **Ch44 — protect the trunk's ending.** "You remembered. That's the whole trick" is complete. Consider ending the chapter there with the cloak-folding realization, and move the Key West postcard into a two-paragraph coda (or make the postmark less legible so the club argues about whether it's real). Also trim Vivienne's "I told you all... draw your own conclusions" — she doesn't need to annotate her own foreshadowing.

**Bonus (line-level):** one pass for "a second longer than she needed to," fractional eyebrows, bell-buoy scene-enders, and the every-chapter button. Let three chapters end without a quip.
