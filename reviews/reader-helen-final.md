# Early-Reader Panel: Helen (Portsmouth, NH, independent bookseller)

**Book:** *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather* (An Archie Fairweather Mystery)
**Read:** Cover to cover, in order, with a pencil. About 81,000 words, 44 chapters.

## My suspect list, chapter by chapter (written as I went)

1. **Upside Down.** No body yet. The medium is the obvious victim. I'm watching Ivy (she watches his hands), Teddy (phones), and Hollis, because she *invited them in May* and she has the keys to everything.
2. **The Trunk.** The monkshood is put on the table in chapter two, so it's a deliberate plant and the poison is settled. That puts the Fairweather garden in it, which makes it a Vivienne red herring. Gus's book has a trumpet in it.
3. **The Liars' Table.** Hollis teaches the séance unit ("Page one-twelve"). Warren hates Archie and runs the sale. Shannon is the widow. I wrote down "Peter Langley, 2003" from the memorial for no particular reason.
4. **Sea View.** Carol is waiting for "Warren", so that's Warren's wife. Walt says "Gus still owes me a boat", which I filed as the series mystery. My list is Hollis, Ivy, Warren, Teddy.
5. **The Sign-Out Card.** "H. Langley — 12/03." I circled it. A Langley on the memorial in 2003 and an H. Langley borrowing the debunking book a month later. W. Calloway borrowed it in 11/88.
6. **Two of a Kind.** Teddy's key-person policy is the money motive. Ivy pours her mother's drinks and wants out ("You're done when I say"). Lorelei worked "Maine, back in the day", so she may have a past here. Ivy / Teddy / Hollis.
7. **Controls.** Archie's chamomile from Bev's thermos. Warren had the prop-room key, goes white at the whisper, then leaves "sick". **Hollis lights the candles, sets the water and slides the horn.** Shannon touches the small end. The crew wear black gloves. My list is Hollis (quiet favorite), Warren (loud favorite), Ivy.
8. **The Other Side.** Poison on the mouthpiece. Ivy wipes it, which looks too guilty to be guilty, so she's covering the act and not a murder. Hollis was in the booth and ran the lights. Hollis / Teddy / Warren.
9. **The Waiting Room.** Ivy says "She held my hand" twice. She's lying about the act, not the death. No change.
10. **Pancakes.** June's list makes the field everyone. Archie is a suspect too. No change.

12. **The Captain's Suite.** The binder has a GILLEY tab, so Lorelei had leverage on Warren. Ivy didn't know about the retirement. Warren / Hollis / Teddy.
13. **Rosie and Liam.** Hollis is "Aunt Hollis", godmother to Rosie and protective of Shannon. That's a motive by proxy. **Hollis is now first.**
14. **Five-Card Draw.** Bev washed the thermos. The Baja theory is not one I took seriously. Hollis / Teddy.
15. **Hauling.** Gus's empty skiff with the cloak folded on the thwart. Homicide confirmed. Hollis / Teddy / Warren.
16. **Person of Interest.** Archie is the police's man. Hollis / Teddy.
17. **Malvolio.** *A boy of about twelve on a dock with a mackerel. "Danny. My son."* She was in the booth in 1988. With the H. Langley card in my head, I put a pencil star next to Hollis.
18. **Harborview Antiques.** Warren's posters. Too easy, and it's the middle of the book, so I took him off.
19. **Town Business.** Warren is cleared, emotionally. Hollis / Kev / Teddy.
20. **Outside. Now.** Hollis / Kev.
21. **Spectral Kev.** **The "Trumpet Hiatus", 2003 to 2006.** That lines up with Peter Langley (2003), H. Langley (12/03), and Danny on the piano. **I'm sure it's Hollis.** I'm waiting to learn how.
22. **Evidence.** Ivy is arrested, which is the classic false solution around the two-thirds mark. Hollis.
23. **The Wrong Woman.** The chapter title agrees with me. Hollis.
24. **Family Meeting.** Hollis.
25. **Owls Head.** Teddy's alibi on motive is solid, so he's off. Hollis.
26. **Forty Seconds.** The candle flare blanks the only camera while the woman who lit the candles moves the horn. That's the method. Hollis.
27. **Narrowed Down.** The book raises Vivienne and Shannon as suspects. I don't buy either. Hollis.
28. **Paint Day.** The gloves are collected, and Hollis's are fingerless, which is a nice touch. Hollis.
29. **The Method.** Hollis. June's chapter is a delight.
30. **Visiting Hours.** "The man in Maine", November 2003. Hollis.
31. **Dress Rehearsal.** "The water's never warm here" are Lorelei's exact lines coming back. The fire door unlocked is a Chekhov's door. Hollis.
32. **The Hatbox.** P.L., D., fifteen, quarry, "Come see me on the water", "God forgive me." Confirmed. Hollis.
33. **Blueberry or Apple.** Bev confirms it. Hollis.
34. to 44. Hollis is confirmed, followed by the confession, the rescue, and the Gus hook.

## 1. When I suspected, when I was sure, and why

- **First suspicion: Chapter 1, then Chapter 7.** Long experience says the sensible, warm local who arranged the event and holds every key is the one to watch. Chapter 7 put her hands on the candles, the glasses and the horn in the fifteen minutes that mattered. Chapter 11 has her say on the record that she "slid the horn over."
- **Leaning hard: Chapter 13** ("Aunt Hollis" and her protectiveness of Shannon) **and Chapter 17** (the Danny photograph on the piano, and the fact that she ran the board in 1988).
- **Sure: Chapter 21.** Kev's "Trumpet Hiatus, oh-three … to oh-six" connected two clues I'd already circled: *Peter Langley, 2003* on the granite (Ch. 3) and *H. Langley — 12/03* on the library card (Ch. 5). After that I was reading for method and motive, not for the name.
- **Confirmed:** Chapters 32 and 33 (the hatbox and Bev's "Langley? That's Hollis.").
- **Alternatives I genuinely considered:** Warren (Chapters 7 to 18). His key, his whisper and his sick exit made a good false trail, and his reveal is the best scene in the middle of the book. Teddy (the key-person policy, Chapters 6 to 25). Ivy (the wipe and the lie, Chapters 8 to 22). I never took Ivy seriously for the murder because her guilt was shown too openly. I gave Kev and Shannon a few minutes each. The book raises Vivienne and Archie in Chapter 27, but no cozy reader will believe the author would make it the grandmother.

**My verdict on the puzzle:** it plays fair, and the clueing is elegant (the memorial, the library card, the piano photo, and Hollis's own "The water's never warm here"). For a veteran cozy reader, though, Hollis is **guessable by the 40% mark**, and certain by the halfway point. The red herrings are each dismissed cleanly and early, so the middle stretch has only one live suspect, and that's the person the reader already suspects. Customers will still love it, because the *why* hits so hard. But the regulars who come in boasting "I got it on page 50" will have got it on page 50.

**Where customers stall:** Chapters 22 to 27. The middle sags because it has three scenes in a row where a cop tells Archie off (the porch in 14, the wharf in 20, the parking lot in 27, plus Brannock in 16), a recap chapter (24), and a second careful pass over the séance footage (26). A reader who has already pegged Hollis will feel the book is catching up with them. The second soft spot is the Saint-Pierre crate aside in Chapter 11.

## 3. Archie and the ensemble

**Yes, I fell for him.** He's a show-off with a good heart who counts the days the liars' table has ignored him, keeps a lobster in the sink, and lies to a dying woman because it's the kind thing to do. The Galley ambush of Warren (Ch. 19) will lose a few readers for a chapter, and it should. The book makes him pay for it and apologize properly, which is the right shape.

Would customers *want to spend time with him?* Absolutely. Would they want to *be* him? No. They'll want to be **Vivienne** ("out is a complete sentence") or **Bev**, and they'll want **June** for a granddaughter. The ensemble is the selling point: Lenny ("You came back"), Bev, Dale and his riverboat, Lionel, Kev, Malvolio, and a slow burn with Tess done with restraint. Tess at twelve sending a letter with two stamps, and Archie writing back "You're right. Don't tell anyone", will have the romance readers ordering Book 2 on the spot. Hollis is the best-drawn murderer I've read in a cozy in years. Customers will grieve her.

## 6. Does it read like a seasoned author with a top team?

**Yes, overwhelmingly.** The voice is confident, funny and humane, with real control of timing and set-ups (the folded cloak in Ch. 2 pays off in Chs. 38 and 44; the raspberry is both a gag and the key alibi; "Don't tell anyone" comes back from the other side). The Maine detail is right: Pen Bay in Rockport, Owls Head and Cape Air, Knox County Jail, the ME in Augusta, measuring a lobster eye-socket to carapace, the bight, *ayuh* used sparingly. The dates also check out: Halloween 2025 falls on a Friday, Gus was 29 in 1988, and the ages of Ivy, Tess and Danny all add up. Someone kept a timeline.

Very little sounds machine-made. What I did flag were **tics that repeat**:
- **"for the first time"** appears about 15 times ("looked at Archie for the first time that morning", "for the first time that evening", "For the first time something moved in her face").
- **The unreadable-face device**, five times: "full of something he couldn't read"; "anything Archie had a word for"; "nothing in it he could name"; "a face he had never seen on her"; "an expression he had never seen on her and didn't have time to read."
- **Peering "over her glasses / over her reading glasses"**, about 8 times (Hollis, Bev, Deb all do it).
- **Hollis's "short bark of a laugh"**, 4 times.
- **"He thought about… / He thought of…"** litanies, about 11 times. Several big decisions are staged as a list of three things he thinks about.
- **"He opened his mouth. He shut it again." / "had nothing to put in it"**, several times.
- **The résumé litany** ("walked through a wall at Fenway… a safe at the bottom of Lake Tahoe… four minutes in a water tank") comes up three times.
- Short-sentence reversals like "It wasn't much. It was what he had." and "He took it. It was more than he'd had on Sunday." Each is fine on its own, but there are enough of them to notice.

None of this is fatal. A copyeditor's pass would thin it.

## 7. Shelf-talker, rating, top 5 fixes

**Shelf-talker (25 words):**
> A retired TV magician, a Maine harbor town, and a medium who dies mid-séance. Funny, generous, heartbreaking — the cozy I'll be hand-selling all winter. Bring tissues.

**Rating:** ★★★★½

**Top 5 remaining fixes:**
1. **Tighten the middle (Chs. 22 to 27).** Merge 26 and 27, trim 24's recap, and cut one of the four "authority scolds Archie" scenes. Readers who have already guessed Hollis need momentum here, not review.
2. **Make the killer harder to spot, or make a rival stronger.** Hollis is in the frame too often too early (she invited them, she runs the lights, she slid the horn, she's the godmother, there's a piano photo), and every alternative is cleared quickly. Keep one rival alive through Chapter 30: let Shannon's "I've always known" and her borrowed insurance money build, or give Vivienne's secret phone calls real menace before they turn out to be Ozzie.
3. **Let Archie solve something himself.** At the moment the key steps come from Kev (the camera, the Hiatus), Teddy (the card), June (the decoding), Ivy (the notebooks), Bev (the name) and Walt's files (Tess). Give Archie the clinching deduction: for example, he realizes the candle was *placed* to flare, because the person who lit it "knows what a flame does to polished tin." That's the magician's insight the series promises.
4. **Plant the gloves visually.** Tess says Hollis slid the horn "in gloves," but the footage chapter (26) never shows us black crew gloves on a woman known for fingerless gray ones. One line there makes the Chapter 28 glove collection and the Chapter 41 confession pay off for the careful reader.
5. **A copyedit pass for the tics in section 6**, and spread out the Chapter 42 ribbon-tying (Ivy's check, Shannon, Warren's posters, the liars' table) so it doesn't all resolve in one lunch. Small Maine note: some readers may point out that most Midcoast seasonal lobster pounds have closed by Halloween, so Ozzie's Lincolnville date night is worth a fact-check.

*Bottom line: I'd order it deep, face it out by the register, and hand-sell it to every customer who ever asked me for "something like a hug with a body in it."*
