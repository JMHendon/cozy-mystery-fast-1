# Early-Reader Panel Report: *Nobody Fools Archie Fairweather*

**Reader:** Tom, 52, Providence. Former newspaper copy chief. I've read the major mystery series of the last forty years and I keep a clue log. I distrust any character the author seems to love too much, I usually solve the book before the halfway mark, and I'm a soft touch for a father-daughter story.

**Read:** cover to cover, in order, all 44 chapters (about 81,000 words). No skimming, no peeking ahead.

**Short verdict:** I named the killer at Chapter 21 and was sure of it by Chapter 32. I enjoyed being right, and I enjoyed the people more. Four stars. I want Book 2.

## 2. First suspicion, confidence, certainty

- **First suspicion: Chapters 1–2, flagged in earnest at Chapter 7.** Hollis invited the medium, and her line *"Don't cancel her… Beat her. In Gus's house"* is someone who wants Lorelei on that stage. The passage that put her on my board for real was Chapter 7: *"Hollis, in a pair of the crew's black stage gloves, lit the candles… when Teddy called from the wing for her to slide the horn away from the candle, she slid it over on her way off."* She was the one person with official permission to handle the props, she was wearing full gloves, and she was the teacher behind "lips to the small end."
- **Confidence (over 50%): Chapter 21.** Kev's "Trumpet Hiatus… oh-three or oh-four… to oh-six" connected four separate entries: *Peter Langley* (Ch 3 memorial), *H. Langley — 12/03* (Ch 5 card), *"Danny. My son."* (Ch 17), and the unit "in the last twenty years" (Ch 10). Kev's footage confirming the horn sat untouched until 8:17 narrowed the window to before lights-out.
- **Certainty: Chapter 32** (*"P.L., Mrrw… W. H. dsnt kno"* and *"11/20. P.L. lost at sea… God forgive me"*), sealed by Bev in Chapter 33.

I'll give the author credit here. The chain is genuinely there for a reader who logs names, and I enjoyed assembling it. The book did *not* let me prove Hollis = Langley before Chapter 33, though, and that's the main fair-play complaint below.

## 3. Fair play

**What's fair.** The method is fully clued: the trumpet chapter, the raspberry control, Hollis's gloves, the slide, the forty seconds. The motive chain is clued through the memorial, the card, Danny, the Hiatus, and "it's warm here." The escape is clued by the fire door in Chapter 31. The candle flare is engineered rather than lucky, and Hollis says so outright: *"I put it a hand from the horn on purpose."* That's good craft.

**What's withheld or cheated:**
- **The memorial years.** Chapter 3 gives names only: *"Arthur Coombs. Robert and James Haskell. Peter Langley. Dennis Pinkham."* In Chapter 32 Archie "remembers" *"Arthur Coombs, 1978. Robert and James Haskell, 1991. Peter Langley, 2003."* The year 2003 is the hinge of the whole case, and the reader never saw it. Either print it in Chapter 3 or don't let Archie recall it.
- **Hollis's monkshood.** Her bed of "tall blue flowers" first appears in Chapter 35, already dug up. On her only earlier visit (Ch 17) it's full dark and the garden isn't mentioned. "Half the gardens in Maine" is not a clue.
- **Hollis = Langley.** Nothing on the page links the two before Bev says so. The boat was the *Hollis T.*, but we only learn that at Chapter 33. One planted caption would make it fair, something like a Players cast photo reading "costumes: H. Langley."

**Coincidence and implausibility:**
- **Lorelei and Merrow.** She wrote *"God forgive me"* over a Merrow lobsterman in 2003. Twenty-two years later she signs to perform in Merrow and runs the same *"It's warm here… the water's warm"* on another Merrow fisherman's widow, and the book never shows her registering the town's name. That's either the darkest irony in the book or a hole. Give it one beat.
- **Method hole (Ch 41).** *"In the wings, I put one finger in it. Once."* Yet the Chapter 26 footage shows Hollis setting "a glass of water at every place" *before* the slide. She'd be carrying a coated, cotton-gloved finger over seven water glasses. Have her dip after the glasses are set.
- **Toxicology speed (Ch 15).** A preliminary aconitine result in the blood by Monday morning, after a Sunday 4 p.m. autopsy, is very fast. Brannock covers it in Chapter 27 (*"came back fast because the sergeant asked for one compound by name"*), but it should be acknowledged earlier.
- **The Ivy murder charge (Ch 29).** The AG files murder *after* the Marco card shows Ivy never got within 23 inches of the horn after 7:35. Brannock's "maybe the toot was fake" reasoning is thin to carry a murder filing.
- **Procedure, the big one (Ch 41).** Tess sits outside Hollis's hospital room and says *"if her lawyer comes up those stairs, I wasn't here,"* while a State's witness takes a charged murderer through the method with a paper cone. Any defense lawyer would make a meal of that. It's a lovely scene that would wreck the prosecution.
- **Consequences.** Archie holds a sealed confession from 3 p.m. to intermission and texts the detective a riddle. A murder suspect gets the run of a 311-seat house while one plainclothes officer watches the wrong exit. His reward is a reprimand "in writing" and a consulting offer. That's cozy logic, and I noticed.
- **Medical, minor.** The aconite course (perioral numbness, slurring, arrhythmia, death about 80 minutes later) is accurate. Archie's 3 minutes 40 seconds in 50–52°F water, a 40-foot crawl and a stage entrance is heroic but survivable. I let it go.

**Loose threads I logged and never saw paid off:**
- Ivy rubbing her fingertips (Ch 9). Aconite tingle, or just nerves?
- The lip swabs Tess ordered (Ch 11), and the redone lipstick and menthol.
- The Saint-Pierre rum-runner "drop" (Ch 11) was raised and abandoned.
- Who left the prop room unlocked: Warren says he locked it, and Kev found it open at 9:30.

## 4. Were the alternative suspects live?

- **Ivy.** Live from Chapter 6 to Chapter 21. I never had her above 28%. She's too obviously framed by her own behavior (the wipe, the lie, the remote), and the Chapter 22 arrest is the midpoint misdirect. I dropped her as the killer at 21. Footage showing the horn untouched after lights-out, plus a raspberry at 7:35, puts her out of the window.
- **Warren.** Live Chapters 3–19, then cleared in a single exchange at Chapter 20. The Galley scene is the best-written chapter in the book, but as a suspect he was dead on arrival once Tess reveals he confessed on Sunday.
- **Teddy.** Raised three times: the insurance (Ch 6), the Baja fake-death theory (Ch 14), and the withheld card (Ch 25). Dropped at 25. The Baja theory cost Archie credibility with me.
- **Kev.** A comic suspect. Archie revives him in Chapter 26 ("twelve seconds from the radiator"), but nobody believes it.
- **Vivienne.** The one live alternative late in the book: the monkshood, the thermos in her bag, two feet from the horn in the forty seconds, "fresh on Saturday morning," the pantry calls. I held her at 12–15% only because I distrust beloved characters. The book never commits to her, so the dread runs thin, and the calls turn out to be Ozzie (a nice payoff).
- **Shannon.** Had motive and was in the forty seconds, but the book never presses her. 5–7%.
- **Bev.** Only the thermos-washing. 3%.

**Structural note:** four wrong accusations (Teddy, Warren, Kev, Teddy again) are each knocked down in a single chapter, usually by information the police already had. That makes Archie look foolish more than misled, and it thins the field. One more suspect who stays genuinely live to Chapter 30 would raise the tension. Vivienne is the obvious candidate.

## 6. Climax, confession, ending

- **Earned?** Mostly yes. Chapter 38's turn, *"She remembered Peter… God forgive me,"* is the right key. It answers Hollis's grievance ("She didn't remember *any* of us") with the one thing that could reach her, and it's built from evidence we watched June decode. Hollis pushing Archie *away*, back toward the boat and his daughter, is the best single beat in the book.
- **The dock confession** (Ch 39) is a little convenient, but Tess cutting her off with the Miranda warning and *"Not here, Hollis"* rescues it. The hospital explanation (Ch 41) is a satisfying tell-the-trick scene, procedural problems aside.
- **The Empty Chair** (Ch 40) is the payoff of the June-and-Archie spine: the crawl, June stalling for five minutes, Lenny's "You came back," and *"You're late." / "I came the long way."* I was had, and happily.
- **The coda.** The SPARROW combination is guessable from Chapter 8. I had it then. The trunk contents land anyway: *"You remembered. That's the whole trick."* The Key West postcard and Walt's boat set up Book 2 cleanly.
- **Catharsis: 9/10.** I knocked off a point for Archie's soft landing with Brannock.

## 7. Archie and company

Yes, without hesitation.
- **Archie.** A show-off with a conscience. The book knows his flaws, including the public humiliation of Warren and the "Intermission. Please." text, and makes him pay in the right currency: his daughter's trust and Tess's anger.
- **June.** The best character in the book. The library pass, the notebook decoding, and the Chair are three of the best scenes.
- **Tess and Walt.** The second father-daughter line, and quietly devastating ("She's fifteen"). The A.F. photograph in her shoebox is a perfect reveal.
- **The supporting cast.** Lenny ("You came back") and Bev ("There's no third pie") are top-tier supporting work. The liars'-table counter is a running gag that pays off twice, with "Morning, Archie" going unheard and the chair pushed out.
- **Book 2 interest: 9/10.**
- **Watch for:** Tess and Archie's flirtation risks becoming a sequence of pickpocket gags, and Brannock's "consulting" arrangement needs a credible footing.

## 9. Star rating and top 8 fixes

**★★★★ (4 of 5).** It's a warm, well-built cozy with a better heart than most and a solvable puzzle. It loses a star for the withheld hinge clues and for police work that bends to the plot.

**Top 8 fixes:**
1. **Print the memorial years in Ch 3** (at least *Peter Langley, 2003*), or remove Archie's recall in Ch 32. That's the single biggest fair-play repair.
2. **Plant Hollis's monkshood before Ch 35.** For example, a frost-bitten blue border by the porch light in Ch 17, or a jar of the same blue flowers on her piano.
3. **Plant Hollis = Langley once before Ch 33.** A cast-photo caption, a Players program credit, or the *Hollis T.* in a photo on her piano. Let the reader beat Bev.
4. **Fix the procedure.** Put counsel in the hospital room in Ch 41 (or move the scene post-plea). Give Archie a real cost for sitting on the sealed confession. Justify the AG's murder filing against Ivy after the Marco card.
5. **Keep one alternative suspect alive to Ch 30.** Commit to the Vivienne dread or cut it back. Merge the Teddy Baja theory and the Kev revival so Archie isn't wrong four times in the same way.
6. **Close the loose threads:** Ivy's fingertips, the lip swabs, the Saint-Pierre crates, the unlocked prop room. And give Lorelei one beat recognizing *Merrow* in her own book (a 2025 entry, a mark), which would deepen her and sharpen Hollis's grievance.
7. **Fix the method order** (dip *after* the water glasses) and the continuity slips in section 8: the monkshood day, Mattie and the unit, "Dale's girl," the hearse callback.
8. **Trim about 20% from Chs 14–15, 17, 28, 31 and 42,** and run a pass on "flat," "He knew," "exactly," "didn't say anything" and "a second longer than…". Cut "Holy Houdini."

**The verdict on the slip:** I'd buy Book 2 the day it came out. I'd tell the author to give me the year on the stone next time.
