# Cozy Mystery Craft Documents — how to use this set

These documents are the house standard for planning, drafting, reviewing and editing **any** cozy mystery. They describe the genre and the craft, not a particular series, sleuth or book. Everything specific to a book — its sleuth, cast, setting, crime, solution, voice and any approved exceptions — belongs in that book's own foundation documents.

## The documents

| File | What it governs | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| `checklist.md` | One page: the gates, the lead rules and the review questions. Paste it into every planning, drafting and revision brief | Everyone, every brief |
| `cozy-mystery.md` | The genre standard: the hard gates, the reader promise, the lead, commissioning and outline review | Planners, outline reviewers, every critic adjudicating "is this a cozy?" |
| `storygrid-commandments.md` | Scene and sequence mechanics: the five commandments, cohesion, variation, obligatory scenes | Outliners and structural critics |
| `light-energy-structure.md` | Register: delight, comedy, gravity, the lead's presence, the climax-and-coda shape | Register critics, planners |
| `cozy-revision-protocol.md` | The revision pipeline and the review questions; the one document actively run against drafts | Revisers and reviewers |
| `line-edit-protocol.md` | The final sentence-level pass | Line editors; drafters as a self-check |

## Order of authority

When two sources disagree, the higher one wins. Nothing lower can waive anything higher.

1. **The author's brief for the book.** Only the author can grant an exception to a hard gate, and only in writing, recorded in the book's brief.
2. **The hard gates** (`cozy-mystery.md`). Pass/fail. No planner, critic, reviewer, reader panel or score can waive or offset one.
3. **The book's lead specification** (its lead character card, including any named reference character). Where a craft default would restrain the lead's voice, wit or display, the lead card wins.
4. **The craft guidance** in these documents.
5. **Reviewer and reader opinion,** including simulated reader panels. Opinion diagnoses; it does not authorise.

Report every conflict you resolve this way rather than resolving it silently.

To start a new book, use `Cozy Mystery/Prompt Template.md` in the `genre-fiction` repo.

## What each series must supply

- **Series style guide.** Spelling convention (US or UK, set by the series' location and author), jurisdiction and police procedure, permitted profanity if any, and the recurring cast's voices.

## What each book must supply before drafting

These documents assume the book has its own short foundation. Keep it lean and get the author's approval before drafting.

- **North star.** Premise, the reader promise for this book, the controlling idea, and the global external and internal values.
- **Lead card.** Who the sleuth is, written as behaviour rather than adjectives. If the author names a reference character (for example "modelled on Richard Castle"), the card states what that means concretely: how the lead enters, talks, wins, loses and enjoys themself, with sample lines. **That reference section is copied word for word into every drafting and revision brief**, so the specification is never lost in translation.
- **Cast cards** for the principals, each with an independent positive want.
- **Mystery spine** (planner-only). The causal truth, the four clocks, the clue ledger and the latest point by which the reader needs each fact.
- **Outline,** with a **gate table** showing the chapter where each hard gate is met.
- **Comp benchmark.** A short table built from two or more published comparison titles: chapter of the first death, when the sleuth and the official foil meet, typical chapter length, and how the lead is introduced (`cozy-mystery.md`, "Benchmark against published comps").

Do not import another book's or another series' foundation documents as working material. They are decisions for a different book, written as rules, and they leak.
