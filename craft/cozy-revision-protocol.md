# Revision Protocol — the pipeline, then the questions

## The pipeline (revision priorities, not permission to stop reading)

- **A. Continuity and canon check** — against the bible, cards, place cards, day-clock, and must-not-leak lists. Flag true contradictions early; distinguish them from changes to optional staging. Other readers may diagnose the draft independently, but settle material causal conflicts before implementing dependent revisions.
- **B. The twelve questions** (below) — the critic's structural pass, answered in writing.
- **C. The asymmetry questions** (below) — the ensemble/energy pass, plus the qualitative standards in the book's `format.md` (encounter space, readable exposition, consequences played).
- **D. Blind panel** *(optional; budget-gated)* — also ask readers to describe what they pictured at selected arrivals, where they imagined entrances/people/light, and which details gave that picture; do not give them a floor plan or prompt the intended answer. Read panels for their **reasons, never their rank** (`series-learnings.md` #24–25). Give readers the prose, not the intended solution. Ask what they enjoyed, wanted next, believed and suspected, with the passages that caused those impressions. Ask what they hoped would happen near the beginning, how that changed and where they lost that feeling. Do not supply the intended chapter question or name a suspected fragmentation problem; an unprompted answer is the test. Check proposed fixes against causal truth, but do not dismiss a reading failure because the outline says the scene works. When the chapter follows accepted prose, give readers the preceding accepted chapter first, then the candidates, without stating the intended effect; ask what they wanted next and where they became impatient. Choosing the best of several near-identical versions does not show that any of them is interesting enough.
- **E. The author's read** — outranks every instrument above (#21). One human cold reader outranks every panel.
- **F. Line edit, last** — `line-edit-protocol.md`. Do not disguise structural changes as sentence repairs; if one is needed, return to the relevant stage and recheck affected passages.

**Prose passes run as three touches** *(recalibrated 2026-09-09)*: (1) a report-only clarity + character critic (the `clarity` packet stage); (2) ONE revision at the scale the findings require — rebuild a genuinely broken paragraph or scene from its factual content with the old wording set aside, but preserve working passages and make small changes when those resolve the problem; (3) a blind comprehension read asking where the reader reread or had to infer a basic physical fact. Judge the revision by the named defect and the reading experience, not percentage changed. A 3–5% change can succeed; a wholesale rewrite can fail. Do not rebuild merely to demonstrate effort.

Cutting rule at every stage: **consider removing a dispensable beat before thinning every good passage** (#28). Preserve or relocate a necessary story function and record that move in `chapter-briefs.md`; do not re-home business the book no longer needs. Several functions do not automatically justify a long scene: a technical repair plus a joke plus a competence demonstration may still repeat what the chapter has already established.

**Character check before revising an encounter:** use the actual cards, not a remembered adjective or an unlabelled voice-bank peak. Quote any action, work habit, permission request, concession or change of intimacy that contradicts the character's default in this situation. State what event, if any, earns the exception. Read the entire local negotiation: a shortened reply must not acquiesce to a demand the character would first try to evade. The critic must not repair structure by replacing the people. This is part of the existing passes, not a new stage.

**Murder-timing check (every outline review and every structural pass):** state the chapter in which the death is on the page. If it is later than Chapter 2 without the author's written approval (`cozy-mystery.md`, non-negotiable 2), report it at the top of the review, ahead of every other finding, as a plan defect, and propose a restructure: open on the day of the death, move the scheduled event forward, or fold the setup into the investigation. Do not offset it with strong complications, charming setup, reader scores or an added "jolt", and do not accept a market-percentage benchmark in place of the chapter rule. A reviewer who finds this defect accepted by an earlier stage reports it again rather than deferring.

**Constraint check before revision:** distinguish invariant canon, causal evidence and knowledge limits from dramatic functions and optional execution (`cozy-mystery.md`, "Commissioning a book"). Do not preserve repeated clue business while trimming the event's human interest because the former is easier to tick off. Keep the necessary fact; change how it enters when the scene improves. Reconcile consequential changes with the brief and downstream chapters. Voice samples demonstrate a range, not required exchanges, and examples in a brief are not hidden locks.

**Pleasure-preservation check before compression:** name the existing exchanges that make the company enjoyable, alongside the defects to repair. An encounter must matter; every reply need not produce evidence, conflict or a lesson. Cut repeated explanation or dispensable business before reducing a distinctive exchange to generic information. After revision, compare the whole exchange, not just individual lines: what pleasure, relationship texture or voice was lost while clarity and causality improved? Report a concrete before/after regression and repair it. This belongs within the existing structural/voice passes, not a new scored gate or a demand to retain every joke.

**Calendar and placement changes have costs.** Before moving or merging encounters, name any loss of duration, anticipation, recurring amusement, attachment or breathing space. A later payoff is not automatically improved by moving it next to its setup. Keep the evidence and permission chronology intact, and distinguish a candidate restructuring from an accepted change to canon.

**Reader-tested pleasure is evidence too.** Before cutting a named cameo, exchange or ordinary detail as “nonessential,” ask what it gives the reader. A name on a scoreboard can make a game personal without adding a cast member the reader must track. A human reader's attachment to a small joke is a reason to reconsider the cut, not a mandate to restore every old passage. Keep or tighten the part that carries the pleasure, and cut actual repetition elsewhere. A protect list preserves what a passage does, not its wording, position or survival; a protected passage that fails the reused-events test is compressed like any other.

## Stage B — the twelve questions

Run on a chapter, an act, or the whole draft. Each question names a failure mode; some answers will be "no, fine" — writing that down is the point.

1. **Did the chapter develop, rather than keep starting over?** Apply `storygrid-commandments.md`, “Chapter cohesion — what carries forward.” Report (a) the opening expectation, (b) which substantial encounters change it, (c) where the prose resets and (d) what the ending answers, interrupts or newly makes important. **Judge the principal encounter apart from its opening hook and exit consequence:** name the protagonist's first approach, the response that changes it and her next action. If the middle is mainly a list of subjects covered, a good last-page reversal does not repair it — recommend reconstructing the encounter, not tighter wording. Apply "Reused events must change something now" (`storygrid-commandments.md`) with the preceding accepted chapter in view; if that prose was not supplied, report the check NOT VERIFIED in the summary rather than passing it. Quote causal or experiential carry-over; the brief's question and a retrospective five-commandment map do not prove the reader felt it. Check repeated obstacles and whether a substantial event fulfils, redirects or defeats a live expectation. Count genuine scenes, not rooms or asterisks; note word count against the house default or a recorded exception. Propose selection, relocation, combination or an earned boundary before thinning good prose. **Then run the variation check:** state the place, cast, scale and register of this chapter and of the one before it, and of the one after where it exists. At least two of place, cast and scale must differ across each boundary; a third consecutive chapter in one location is a defect to report, not a preference. Report a violation as a plan defect and propose which encounter relocates or re-casts — do not repair it by adding travel or a walk-and-talk. For complete dramatic units, explicitly identify all five commandments and the evidenced value shift. Mark partial units incomplete and verify resolution in the supplied sequence; do not invent a crisis or claim an unseen later chapter resolves it.
2. **Was the protagonist the cause — on both sides of the transaction?** Where does an appetite or gift produce benefit and also cross a boundary? Trace the affected person's response, without requiring punishment for every success. A comic household failure is not payment for relational damage; an apology is not automatic repair.
3. **Is the climax-shape clean?** The case's decisive confrontation is in motion, and the protagonist helps cause its outcome. At smaller scales, distinguish an enacted answer to a crisis from a revelation, discovery or interruption. A death may turn the story without anyone choosing it; do not fabricate an alternative to ordinary rescue. Ordinary gatherings and conversations are not banned by the case-climax rule.
4. **Is the register light-energy?** Does pleasure continue to live beside pressure; do plainness and silence give wit contrast; and does Penn's fascination remain active without making grief into entertainment? Use `light-energy-structure.md` as guidance, not a timing grid.
5. **Is the comedy human?** Every laugh belongs to a character, not the author. A fumble may merely puncture Penn rather than pay in plot currency. Check card rations for repetition, but do not manufacture a transaction to justify a joke.
6. **Does the emotional current run under, not on, the surface?** Wound as subtext; no compulsory diagnosis; counter-evidence can be unregistered. Plain regret is allowed. Do not confuse clear emotion with full self-knowledge or demand a solemn response whenever another person is hurt. Protect genuine grief and earned vulnerability.
7. **Is the mystery fair and proportionate here?** Required visible facts and viewpoint knowledge remain legible. Across neighbouring chapters, list what the reader would now suspect and the passages causing that hypothesis: repeated objects, isolated tastes, handling close-ups, reminders and the last substantial action before an outcome. Elapsed story time does not supply reading-time separation. Preserve true chronology; consolidate redundant presentations or restore the event's own absorbing human action rather than hide a seen fact, suppress a thought or invent spurious evidence. An innocent context or plausible wrong interpretation can carry a clue without a special false explanation for every object.
8. **Are the people pursuing lives, not serving interviews?** Suspects must be plausible as killer and as innocent; no herring without honest evidence. At important encounters, identify the other person's independent want and the action that changes the protagonist's options. Who redirects, refuses, bargains, acts first or enforces an obligation? Allies can interfere without becoming villains; easy cooperation need not be sabotaged. Do not make only the guilty person active. Grief and motive belong to lives, not plot functions.
9. **Is the investigation procedurally honest?** England & Wales; the authorities competent, holding an honest wrong answer rather than being negligent; chain-of-custody and caution rules respected or knowingly, consequentially broken.
10. **Do the relationships have agency without becoming contests?** Where a relationship is at stake, its response should survive the exchange rather than reset at the next joke. Nobody needs to win a prescribed share of exchanges or change a standing relationship in every chapter. Is the company enjoyable in its own right, including familiar play that yields no clue? A conversation can preserve a boundary and still be warm. Where Leo appears, is he nineteen as well as responsible — with his own interests, pride, embarrassment or need for encouragement? Can Penn give him pleasure or confidence rather than only receive his care? Do not answer by adding an eye-roll or a technical repair to every scene.
11. **Can a stranger picture this world?** Read the first encounter with each important place and person without the cards in view. State the image the prose actually supplies: room shape and use, relevant route and light, season where it affects the experience, a person's recognisable physical presence. Flag disconnected close-ups, incompatible implied conditions and vague approving labels. Compare neighbouring portraits for repeated construction and interchangeable detail. Build the missing picture at paragraph scale, not by adding an adjective to each action. Description may be still and need not end in interpretation; not every visible feature has to reveal character or become a clue. Quote the gap and the repair, then check that wit and momentum survived. This belongs to the existing structural/prose review, not a new scored gate.
12. **What carries the reader out?** State what the reader now wants, and identify where the chapter created that appetite. An answered question can create a different one; an interrupted promise can become newly urgent. Do not require a cliffhanger or force every pleasure to generate plot. If proposing a cut or new boundary, state what would be lost in attachment, enjoyment, orientation and momentum as well as causal information. Record trade-offs honestly; the revised prose and an unprimed read must establish whether the result works.

## Stage C — character, delight and asymmetry questions

Qualitative, not quotas. Run on every chapter:

13. **Does the character behave like herself as well as sound like herself?** Check work habits, who asks permission, who sets the agenda, how each resists and what intimacy is earned. A guarded celebrity made chummy or an interviewer made a co-writer is character drift even if every sentence is recognisable. Quote the card's default and the passage; name the event justifying any exception. Then ask whether several lines could be reassigned to other mouths without anyone noticing.
14. **Does everyone answer immediately and incisively, and get a polished final word?** Exchanges may also end in silence, confusion, an unanswered joke, a literal response, somebody continuing their task, annoyance, a delayed reply, or a mundane human answer.
15. **Is competence human and local?** Notice misunderstanding, hesitation, failure or need for help where the situation produces it. Across a run of scenes, flag an ensemble that is uniformly accomplished and incisive. Do not add a compulsory mistake to every chapter; a real limit or incompatible want is more revealing than a decorative fumble (see the competence/shadow lines on the cards).
16. **Is Penn recognisably alive in the encounter, or one wit among eight?** What does she want besides information, and what does that appetite make her do or say? Does she enjoy this person, not merely the answer or audience? Has stronger physical activity displaced conversational charm? Would we want to hear a suitable pair continue after the necessary information, and does their pleasure differ from hers with other people? Looking magnificent, enjoying work, competing or joining somebody else's occasion can count. Another major character may command the room without being flattened to preserve her brightness. Do money, connections and enthusiasm sometimes let Penn give people a genuinely wonderful time? Her own effort and generosity should be visible. Where a solution is unwelcome, preserve the other person's choice; do not manufacture a refusal, privilege lesson or hidden bill for every gift.
17. **Can the reader hear and feel Penn's delight and forward motion?** Does ordinary interest lead to asking, trying, joining or recruiting? Where she stops to weigh an available action, quote what stopped her and decide whether the pause is earned. If not, suggest the direct question or action instead. Enjoyable work, quiet absorption and attentive listening count; preserve clear narration and genuine grief, danger or restraint. Where her delight is shared, can another person join, resist or misread it? Do not satisfy this with a decorative smile. The before/after calibration in Penn's voice bank illustrates the scale of a successful change, not three compulsory beats.
18. **Has the writing made Penn prematurely serious or reformed?** Separate (a) what happened to the other person, (b) what Penn thinks happened, and (c) anything she actually changes. Quote where the chapter collapses these into instant guilt, a psychological verdict or a complete lesson. Recovered cheerfulness is not a failure if a refusal or loss of trust still matters. Conversely, another joke must not magically make the injured party forgive her. Recommend sustained chastening only when the events and relationship earn it.
19. **Did the cast receive intentional, unequal weight?** Full-presence characters need sustained wants, a recognisable voice and agency in the encounter; relationship anchors need enough of the relationship to be felt; cameos need only enough identification for retrieval. These are levels of attention, not mandatory gestures or a separate five-commandment arc for every person. Flag both flat principals and over-rendered cameos. A character can obey every verbal restriction and still have no felt personality: look for a distinctive positive response to the changing situation, not just withheld jokes, silence, short sentences or repeated gestures. A card that prescribes monotony is itself a finding — report it against the card, not only the draft.
20. **Did the narrator gild anyone or supply unearned insight?** Not every exit gets an epigram, and not every awkward exchange gets an explanation of Penn's flaw. Supporting characters may object for their own reasons without naming her whole mechanism. Quote the unnecessary capper or diagnosis; do not strip useful orientation or ordinary feeling.

**Rule for using these lists:** they are diagnostics, not prescriptions — they find the broken thing; the fix must come from the story, not from the checklist.
