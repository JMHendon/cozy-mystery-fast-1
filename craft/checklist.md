# Cozy Mystery Checklist

**Order of authority:** author's brief → hard gates → the lead card → craft guidance → reviewer and reader opinion. Only the author can waive a gate, in writing. Report any failed gate first.

## Hard gates (pass/fail)

**Opening and structure**
1. Someone is dead on the page by the end of **Chapter 2**. The crime opens the book; nothing comes before it as a run-up.
2. The lead appears within the **first three pages**, recognisably themself. Opening on the body is fine.
3. The lead **commits to investigating by Chapter 3**, for a reason rooted in character.
4. The **official foil** is on the page no later than the chapter after the lead commits. A recurring partner never disappears for more than three chapters.
5. **Every suspect, including the killer,** is on the page by about 25%. No new suspect after the midpoint.
6. **Five commandments** in every chapter (inciting incident, turning point, crisis, climax, resolution) with a real value shift; every act and the global story mapped.

**Chapter mechanics**
7. Each chapter is **one scene of 1,800–2,200 words**. Exceptions need the author's approval.
8. Each chapter changes **at least two of place, cast and scale** from the last. Never three chapters running in one location.
9. Book length within the brief's target (default 65,000–85,000 words).

**The mystery**
10. **Fair play:** every clue is on the page before the reveal; the lead solves it by deduction, not confession.
11. The sleuth is an **amateur** with a legitimate reason to be there.
12. The **killer is human** and the motive is felt.
13. The **climax is in motion**, the lead is briefly at risk, and the lead causes the outcome.

**The cozy contract**
14. No gore, no sex on the page, no profanity beyond the mildest, no harm to children or animals, no sexual violence. **Light-energy, never thriller-dark.**
15. Closed circle, home base, recurring ensemble, procedural foil.

**The ending**
16. The case resolves completely, justice is done, the community resets with one visible cost, and **a personal or ensemble thread pulls toward the next book**.

## The lead

- Written from **behaviour**, not adjectives. A named reference character (for example Castle) is a specification: copy the lead card's reference section into every brief and test against it ("Would they say this out loud?").
- **The lead owns the room:** the most lines, the best lines, the quotable lines. Wit is **spoken to people**, not parked in the narration.
- Wit restraints (cappers, epigrams, manner clauses, ambient wit) apply to the **supporting cast and narrator, never the lead**.
- **Introduce at strength.** Show why the lead is magnetic before showing them humbled.
- The lead **wins as well as loses**. They grow; they never shrink.

## Before planning

- Benchmark **two or more published comps**: chapter of the first death, when the sleuth meets the foil, chapter length, how the lead is introduced.
- Build the **gate table** before the chapter briefs. If it can't be filled, change the plan.
- Design **encounters, not interviews**: every principal has their own positive want and acts on it.

## Reviewing

- Run the **gate check first**, every time.
- **AI reader panels are not validation.** Ask: Where did you want to stop? When did you expect something that hadn't happened? "Which character would you most like to spend a day with?" and "Which character would you most like to be like?"
- **Blocking:** impatience before the crime, or readers consistently naming someone other than the lead in answer to *both* of those last two questions.
- **Lead-presence check** before and after every pass. A pass that makes the prose cleaner and the lead smaller has failed.
- One human cold reader outranks every panel.
- **Sentences:** one main action or idea per sentence; name people instead of stacking same-gender pronouns (`line-edit-protocol.md`, rules 3 and 11).
